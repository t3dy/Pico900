#### 1. Brian P. Copenhaver ✓ DRAFT

> **Brian P. Copenhaver** (b. 1940)
>
> Harvard's leading historian of Renaissance magic and philosophy. Over four decades, Copenhaver has systematically dismantled the 19th-century scholarly tradition (Burckhardt, Garin) that read Pico as a herald of Renaissance humanism and individual dignity. Instead, his meticulous historical reading demonstrates that Pico's *Oration* defends Kabbalistic mystical ascent and magical practice—a reframing that fundamentally reorients the entire 900 Conclusions away from humanism toward syncretistic theurgy.
>
> **Scholarly Position**: Copenhaver is the canon-maker of Pico studies in the 21st century. His historiographical revolution is to insist on taking Pico's own words seriously: the Oration repeatedly invokes Kabbalah, magic, and angels as the means of spiritual ascent. By closely reading Pico against contemporary Kabbalistic and magical texts (Abulafia, Zohar, Ficino), Copenhaver shows that the entire humanist reading of Pico is based on selective quotation and anachronistic projection of later values. This is not a matter of interpretation but of evidence. His work demolished what had been consensus since Burckhardt: that the Oration is a Renaissance celebration of human dignity and autonomy. It is not. It is a defense of magical ascent and Kabbalistic mysticism as the pathway to union with God.
>
> **Major Works on Pico**:
> - *Magic and the Dignity of Man: Pico della Mirandola and His Oration in Modern Memory* (2019) — The definitive historiographical intervention. Traces how the humanist reading of the Oration was constructed through selective quotation from Burckhardt onward. Canonical.
> - *Pico della Mirandola on Trial: Heresy, Freedom, and Philosophy* (forthcoming; 2022) — Comprehensive analysis of the Apology (1487) as Pico's defense of his 900 Conclusions against papal heresy indictment. Shows how Pico uses scholastic method to defend syncretism and magic.
> - *The Secret of Pico's Oration: Cabala and Renaissance Philosophy* (2002) — Argues that the Oration is a Kabbalistic program for mystical ascent and union with God (mors osculi). Shows how Abulafia's letter-combination technique (tzeruf) grounds Pico's mystical curriculum.
> - *Ten Arguments in Search of a Philosopher: Averroes and Aquinas in Ficino's Platonic Theology* (2009) — Demonstrates the mediation of Aquinas in Renaissance readings of Averroes; relevant for understanding how Pico synthesizes scholastic and Islamic sources.
>
> **Key Arguments**:
> - The Oration is not a Renaissance humanism text but a defense of Kabbalistic mysticism and magical ascent.
> - The historiographical tradition reading Pico as a humanist began with Burckhardt and was perpetuated through selective quotation; Copenhaver demolishes it by close textual analysis.
> - Pico's *Conclusiones* are not a Renaissance philosophical program but a syncretistic compilation designed to defend Kabbalah and magic against ecclesiastical condemnation.
> - Magic in Pico is not "superstition" but a licit philosophical practice of aligning human will with divine emanation through ritual and invocation.
> - The Apology (1487) is Pico's most mature and rigorous work, defending syncretism through scholastic theological method.
>
> **Historiographical Role**: Copenhaver is to late 20th/early 21st-century Pico studies what Kristeller was to 20th-century Renaissance philosophy: the figure who resets the field. He demolished consensus. His work is now the baseline against which all other Pico scholarship must argue. Every subsequent scholar cites Copenhaver, either to build on his thesis or to contest it. There is no major Pico work after 2000 that does not engage Copenhaver's historiographical reframing.
>
> **Ongoing Debates**: Copenhaver argues against the entire humanist tradition (Burckhardt, Garin, Cassirer, Gilmore). He contests Kristeller's more moderate reading of the Oration as compatible with humanism. More recently, he engages with Howlett, who argues for a more nuanced Aristotelian synthesis in Pico; Copenhaver grants Pico's Aristotelianism but insists it is always subordinate to Kabbalistic goals.
>
> **Related Scholars**: Wirszubski (Kabbalah), Allen (Platonism), Edelheit (Scholasticism), Howlett (Aristotelianism), Busi (Kabbalah + textual criticism), Akopyan (Astrology)

#### 2. Chaim Wirszubski ✓ DRAFT

> **Chaim Wirszubski** (1913–1990)
>
> Hebrew University scholar and the foundational authority on Pico and Jewish mysticism. Wirszubski's 1989 monograph *Pico della Mirandola's Encounter with Jewish Mysticism*, co-written with Paul Oskar Kristeller, remains the definitive study of how Pico engaged the Kabbalah and why his engagement was so profound. Wirszubski demonstrated that Pico's interest in Kabbalah was not superficial erudition but central to his entire philosophical project.
>
> **Scholarly Position**: Wirszubski's innovation was to take Pico's Kabbalistic references seriously as authentic engagement with Jewish mystical sources, not as orientalist fantasy. He showed that Pico studied Hebrew directly, consulted Kabbalistic texts (Zohar, Sefer Yetzirah) with the aid of Jewish scholars (especially Elia del Medigo), and integrated Kabbalistic doctrines into his philosophical system. Wirszubski's work establishes that Kabbalah is not ornamental in Pico but essential. This paved the way for Copenhaver's later argument that the entire Oration is Kabbalistic in structure and aim.
>
> **Major Works on Pico**:
> - *Pico della Mirandola's Encounter with Jewish Mysticism* (1989, with P.O. Kristeller) — The canonical study of Pico's Kabbalistic sources and his direct engagement with Hebrew texts. Establishes Pico as a serious student of Kabbalah, not a dilettante.
> - Essays in *Harvard Theological Review* and *Jewish Quarterly Review* on specific Kabbalistic doctrines (Sefiroth, divine names, mystical union) as they appear in Pico's Oration and Conclusiones.
>
> **Key Arguments**:
> - Pico engaged the Kabbalah directly through Hebrew study and consultation with Jewish scholars; his interest was not superficial.
> - Kabbalistic doctrines (especially the Sefiroth and divine names) are integrated into Pico's metaphysics and angelology.
> - Pico's syncretism aims to show that Kabbalah and Christian theology are compatible; this is not a polemical gesture but a genuine philosophical claim.
> - The connection between Pico and Jewish scholars (del Medigo, Allemanno) was substantial and mutual; Jewish figures took Pico seriously.
>
> **Historiographical Role**: Wirszubski legitimized Kabbalah as a serious philosophical tradition worthy of Pico's engagement. Before Wirszubski, Pico's interest in Kabbalah was often dismissed as eccentricity or orientalism. Wirszubski showed it was central. He opened the door for Copenhaver's later argument that Kabbalah is not supplementary but foundational to the entire Oration.
>
> **Ongoing Debates**: Wirszubski engages with Kristeller on the scope of Pico's Kabbalistic knowledge. He debates scholars like Garin who saw humanism as essentially secular; Wirszubski shows that for Pico, humanism and mysticism are intertwined. His work anticipates later debates about Pico's "syncretism": is it synthesis (harmony among traditions) or juxtaposition (placing traditions side by side)? Wirszubski argues for genuine synthesis, grounded in Kabbalistic logic.
>
> **Related Scholars**: Kristeller (edition history, Ficino), Copenhaver (later Kabbalistic reading), Busi (Kabbalah), Allen (Platonism), Edelheit (Scholasticism)

#### 3. Sophia Howlett ✓ DRAFT

> **Sophia Howlett** (b. 1970s)
>
> Trinity College Dublin. In recent work (2010s–2020s), Howlett has reframed Pico's Aristotelianism as a crucial but overlooked dimension of his synthesis. Against the dominant Neoplatonic reading (Copenhaver, Allen), Howlett argues that Pico's engagement with Aristotle and medieval Aristotelianism (Aquinas, Albertus Magnus, Averroes) is not a concession or secondary layer but a genuine and sophisticated philosophical achievement. Her work restores Aristotle to the center of Pico studies.
>
> **Scholarly Position**: Howlett's contribution is to complicate the Neoplatonic consensus established by Copenhaver and Allen. She shows that Pico synthesizes Aristotelian and Neoplatonic traditions through scholastic mediation (Aquinas), creating a genuinely novel metaphysics. Howlett argues that this synthesis is not ad-hoc but logically rigorous: Pico uses Aquinas's reconciliation of Aristotle and Augustine to argue that Aristotelian intellectual substance (separate intellects, pure forms) and Neoplatonic emanation (procession from the One) describe the same reality. Howlett's Pico is more philosophically ambitious than either the humanist or the pure-Kabbalist readings allow: he is attempting to show that all the major philosophical traditions (Aristotle, Plato, Aquinas, Kabbalah) are fundamentally saying the same thing about the structure of reality.
>
> **Major Works on Pico**:
> - *Life and Works of Giovanni Pico della Mirandola* (2019) — Comprehensive biography and philosophical study. The definitive modern life of Pico; integrates biography with philosophy.
> - *Re-evaluating Pico: Aristotelianism, Kabbalism, and Platonism in the Philosophy of Giovanni Pico della Mirandola* (2021) — Restores Aristotelian synthesis as central to Pico's project.
> - Essays in *Renaissance Quarterly* and other venues on Pico's engagement with Aquinas, Averroes, and scholastic logic.
>
> **Key Arguments**:
> - Pico's Aristotelianism is not a departure from his Platonism but a deliberate synthesis through scholastic mediation (Aquinas).
> - The 900 Conclusions are not arranged haphazardly but follow a logical structure derived from scholastic metaphysics (Aquinas, Albert the Great).
> - Pico's "concord" between traditions is not merely poetic but philosophically rigorous: he argues that Aristotle, Plato, and Aquinas are describing the same metaphysical structure.
> - The Oration's ascent (beauty → intellect → union) follows both Plotinian emanation AND Aristotelian intellect theory; these are not competing but complementary.
>
> **Historiographical Role**: Howlett is the first major scholar to systematically restore Aristotelianism and scholasticism to the center of Pico studies. She contests the Neoplatonic hegemony (Copenhaver, Allen) without denying their findings. Her work synthesizes Copenhaver's Kabbalistic reading with Allen's Platonism while adding a rigorous Aristotelian layer. She is the closest Pico scholars get to a grand synthesis of the field's competing positions.
>
> **Ongoing Debates**: Howlett engages with Copenhaver on the primacy of Kabbalah; she argues that Kabbalah, Aristotelianism, and Neoplatonism are genuinely integrated, not hierarchical. She challenges Cassirer and the humanist tradition by showing that Pico's synthesis is metaphysically rigorous, not merely rhetorical. She engages with Edelheit on the Scholastic sources and their integration into Pico's system.
>
> **Related Scholars**: Copenhaver (Kabbalah), Allen (Platonism), Edelheit (Scholasticism), Wirszubski (Kabbalah), Farmer (Debate structure), Black (Hermeneutics)

#### 4. Amos Edelheit ✓ DRAFT

> **Amos Edelheit** (b. 1970s)
>
> Maynooth University. In a series of monographs and articles (2008–2022), Edelheit has established himself as the leading authority on Pico's Scholastic sources and his transformation of medieval theology through humanist method. His most recent work, *A Philosopher at the Crossroads: Giovanni Pico Della Mirandola's Encounter with Scholastic Philosophy* (2022), is the most comprehensive study of how Pico read Aquinas, Scotus, Bonaventure, and Albert the Great, and how he both preserved and transformed scholastic theological method in the service of Renaissance syncretism.
>
> **Scholarly Position**: Edelheit's innovation is to show that Pico is not an enemy of scholasticism (as the humanist tradition claimed) but a sophisticated reader of scholastic metaphysics who transforms it from within. Edelheit argues that Pico's *Apology* (1487) is not a defensive tract but his most rigorous philosophical work, in which he uses scholastic dialectical method to defend Kabbalistic and magical theses against papal inquisitors. The Apology shows that Pico was fluent in scholastic theology and used it as a tool of philosophical innovation. Edelheit's Pico is neither "humanist" nor "mystic" nor "magician" but a sophisticated metaphysician engaging all available philosophical traditions with rigor.
>
> **Major Works on Pico**:
> - *A Philosopher at the Crossroads: Giovanni Pico Della Mirandola's Encounter with Scholastic Philosophy* (2022) — The definitive study of Pico's Scholastic sources (Aquinas, Scotus, Bonaventure, Albert). Canonical for understanding Pico's metaphysics.
> - *Ficino, Pico and Savonarola: The Renaissance of Christian Neoplatonism* (2008) — Shows how Ficino, Pico, and Savonarola transform medieval theology through Neoplatonic and humanist innovation.
> - *Scholastic Florence: Moral Psychology in the Quattrocento* (2016) — Contextualizes Pico within Florentine intellectual culture; shows continuity between medieval scholasticism and Renaissance philosophy.
>
> **Key Arguments**:
> - Pico is deeply indebted to medieval scholastic metaphysics, not opposed to it; he reads Aquinas, Scotus, and Bonaventure with sophistication.
> - The *Apology* is Pico's most rigorous work, using scholastic theological method to defend syncretism and Kabbalah.
> - The essence-existence distinction (from Avicenna via Aquinas) is the hinge of Pico's entire metaphysical system; it is neither humanist nor esoteric but scholastic in foundation.
> - Pico's relationship to Savonarola is not incidental but deep: both are transforming medieval theology through Renaissance methods.
>
> **Historiographical Role**: Edelheit has restored Scholasticism to the center of Pico studies after decades of humanist and Platonist hegemony. His work shows that the dichotomy "humanist vs. scholastic" is false when applied to Pico; Pico synthesizes both. He is the best historian of Pico's philosophical method and his sources.
>
> **Ongoing Debates**: Edelheit engages with Copenhaver on the relationship between Kabbalah and Scholasticism; he argues they are genuinely integrated, not competing. He contests Cassirer and the pure humanist reading by showing Pico's deep engagement with scholastic metaphysics. He debatesHowlett on the degree to which Aristotelianism is a distinct layer or fully integrated.
>
> **Related Scholars**: Copenhaver (Kabbalah), Howlett (Aristotelianism), Wirszubski (Kabbalah), Allen (Platonism), Kristeller (Ficino)
