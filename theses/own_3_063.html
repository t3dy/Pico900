Latin

Quamuis in anima ita sit actu natura intellectualis per quam cum angelo conuenit, sicut est natura rationalis per quam ab eo distinguitur, nihil tamen intrinsecum est in ea, per quod possit sine propria specie aliquid a se distinctum intelligere.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 21264

Apparatus (Farmer): 1486 distinguitur. Nihil

The 1486 print divides the sentence differently, starting a new sentence at "Nihil" (farmer1998:21282).

English

Although the intellectual nature by which the soul agrees with the angel is as fully actual in it as the rational nature by which it is distinguished from the angel, nothing intrinsic to the soul enables it, without a species of its own, to understand anything distinct from itself. unverified draft

"species" is kept as the scholastic technical term rather than translated as "form" or "image" (farmer1998:21264).

Attribution: Pico's own opinion · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

The thesis holds that although the soul possesses, as fully in act, the intellectual nature by which it agrees with the angel as it possesses the rational nature by which it is distinguished from the angel, nothing intrinsic to the soul enables it to understand anything distinct from itself without a species of its own (farmer1998:21264).

unverified draft

Context

Dougherty's edited volume quotes the thesis's closing clause within a discussion of Pico's account of the soul's need for a species of its own to know what is other than itself, and cross-references it to 2>31 and to 2>77, where Pico rejects abstraction as the intellect's "first operation" (dougherty2008:9517).

unverified draft

Historiography

Dougherty's volume is the only scholarly discussion of the thesis located in the sources consulted, and situates it within a progression it traces across Pico's conclusions on knowledge, beginning with his citation of Albert the Great in the first of the historical conclusions (dougherty2008:9517).
