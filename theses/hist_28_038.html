Doctrine

Farmer's note reads the thesis, paired with 28.39, as resolving a commentarial debate over the relative superiority of fear and love of God in medieval Kabbalism, citing Scholem (1974: 175) for the debate (paraphrase, farmer1998:18317). He states that Pico resolves the problem 'in typically syncretic fashion by distinguishing hierarchical "modes" of fear', associating these modes with sefirot above and below the fourth, Hesed, which Pico calls 'love' or 'piety' and symbolizes by Abraham (paraphrase, farmer1998:18317). Farmer connects the lower mode of fear to 'Isaac' and the fifth sefirah, cross-referring thesis 10>10, and states that Pico's apparent source is Recanati, citing Wirszubski (1989: 47-48) (paraphrase, farmer1998:18317).
