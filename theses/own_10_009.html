Latin

Idem sunt curetes apud Orpheum, et potestates apud Dionysium.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 25779

English

The same beings are the Curetes in Orpheus and the Powers in Dionysius. unverified draft

'potestates' is kept as 'Powers', the standard name for the sixth of the nine angelic orders in Pseudo-Dionysius's Celestial Hierarchies, per Farmer's note (farmer1998:25813).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch17, from Latin at farmer1998:25779

Attribution: Pico's own opinion, section 10> (Orphic Hymns) (farmer1998:25779). · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

Farmer identifies the 'curetes' as an order of gods in the Orphic Hymns and the 'potestates' as the sixth of the nine angelic orders in Pseudo-Dionysius's Celestial Hierarchies, and suggests, as his own conjecture, that Pico would presumably have further correlated these with Proclus's 'trinity of guardians' at 24.13-14 (farmer1998:25813).
