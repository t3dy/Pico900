Albert the Great · thesis 1.6

Thesis 1.6

Work in progress. This entry is a draft awaiting verification: every claim carries a locator, but no second reader has yet re-found each one.

Latin

Anima separata intelligit per species sıbi a principio sui esse concreatas, quibus dum est in corpore aut nunquam aut raro utitur.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 11040

Farmer's text prints 'sıbi' with a dotless i for 'sibi' (farmer1998:11040). Dougherty's and Edelheit's quotations both give the standard spelling 'sibi' (dougherty2008:9526; edelheit2022:9376).

English

The separated soul understands through species created together with it from the beginning of its existence, which, while it is in the body, it either never or rarely uses. unverified draft

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch1, from Latin at farmer1998:11040

Attribution: Reports Albert the Great (farmer1998:11040). · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

Farmer's note ties the thesis to Pico's harmonized Aristotelian/Platonic theory of knowledge, listing related theses spanning both the historical and the 'own opinion' sections (farmer1998:11072).

unverified draft

Historiography

Dougherty's essay footnote quotes the thesis's Latin text as edited by Farmer, in a series of citations to related theses on the possible intellect and the separated soul (dougherty2008:9526). Edelheit's chapter states that thesis 6 concerns the separated soul, declaring that it understands through species created together with it from the beginning of its existence but rarely or never uses them while embodied, and sets this beside Albert's commentary on De anima, Book 3 (edelheit2022:9351).
