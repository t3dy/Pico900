Thesis 10>24

Latin

Non inebriabitur per aliquem Bacchum, qui suae musae prius copulatus non fuerit.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 26018

English

No one will be made drunk by any Bacchus who has not first been joined to his own Muse. unverified draft

'copulatus' is rendered 'joined', keeping the verb's ordinary sense of union rather than a specifically marital one (farmer1998:26018).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch18, from Latin at farmer1998:26018

Attribution: Pico's own opinion. · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

The thesis holds that mystic intoxication by a given 'Bacchus' requires prior union with one's own Muse (farmer1998:26018). Farmer's note states that this thesis opens a series (starting at thesis 2.12) in which the several 'Bacchae' stand for different modes of will (farmer1998:26098).

unverified draft

Context

The thesis belongs to the Orphic Hymns own-opinion series (farmer1998:25560). Farmer cross-references thesis 8>6, among the Zoroaster and Chaldean conclusions, which states that the Chaldean interpreters' sayings on the double intoxication of Bacchus and Silenus are perfectly understood through the Kabbalists' teaching on two wines (farmer1998:24842).

Reception

Busi and Ebgi print the thesis's Latin text verbatim and cross-reference it, in their commentary apparatus, to their entry on the Muses (busi2014:24644; busi2014:17253).
