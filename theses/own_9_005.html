Thesis 9>5

Latin

Nulla est uirtus in caelo_aut in terra seminaliter et separata quam et actuare et unire magus non possit.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 25190

English

There is no power in the sky or on the earth, existing seminally and separately, that the magus cannot both actuate and unite. unverified draft

'caelo' renders the celestial or astronomical realm, not heaven in the religious sense, throughout Pico's magical theses, per Farmer's note (farmer1998:25215); 'seminaliter et separata' are technical terms for powers latent in seed-form and for powers existing as discrete, scattered instances.

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch17, from Latin at farmer1998:25190

Attribution: Pico's own opinion, section 9> (Magic) (farmer1998:25190). · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

The thesis claims the magus's power to actuate and unite extends to every power latent or scattered in heaven and earth (farmer1998:25190). Farmer's note glosses 'caelum' here as the celestial realm rather than the religious heaven, and its text explicitly cross-references thesis 9>11 as making the same claim, using the abbreviated id notation of the underlying OCR (farmer1998:25215).

Historiography

Akopyan (2018) and Akopyan (2021) both quote this thesis's Latin verbatim in a single footnote alongside 9>11's Latin, citing the two together, from consecutive pages of Pico's magic conclusions ('ibid., 5' and 'ibid., 11'), as paired statements of the magus's power over seminal and separated natural virtues (akopyan2018:580; akopyan2021:1338).
