Latin

Cum dicit Abucaten: Omnis anima nobilis tres habet operationes, diuinam, intellectualem, et animalem, ita est intelligendum, quod primam habeat per imaginem proportionalitatis, secundam per formalitatem participationis, tertiam per proprietatem essentialitatis.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 23437

Apparatus (Farmer): 1486 operationes.diuinam:

The 1486 print punctuates the opening differently: 'operationes.diuinam:' (farmer1998:23469).

English

When Abucaten says: 'Every noble soul has three operations, divine, intellectual, and animal [psychic],' it is to be understood in this way: that it has the first through the image of proportionality, the second through the formality of participation, and the third through the property of essentiality. unverified draft

'Abucaten' (elsewhere 'Abucaten Avenan') is Pico's name for the purported author of the Liber de causis, and 'animalem' is glossed '[psychic]' since the Latin here does not mean 'pertaining to animals' but the soul's own operation, as the thesis itself distinguishes it from the divine and intellectual operations (farmer1998:23437); Farmer's note situates the thesis among Pico's attempts to reconcile the Book of Causes with his own views (farmer1998:23475).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch15, from Latin at farmer1998:23437

Attribution: Pico's own opinion, in the doctrine of the Book of Causes (section 6>, farmer1998:23348). · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

The thesis glosses a saying attributed to 'Abucaten,' the purported author of the Book of Causes: every noble soul has three operations, divine, intellectual, and animal (psychic), and it has the first through the image of proportionality, the second through the formality of participation, and the third through the property of essentiality (farmer1998:23437). Farmer's note states that the thesis, like the following 6>3, is meant to reconcile the Book of Causes with Pico's own views, and compares thesis 3>1 (farmer1998:23475).
