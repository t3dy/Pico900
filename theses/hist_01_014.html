Latin

Corpus mobile est subiectum scientiae naturalis.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 11153

English

The movable body is the subject-matter of natural science. unverified draft

'Subiectum': scholastic technical term. 'Scientia naturalis': natural philosophy.

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-swarm-batch5, from Latin at farmer1998:11153

Attribution: Reports Albert the Great (farmer1998:11153). · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

Farmer's note ties the thesis to a scholastic dispute over the proper objects of physics and metaphysics, cross-referencing 2.42, 7.20 and 7.38 (farmer1998:11219; note text cut off after 'Pico's usual hierarchical means of resolving such conflicts is'). Edelheit states that thesis 14 'states that movable body is the subject of natural science' and locates it in Albert's commentary on De anima, Book 1, Tract 1, Chapter 1 (edelheit2022:9928). There Albert argues that although the soul and its operations are not themselves the movable body that is the subject of natural philosophy, the soul is nonetheless an essential principle of such a body, and so belongs to natural science in that respect (edelheit2022:9954).

Historiography

Edelheit treats thesis 14 as one instance in which 'the same basic Aristotelian assumptions and their implications are also reflected in Pico's Theses' as in other commentators he discusses in the same context (edelheit2022:4184). He prints the Latin as it appears in Farmer's edition, page 216 (edelheit2022:4203, 9954).
