Post passionem Christi potuerunt cerimonialia ueteris legis sine peccato obseruari.

English

After the passion of Christ, the ceremonial precepts of the old law could be observed without sin. unverified draft

'Cerimonialia ueteris legis': the ceremonial precepts of the Mosaic law, as distinct from its moral precepts; 'vetus lex' designates the Old Testament dispensation (farmer1998:12262).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch6, from Latin at farmer1998:12262

Attribution: Reports Scotus (farmer1998:12262). · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

The thesis addresses whether the ceremonial precepts of the Old Testament remained licitly observable after Christ's Passion (farmer1998:12262). Farmer states that the thesis stands 'in opposition to Thomas', citing Thomas's Summa theologiae 1a2ae, question 103, article 4, and locates the Scotist discussion at Ordinatio, book 4, distinction 3, question 4 (farmer1998:12345). Edelheit confirms that thesis 14 holds that after Christ's Passion the religious ceremonies of the Old Testament could still be preserved without sin, discussed by Scotus in that same question, where Scotus identifies an intermediate period in which the first Law had not yet been discarded (edelheit2022:14532).

Historiography

Edelheit notes that some aspects of Scotus's account of this issue 'are not reflected in Pico's thesis', though they 'might have played a part in his defending strategy during the public debate which was planned but never took place', suggesting Pico compressed or adapted his source (edelheit2022:14532).
