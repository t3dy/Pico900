Thesis 11>5

Latin

Quilibet hebreus cabalista, secundum principia et dicta scientiae Cabalae, cogitur ineuitabiliter concedere de trinitate_et qualibet persona diuina, patre, filio, et spiritu sancto, illud precise sine additione, diminutione, aut uariatione, quod ponit fides catholica christianorum.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 26476

English

Every Hebrew Cabalist, according to the principles and sayings of the science of the Cabala, is inevitably compelled to concede concerning the Trinity, and concerning each divine Person, Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, precisely, without addition, diminution, or variation, what the Catholic faith of Christians holds. unverified draft

'cogitur ineuitabiliter concedere' ('is inevitably compelled to concede') states the thesis's central and strong claim: not that Kabbalah is compatible with the Trinity, but that its own principles necessitate the Christian doctrine of it (farmer1998:26476).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-swarm-batch6, from Latin at farmer1998:26476

Attribution: Pico's own opinion (farmer1998:26476). · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

The thesis asserts that Kabbalistic principles, taken on their own terms, entail the Christian doctrine of the Trinity without remainder (farmer1998:26476). The immediately following thesis, 11>6, assigns three great quaternary divine names of the secret Kabbalistic doctrine to the three Persons 'by a wonderful appropriation' (paraphrase of Pico's own Latin, farmer1998:26487).

Historiography

Wirszubski names 11>5 'the fifth thesis' of the second Cabalistic set and quotes its Latin verbatim (wirszubski1989:8216). An unsigned German study, *Erkenntnistheorie und christliche Kabbalah: Das Paradigma des Pico della Mirandola* (author not identified in the material opened), groups 11>5 with 10>15, 11>35 and 11>36, citing Greive's *Die christliche Kabbalah* and, 'especially,' Wirszubski's *Encounter* pages 235-238 for parallels from Azriel of Gerona and Moses Nachmanides (paraphrase, kabbalah_paradigma:1504). An unsigned article, *Knots and Spirals I: Pico della Mirandola* (author not identified in the material opened), quotes 11>5 under the heading 'CABALISTIC CONCLUSIONS CONFIRMING THE CHRISTIAN RELIGION', alongside 11>7 on the interpretation of the name of Jesus (paraphrase, knots_spirals:264). Unsigned research notes, *Self-Knowledge, Illumination and Natural: notes on Pico sources* (author not identified in the material opened), cite 11>5 together with 7a>40 (on which account of the Trinity — Arius's, Sabellius's, or the Catholic faith's — is truer) as evidence that Pico sought in the Kabbalistic sources a confirmation of Catholic doctrine (paraphrase, selfknowledge_notes:767). These notes, however, quote for '11>5' a Latin text reading 'Non solum qui negant trinitatem, sed qui aliomodo eam ponunt quam ponat catholica ecclesia, sicut Arriani, Sabelliani, et similes, redargui possunt manifeste si admittantur principia cabalae' (selfknowledge_notes:767), which does not match the Latin given at farmer1998:26476 above; this discrepancy is recorded here rather than resolved, since the sources consulted do not establish which numbering the notes intend.
