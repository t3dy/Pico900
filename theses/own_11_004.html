Thesis 11>4

Latin

Ensoph non est aliis numerationibus connumeranda, quia est illarum

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 26396

Farmer's transcribed line (farmer1998:26396) is truncated mid-sentence; the full text, quoted identically in busi2014:24764 and howlett2021:7597 and matching in substance in wirszubski1989:11832-11833, continues: '...numerationum unitas abstracta et incommunicata, non unitas coordinata' (wirszubski1989 gives the final word as 'coordinate').

English

The Ein Sof is not to be counted along with the other numerations, because it is the abstract and uncommunicated unity of those numerations, not a coordinate unity. unverified draft

'Ensoph' = Ein Sof, God's transcendent nature prior to the sefirot (farmer1998:26462). 'numerationibus'/'numerationum' = the sefirot; Farmer glosses 'Ensoph' as 'God's transcendent nature, the source of his manifested nature in the sefirot', roughly equivalent 'to the abstract essence of God in Latin scholasticism' (paraphrase; farmer1998:26462). 'unitas coordinata' is rendered 'a coordinate unity', i.e. a unity ranked alongside the other numerations, which the Latin at farmer1998:26396 denies of the Ein Sof.

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-swarm-batch3, from Latin at farmer1998:26396 (full sentence per busi2014:24764)

Attribution: Pico's own opinion (farmer1998:26396). · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

Thesis 11>4 holds that the Ein Sof is not to be numbered among the other sefirotic 'numerations' because it constitutes their abstract, uncommunicated unity rather than a unity coordinate with them (farmer1998:26396; full Latin at busi2014:24764). Farmer's note explains 'Ensoph' as God's transcendent nature, the source of his manifest nature in the sefirot, roughly equivalent to the abstract essence of God in Latin scholasticism, and adds that Pico apparently meant to correlate theses like this one with the Latin scholastic theses on essence and relation (paraphrase; farmer1998:26462).

Context

Farmer's cross-reference apparatus ties the thesis to 10>15, on Night in Orpheus and Ensoph in the Cabala (farmer1998:25892); to 11>35 and 11>36, on God considered in himself as infinite and one (farmer1998:27129, 27137); and to the historical theses 3.1-3.2 of Francis of Meyronnes (farmer1998:11974, 11978) and 6.1 of Giles of Rome (farmer1998:12671).

Reception

Busi and Ebgi's edition and Howlett's study both quote the thesis's Latin in full (busi2014:24764; howlett2021:6140, howlett2021:7597). Wirszubski likewise quotes it in full as his 'Conclusio iv' (wirszubski1989:11832-11833).

unverified draft

Historiography

Wirszubski places the thesis immediately after Pico's opening divisions and definitions of the Cabala, remarking that it comes "hard on the heels of Pico's divisions and definitions of Kabbala" (wirszubski1989:11832).
