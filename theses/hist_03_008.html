Latin

Esse non est de quiditate dei, sed dicitur de eo in secundo modo.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 12079

Apparatus (Farmer): 1487 Esse non de est quiditate

Farmer's apparatus records a 1487 variant reading, 'Esse non de est quiditate' (farmer1998:12084); the present entry follows the reading at farmer1998:12079.

English

Existence is not of the quiddity of God, but is said of him in the second mode [of per se predication]. unverified draft

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch5, from Latin at farmer1998:12079

Attribution: Reports Francis of Meyronnes, the last of the eight theses drawn from his commentary (farmer1998:11969, 12079). · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

The thesis holds that God's existence (esse) is not part of his quiddity but is predicated of him 'in the second mode' of per se predication (farmer1998:12079), a mode Farmer's note treats as making existence, idiosyncratically, a 'proper accident' of the divine nature, and cross-references to his notes on 2.35 and 2.24 for the sense of 'second mode' (farmer1998:12121). Edelheit states the same content: 'existence is not about the quiddity of God but rather it is said about God in a second mode' (edelheit2022:13642).

Historiography

Edelheit locates the discussion in Francis of Meyronnes's commentary on Sentences 1, Prologue, question 6, citing Scriptum super Primum Sententiarum, pages a8r-b1r, and notes this identification 'was indicated by Farmer' (edelheit2022:13668). He also records, as an open question of his own, whether an apparent discrepancy elsewhere in Francis's text reflects Pico citing from memory or a textual error (edelheit2022:13642).
