Latin

Ideo Moysis sepulchrum nemo nouit, quia exaltatus est in Iobeleo superiore, et super Iobeleum misit radices suas.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 18310

English

Therefore no one knows the sepulcher of Moses, because he was exalted into the higher jubilee, and above the jubilee he sent forth his roots. unverified draft

'Iobeleo'/'Iobeleum' transliterate the Hebrew yovel ('jubilee'); Farmer glosses 'superior jubilee'/'jubilee' as apparent references to the third sefirah (Binah, 'intelligence') and the sixth (Tiferet, 'beauty', the 'great Adam') (paraphrase, farmer1998:18381).

Doctrine

Farmer glosses 'superior jubilee' and 'jubilee' as apparent references to the third sefirah, Binah ('intelligence'), and the sixth, Tiferet ('beauty', the 'great Adam'), cross-referring thesis 28.13 (paraphrase, farmer1998:18381).

unverified draft

Historiography

Wirszubski quotes the thesis (wirszubski1989:2840) and reports that the Zohar, part I, folio 21v, considers why Moses's sepulcher remained unknown, though the excerpt consulted breaks off before completing the Zohar's own answer. He then argues, from the absence of the 'Superior Tree', 'Superior Earth', and Jeremiah 17:8 from that Zohar passage, that these elements' presence in Pico's theses 'argues conclusively that the direct source of these theses is Recanati, not the Zohar' (wirszubski1989:2880). He further notes that the Latin rendering of Jeremiah 17:8, 'et super Iobeleum misit radices suas', shows what he calls 'the touch of the same expert hand' that translated a related manuscript, the Liber de Radicibus (wirszubski1989:2880).
