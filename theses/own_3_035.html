Latin

Sicut de solo intellectu uere dicitur quod est ignis, et quod est aqua, quod est motus, et quod est status, ita de sola anima uere dicitur quod frigefit, quod calefit, quod stat, quod mouetur.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 20762

English

Just as it is truly said of the intellect alone that it is fire, and that it is water, that it is motion, and that it is rest, so it is truly said of the soul alone that it is cooled, that it is heated, that it stands still, that it is moved. unverified draft

The Latin pairs static predicates for intellect (ignis, aqua, motus, status) with corresponding verbs of undergoing for soul (frigefit, calefit, stat, mouetur); the rendering keeps that pairing (farmer1998:20762).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch13, from Latin at farmer1998:20762

Attribution: Pico's own opinion · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

The thesis draws a parallel between intellect and soul through opposed grammatical modes: the intellect alone is truly said to be fire, water, motion, and rest (predicated as what it is), while the soul alone is truly said to be cooled, heated, to stand still, and to be moved (predicated as what happens to it) (farmer1998:20762).
