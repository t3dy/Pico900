Latin

Actus intelligendi nobiliori modo causatur ab intellectu quam ab obiecto, quodcunque sit obiectum, modo non sit beatificum.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 12375

English

The act of understanding is caused in a nobler way by the intellect than by the object, whatever the object may be, provided it is not the beatific object. unverified draft

'Beatificum': the object beheld in the beatific vision, God as seen by the blessed (farmer1998:12375).

Attribution: Reports Scotus (farmer1998:12375). · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

Continuing the note begun at thesis 19, the thesis asserts that the act of understanding is caused in a nobler way by the intellect than by the object, whatever the object, unless that object is the object of beatific vision (farmer1998:12375). Farmer's note groups theses 19-20 together under a single cross-reference to thesis 2.40, on the source of 'difficulty' in understanding (farmer1998:12459; 2.40, farmer1998:11882). Edelheit confirms that, moving to thesis 20, the act of understanding is caused in a nobler fashion when it results from the intellect than when it results from the object, and connects the claim to Scotus's broader account of intellection, in which the superiority of intellect over object is exemplified by the case of the Father's cognition, though without citing a specific Ordinatio or Lectura location for thesis 20 itself (edelheit2022:15020).
