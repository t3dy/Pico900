English

The Chaldean interpreters' remarks on the eleventh aphorism, concerning a twofold drunkenness, that of Bacchus and that of Silenus, will become fully intelligible through what the Cabalists teach about a twofold wine. unverified draft

'amphorismus' is rendered 'aphorism'; in this section it denotes an oracle, as Corazzol reads it (corazzol2019:429-430). 'perfecte intelligentur' is future: the thesis promises an explanation to be given at the disputation. 'ebriatio' is rendered 'drunkenness' to keep the pairing with 'wine'.

Doctrine

The thesis claims that a Chaldean commentary on an eleventh aphorism, which distinguished two kinds of drunkenness, of Bacchus and of Silenus, is fully explained by the Cabalists' doctrine of two kinds of wine (farmer1998:24842-24844). Farmer reads the two drunkennesses and the two wines as symbols of higher and lower modes of love, will or mystical frenzy, refers the sense to 11>17, and places the thesis in the series on beatitude that begins at 2.12 (farmer1998:24916-24918). Thesis 11>17 says that whoever knows what the purest wine is among the Cabalists will know why David said 'I shall be inebriated with the plenty of your house', what drunkenness the ancient poet Musaeus called happiness, and what the many Bacchuses of Orpheus signify (farmer1998:26715-26718). Wirszubski reports Edgar Wind's reading of the duplex ebriatio as the Neoplatonic distinction between vulgar excess and divine madness (wirszubski1989:9785-9787). For the two wines he points to the Liber de Radicibus, a glossary of Kabbalistic symbols, where 'wine preserved in its grapes' signifies the tenth sefirah and 'wine of libation' signifies the unclean side (wirszubski1989:9788-9797). A sefirah is one of the ten emanated entities through which, in Kabbalah, God created the world (corazzol2019:458-459). Corazzol derives the doctrine from Recanati's commentary on Genesis 9:20-21, where the Shekhinah is the vineyard and yields two wines: the stored wine from the side of Piety, the cup of salvation, and the wine of libation drawn from the side of Impurity (corazzol2019:509-530). In Recanati's reading Noah drank the second wine and so committed a sin like Adam's, an act of idolatry (corazzol2019:530-545, 575-577). Corazzol concludes that for Recanati both perfect union with God and idolatry are figured as inebriation, the two wines flowing from the tenth sefirah according to whether the worshipper keeps or breaks the commandments (corazzol2019:563-568).

Context

The section contains fifteen conclusions on understanding the sayings of Zoroaster and his Chaldean commentators (farmer1998:24715-24718). Corazzol recounts Pico's letter of September 1486 to Ficino announcing Chaldean books: the oracles of the magi Ezra, Zoroaster and Melchiar, an interpretation by the Chaldean wise men, and a booklet on Chaldean theology (corazzol2019:111-120). He argues that the author of these texts was Guglielmo Raimondo Moncada, alias Flavius Mithridates (corazzol2019:199-220). Farmer likewise judges a forgery by Flavius the likeliest explanation (farmer1998:24794-24799). Farmer observes that the section contains material not found in the Greek collections of Psellus or Pletho, and that Pico's numbering of the oracles does not match theirs (farmer1998:24750-24752). Corazzol classes 8>6, 8>7 and 8>8 among the theses that cite aphorisms without naming their author (corazzol2019:429-430). He traces the Kabbalistic notions in 8>5, 8>6, 8>7, 8>9 and 8>11 to Recanati's Commentary on the Torah (corazzol2019:443-451). He sets them against Recanati's account of prayer as theurgy, the purification and liturgy by which the worshipper brings about the unification of the ten sefirot (corazzol2019:457-466).

Reception

Busi and Ebgi cite the thesis as an instance of Pico's collage, in which a Neoplatonic image is set beside a Kabbalistic placitum: to a drunken god are offered double wines (busi2014:20252-20259, 20272). They state the rule behind such pairings: elements that perform analogous functions in different symbolic systems are interchangeable (busi2014:20264-20268). Their anthology gives the thesis in Italian beside 11>17 under the heading of wine (busi2014:23342-23356, 25202-25210).

Historiography

Wind, as Wirszubski reports him, supplies the Platonic side of the pairing (wirszubski1989:9785-9787). Wirszubski supplies a Kabbalistic glossary and marks his identification as unprovable (wirszubski1989:9797-9799). Corazzol rejects Wirszubski's source, which he identifies as Mithridates' translation of Yosef ibn Waqqar's Sefer ha-shorashim, because too much would remain unexplained, and turns to Recanati (corazzol2019:505-511). On his reading the two wines hint at the difference between perfect union with God and idolatry (corazzol2019:602-605). He argues that with 8>6 and 8>7 Pico meant to show that Kabbalistic and Zoroastrian doctrine agreed on the theurgical effects of communion with God as against those of witchcraft (corazzol2019:632-636). Corazzol ties this to the Oration's contrast between magic, the consummation of natural philosophy, and witchcraft, which rests wholly on demons (corazzol2019:606-622). The thesis is a promissory note: the Chaldean text it explains is lost or was forged, and what survives is the Kabbalistic key Pico meant to apply to it (editorial inference from corazzol2019:504-505 and farmer1998:24794-24799).
