Thesis 9>6

Latin

Quodcunque fiat opus mirabile, siue sit magicum, siue cabalisticum, siue cuiuscunque alterius generis, principalissime referendum est in deum gloriosum et benedictum, cuius gratia supercaelestes mirabilium uirtutum aquas super contemplatiuos homines bonae uoluntatis quotidie pluit liberaliter.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 25194; 1486 print folio <28v/29r>

English

Whatever marvelous work is done, whether magical, or cabalistic, or of any other kind, must be referred above all to the glorious and blessed God, by whose grace the supercelestial waters of marvelous powers rain down daily and generously upon contemplative men of good will. unverified draft

'supercaelestes...aquas' evokes the 'waters above the firmament' of Genesis 1; Busi and Ebgi's own footnote on this thesis renders the phrase closely as 'acque sopracelesti di mirabile virtu' and glosses it as waters God 'pours upon the heads of contemplative men' (busi2014:3575-3577).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch17, from Latin at farmer1998:25194

Attribution: Pico's own opinion, section 9> (Magic) (farmer1998:25194). · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

The thesis subordinates every marvelous operation, magical or cabalistic, to the grace of God, imaged as supercelestial waters raining daily on contemplative men of good will (farmer1998:25194). Farmer groups this thesis with 9>12 and 9>20 as together suggesting that Pico's imitative magic did not work mechanically but depended on the mediation of the purified soul (farmer1998:25253), and his cross-reference network ties it to the Christological magic theses 9>7-9>9 and to the reconciling principle of 1>6 (farmer1998:18596; farmer1998:25200; farmer1998:25204; farmer1998:25208).

Historiography

Busi and Ebgi's own footnote on this thesis, attached to their discussion of the Symposium myth of Poros and Penia, compares that myth's 'luminous rain' to the waters of this conclusio: 'Sorta di pioggia luminosa che irriga le terre dell'Intelligenza, simile in tutto a quelle «acque sopracelesti di mirabile virtu» che Dio sparge sul capo degli uomini contemplativi, evocate da Pico in una delle Conclusiones' ('A kind of luminous rain that irrigates the lands of the Intelligence, entirely like those "supercelestial waters of marvelous power" that God pours upon the heads of contemplative men, evoked by Pico in one of the Conclusiones') (busi2014:3575-3577). Their Italian 'acque sopracelesti di mirabile virtu' renders Pico's own 'supercaelestes...mirabilium uirtutum aquas' closely, confirming that this is the thesis they have in mind (busi2014:3576).
