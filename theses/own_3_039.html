Latin

Dictum illud mirabile illius barbari, nympharum et demonum consortis, de .clxxxiiii. mundis in figura triangulari cum tribus unitatibus angularibuset una media constitutis, rectissime intelligetur si ab unitate intelligibili trinitatem intellectualem, animalem, et seminalem, et principalium mundi partium per primum sphericum numerum computationem, intellexerimus.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 20779

English

That marvelous saying of that barbarian, consort of nymphs and daemons, about the 184 worlds constituted in a triangular figure with three unities at the corners and one in the middle, will be most rightly understood if we understand, from the intelligible unity, an intellectual, an animate, and a seminal trinity, and the reckoning of the principal parts of the world by the first spherical number. unverified draft

'illius barbari' ('that barbarian') is not identified by name in the Latin text itself; 'primum sphericum numerum' ('the first spherical number') is left untranslated as a technical term of ancient arithmetic, not resolved here (farmer1998:20779).
