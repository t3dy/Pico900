Thesis 3>18

Latin

Ideo in anima apparet incompossibilitas contradictoriorum, quia est prima quantitas, ponens partem extra partem.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 20462

English

The incompossibility of contradictories therefore appears in the soul, because it is the first quantity, positing part outside part. unverified draft

"ponens partem extra partem" renders the standard definition of quantity as an ordered plurality of parts external to one another; "incompossibilitas" is kept as the technical term for the incapacity of two things to hold together (farmer1998:20462).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch13, from Latin at farmer1998:20462

Attribution: Pico's own opinion · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

The thesis explains why contradictories cannot coincide in the soul as, on the preceding theses of this run, they can in a purely intellectual nature: the soul is itself "the first quantity", positing part outside part, and this quantitative extension produces the incompossibility of contradictories within it (farmer1998:20462). Farmer's note treats 3>13-18 as a single unit and refers the reader to his discussion of this part of Pico's metaphysics for traditional logic (farmer1998:20526).

unverified draft

Context

3>18 closes the run begun at 3>13 on whether contradictories can coincide, following 3>17's claim that in the intellect "this and that" are present without "this" being outside "that" (farmer1998:20459). Farmer connects the run to 2.31, from Thomas Aquinas, on the real distinction of essence and existence in every created thing (farmer1998:11760; farmer1998:20526).
