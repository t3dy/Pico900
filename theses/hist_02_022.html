Latin

Deus per speciem non uidetur, sed ipse per suam essentiam intellectui applicatur ut species intelligibilis.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 11546

Apparatus (Farmer): 1487 Deus in patria per speciem

Farmer's apparatus records a variant reading in the 1487 printing: 'Deus in patria per speciem' (farmer1998:11552); theses.json and this entry follow Farmer's main reading.

English

God is not seen through a species [a sensible likeness], but he himself, through his own essence, is joined to the intellect as an intelligible species. unverified draft

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch3, from Latin at farmer1998:11546

Attribution: Reports Thomas Aquinas (farmer1998:11546). · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

The thesis denies that God is known through a mediating sensible likeness, holding instead that God joins himself to the intellect directly, through his own essence functioning as the intelligible species (edelheit2022:11435). Edelheit locates the question in Thomas's commentary on the Sentences, book 4, distinction 49, question 2, article 1, and connects it to Thomas's account, elsewhere in the same discussion, of a thing whose quiddity is its own existence and which therefore admits no plurification (edelheit2022:11435; edelheit2022:11465).

Context

Farmer's note cross-refers the thesis to Thomas's Sentences commentary, book 4, distinction 49, question 2, article 1, and suggests Pico planned to collate it with other theses distinguishing God's transcendent and revealed natures, without further detail in the excerpt consulted (farmer1998:11634).

unverified draft

Historiography

Edelheit is the only scholar found discussing this thesis in the sources consulted; he presents it as an epistemological thesis on the perception of God, continuous with Thomas's argument for the uniqueness of a being whose essence is its existence (edelheit2022:11435).
