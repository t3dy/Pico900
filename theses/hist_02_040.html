Thesis 2.40

Latin

Difficultas intelligendi et ex parte ipsius intellectus et ex parte ipsius intelligibilis prouenire potest.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 11882

English

The difficulty of understanding can arise both from the side of the intellect itself and from the side of the intelligible object itself. unverified draft

'intelligibilis' means the object.

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-swarm-batch7, from Latin at farmer1998:11882

Attribution: Reports Thomas Aquinas (farmer1998:11882). · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

Farmer's note traces the question to exegesis of Aristotle's Metaphysics 2.1, quoting the common medieval Latin version in his own English rendering, which likens the intellect confronting nature's most evident things to a bat's eyes confronting the sun (farmer1998:11906). Farmer calls this 'the locus classicus for scholastic discussions of the limits of human understanding,' and notes that 'the insignificance of those limits for Pico is illustrated in [3>40], which provides his commentary on thesis 7.25 from Averroes' (farmer1998:11906). Edelheit reports that 'Pico in thesis 40 according to Thomas Aquinas simply presents these two options' — that difficulty in understanding may come from the intellect or from the thing understood — without deciding between them (edelheit2022:4119).

unverified draft

Context

Edelheit contrasts Pico's even-handed formulation with the Parisian commentary tradition on Aristotle's Metaphysics (via Hennon), which 'concludes that it comes from our intellect' rather than leaving the question open (edelheit2022:4119), and cites a related, anonymous 'Conclusio responsalis' recorded by Bakker's edition of the Paris commentaries, on the difficulty of understanding both material and immaterial substances (edelheit2022:4141).

Reception

Edelheit records that 'thesis 40, just like thesis 20, is included among the theses which were criticized by Bernardo Torni', to be discussed 'again and with further detail in Chapter 12' (edelheit2022:12649); the content of Torni's criticism itself is not given in the material consulted.

Historiography

Edelheit (2022) reads thesis 40 as juxtaposing, without adjudicating, the two standard scholastic answers to where cognitive difficulty originates (edelheit2022:4119, 12649), against the background of the Parisian commentary tradition that resolves the question in favor of the intellect (edelheit2022:4119).
