Latin

Difficultas intelligendi et ex parte ipsius intellectus et ex parte ipsius intelligibilis prouenire potest.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 11882

English

The difficulty of understanding can arise both from the side of the intellect itself and from the side of the intelligible object itself. unverified draft

'intelligibilis' means the object.

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-swarm-batch7, from Latin at farmer1998:7970

Attribution: Reports Thomas Aquinas (farmer1998:7970). · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

Edelheit reports that 'Pico in thesis 40 according to Thomas Aquinas simply presents these two options' — that difficulty in understanding may come from the intellect or from the thing understood — without deciding between them, in the course of surveying how Hennon's Paris commentary on Aristotle's De anima and Metaphysics maps onto Pico's various theses (edelheit2022:2987-2988). Discussing the thesis directly, Edelheit cites Aristotle's Metaphysics 993b4-11 as the reference point (edelheit2022:10270) and quotes, from Thomas's Summa contra gentiles 3.45, the Latin of Aristotle's own image for the difficulty: that our intellect stands to the most evident things in nature 'sicut se habet oculus vespertilionis ad lucem solis', as the eye of the bat (vespertilio) stands to the light of the sun (edelheit2022:10276-10278). The same footnote also gives, in the original Greek, Aristotle's own statement of the point at Metaphysics 993b7-9, 'isos de kai tes chalepotetos ouses kata duo tropous, ouk en tois pragmasin all' en hemin to aition autes...' ('perhaps, since the difficulty is of two kinds, its cause is not in the things but in us...', this English rendering being the entry's own, not a quotation from Edelheit or Farmer) (edelheit2022:10311-10312).

Context

Edelheit contrasts Pico's even-handed formulation with the Parisian commentary tradition on Aristotle's Metaphysics (via Hennon), which 'concludes that it comes from our intellect' rather than leaving the question open (edelheit2022:2987-2988), and quotes the Latin of a related, anonymous 'Conclusio responsalis' recorded in Bakker's edition of the Paris commentaries: 'difficultas cognoscendi tam substancias materiales quam inmateriales est ex parte intellectus nostri' ('the difficulty of knowing both material and immaterial substances is on the side of our intellect') (edelheit2022:3006-3008).

Reception

Edelheit records that 'thesis 40, just like thesis 20, is included among the theses which were criticized by Bernardo Torni', to be discussed 'again and with further detail in Chapter 12' (edelheit2022:10251); the content of Torni's criticism itself is not given in the material consulted.

Historiography

Edelheit (2022) reads thesis 40 as juxtaposing, without adjudicating, the two standard scholastic answers to where cognitive difficulty originates (edelheit2022:2987-2988, 10251), against the background of the Parisian commentary tradition that resolves the question in favor of the intellect (edelheit2022:2987-2988).
