Thesis 28.6

Latin

Magnus aquilo fons est animarum omnium simpliciter, sicut alu dies quarundam et non omnium.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 17637

Farmer's transcription reads 'alu dies'; Wirszubski's own quotation of the same conclusio (his 'Conclusio VI') reads 'alii dies' at both of its occurrences (wirszubski1989:1479, 1482), which is followed here as the evidently correct reading of an OCR-damaged word in Farmer's copy-text.

English

The great North is the source of all souls absolutely, just as other 'days' are [the source] of some, and not of all. unverified draft

'Aquilo' (the north wind/the North) and 'dies' ('days') are technical terms of a Kabbalistic scheme of the emanation of souls, on which Farmer directs comparison to Scholem (farmer1998:17661); 'quarundam' (feminine genitive plural, 'of some [souls]') is understood to govern 'animarum' carried over from the first clause.

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-swarm-batch7, from Latin at farmer1998:17637

Attribution: Reports Hebrew Cabalists (farmer1998:17637). · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

Farmer directs the reader to Scholem's discussion of the emanation of souls in the Kabbalah for the doctrine's background, glossing 'dies' simply as 'days' (farmer1998:17661). Wirszubski identifies the thesis as his 'Conclusio VI' and begins an analysis by stating that 'the interpretation of this thesis depends entirely on the precise location of' [the referent], though the material consulted breaks off before giving his conclusion (wirszubski1989:1482).

Context

Farmer's cross-reference network links the thesis to two Cabalistic (own-opinion) conclusions confirming the Christian religion that likewise invoke 'Aquilo' (11>37 at farmer1998:27217, 11>47 at farmer1998:27343), and to 28.8 in the same Hebrew Cabalists section, on souls descending from the third light to the fourth and fifth days (farmer1998:17645).
