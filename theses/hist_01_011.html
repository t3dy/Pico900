Latin

Stat speciem cuius dicimur reminisci esse totaliter deperditam et abolitam.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 11141

English

It can be maintained that the species of which we are said to be reminded is wholly lost and destroyed. unverified draft

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch1, from Latin at farmer1998:11141

Attribution: Reports Albert the Great (farmer1998:11141). · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

Farmer's note glosses 'image' in the surrounding discussion as the image of God, and cross-refers Albert's Summa theologiae on the state of innocence as one burdened by neither fault nor punishment (farmer1998:11166). The note contrasts this with a thesis where Pico is said to suggest a contrary view, and with the Heptaplus, which states that through grace human nature can be restored to its earlier dignity (farmer1998:11166).

Historiography

Farmer's note explicitly resists claims he considers excessive, made elsewhere, about Pico and human dignity, arguing instead that Pico's views on the topic rest solidly on ancient and medieval tradition (farmer1998:11166). Edelheit's chapter reports that thesis 11 holds that the species of which we are said to remember is entirely lost and abolished, and relates it to Albert's commentary on De anima, Book 3, on the separate intellect (edelheit2022:9727). Edelheit notes that he could not find the specific reference to Avicenna in this context, though the thesis could plausibly derive from Albert's discussion (edelheit2022:9727).
