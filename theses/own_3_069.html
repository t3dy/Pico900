Latin

Rationabile est secundum philosophiam omnem seriem animalem in proprio beatificari intellectu; quod tamen non assertiue sed probabiliter dictum sit.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 21348

English

It is reasonable, according to philosophy, that every series of souls should be made blessed in its own intellect; let this, however, be said not assertively but probably. unverified draft

Pico appends an explicit epistemic qualifier, marking the claim as held only probably and not asserted outright (farmer1998:21348).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch14, from Latin at farmer1998:21348

Attribution: Among the conclusions Pico titles 'paradoxical, according to his own opinion, introducing new teachings in philosophy'; the third of the cluster grouped by Farmer's note with 3>67-68 (farmer1998:21373). · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

The thesis completes the cluster begun at 3>67 by asserting that every series of souls attains beatitude in its own proper intellect, while explicitly hedging the claim as merely probable rather than asserted (farmer1998:21348); Farmer's cross-reference apparatus again links it to the Averroist theses on the unity of intellect, 7.2 (farmer1998:12937), 7.3 (farmer1998:12940), and 7.4 (farmer1998:12947).

Historiography

An unauthored German-language study reads Pico's distinction here between what he asserts and what he only holds probably as one instance of a distinction that, per a claim it attributes to Farmer's own book at pages 97 to 101 as cited there, came to shape the argumentative structure of the Conclusiones as a whole; this claim is reported here at second hand from the citing footnote and has not been checked against that discussion directly (kabbalah_paradigma:1760).
