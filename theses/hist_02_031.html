Doctrine

The thesis states the real distinction between essence and existence in every created being, the doctrine by which Thomas Aquinas separated the metaphysics of creatures, in which essence and existence differ, from that of God, in whom they are identical (edelheit2022:12161). Edelheit reports that Thomas discusses the distinction in De ente et essentia, arguing that a creature's essence or quiddity can be understood without knowing whether it exists in reality, since 'I can understand what a man or a phoenix is and yet not know whether it has being in nature' (edelheit2022:12189).

Historiography

Edelheit (2022) reads thesis 31 as moving from the unity of being (thesis 30) to the further scholastic distinction between essence and existence, and identifies Thomas's De ente et essentia as its source text (edelheit2022:12161, 12189).
