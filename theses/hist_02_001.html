Doctrine

The thesis opens a series on the distinction of Persons in the Trinity, drawn from medieval commentary on Book 1 of Peter Lombard's Sentences (farmer1998:11290). Edelheit reports that thesis 1 states that if the Spirit did not proceed from the Son, it would not be distinguished from the Son, and locates it in Thomas's Quaestiones disputatae de potentia, question 10, article 5, whose title he gives as asking whether the Holy Spirit would remain distinct from the Son if it did not proceed from him (edelheit2022:10234). Farmer notes that Pico's handling of distinctions in the divine nature was doctrinally difficult for him, since his own theological theses tend to argue for the indivisibility of God's nature while dogmatic constraint required him to accept the Trinity (farmer1998:11294).

Context

Farmer connects the series beginning at 2.1 to Pico's own-opinion theses 4>5, 4>26 and 4>27, and to Pico's project, in other sections of the 900, of linking Christian trinitarian distinctions to the Neoplatonic henads (theses 24.1-55) and the Kabbalistic sefirot (theses 28.1-47 and 11>1-72) (farmer1998:11294).

Historiography

Edelheit (2022) treats thesis 1 as opening his chapter comparing Pico and Thomas, remarking that the series shows both real overlap in themes and a difference in motivation, focus and competence between the two (edelheit2022:10234).
