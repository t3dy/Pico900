Latin

De re nominaliter concepta, primus conceptus qui habetur est conceptus rei conceptae proprius et conuertibilis.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 18698

Apparatus (Farmer): 1486 est conceptus universalissimus. | Emendationes errorum, corrige: est conceptus rei conceptae proprius et convertibilis. | 1487 text not emended

The sheet's underscore in 'habetur_est' is restored to a space. 1486 reads 'est conceptus universalissimus'; the Emendationes errorum corrects to the reading given here ('est conceptus rei conceptae proprius et convertibilis'); the 1487 reprint left the error unemended, apparently having swapped this reading with that of 1>11 (Farmer's note at farmer1998:18731).

English

When a thing is conceived by its name, the concept first grasped is the concept proper to, and convertible with, that conceived thing. unverified draft

'nominaliter' (1>10) versus 'definitiue' (1>11) mark two modes of conceiving a thing — by name versus by definition — kept distinct in translation.
