Latin

Per modum legendi sine punctis in lege, et modus scrıbendi res diuinas, et unialis continentia per indeterminatum ambitum rerum diuinarum, nobis ostenditur.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 27932

English

By the manner of reading without points in the Law, the manner of writing divine things, and the unified containment of divine matters through an indeterminate range, are shown to us. unverified draft

'Unialis continentia per indeterminatum ambitum rerum diuinarum' is the thesis's crux, glossed by Wirszubski as meaning that 'the underlying unity and continuity of divine revelation admit of endless variations of meaning' (wirszubski1989:9272).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch21, from Latin at farmer1998:27932

Attribution: Pico's own opinion (farmer1998:27932). · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

The thesis concerns the textual form of the Torah rather than a single point of Kabbalistic detail, since Farmer's note states that to read Hebrew 'without points' (vowel signs) effectively removes divisions in the text (farmer1998:28012). Wirszubski argues that because an unvocalized consonantal text can be read and combined in more than one way, the thesis's 'unialis continentia per indeterminatum ambitum rerum divinarum' means that 'the underlying unity and continuity of divine revelation admit of endless variations of meaning' (wirszubski1989:9272), which he connects to the Rabbinic maxim, quoted by him, that "The Torah has seventy faces" (wirszubski1989:9272).

Historiography

Wirszubski observes that the theses considered up to this point 'touch upon points of detail', while the seventieth 'concerns the Law as a whole' (wirszubski1989:9243). Of the thesis's central phrase he states, "This is in fact what to my mind Pico means by 'unialis continentia per indeterminatum ambitum rerum divinarum.'" (wirszubski1989:9272). The excerpt consulted then breaks off mid-sentence at "No other thesis brings out Pico's awareness of" (wirszubski1989:9272).
