Latin

Per modum legendi sine punctis in lege, et modus scribendi res diuinas, et unialis continentia per indeterminatum ambitum rerum diuinarum, nobis ostenditur.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 27932

English

By the practice of reading the Law without its points, we are shown two things at once: how divine matters are to be written, and how their oneness is held together across a boundless range. unverified draft

'without points' means without Hebrew vowel signs, which effectively removes the divisions between words; Farmer's note suggests the underlying idea that the Torah is itself the 'great name of God', holding all real or possible things in an undivided state (farmer1998:28012).
