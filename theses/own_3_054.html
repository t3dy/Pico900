Latin

Ad probandum primam materiam esse, magis certificat uel uia numerorum, uel uia catholicae philosophiae, quam ulla ratio physica in qua sensus habeat introitum.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 21090

English

To prove that prime matter exists, either the way of numbers or the way of universal philosophy gives more certainty than any physical argument in which sense-perception has an entry. unverified draft

'catholicae philosophiae' is rendered 'universal philosophy'; 'catholicae' here means 'universal', not the later confessional sense (farmer1998:21090).
