Latin

Species intelligibiles non sunt necessariae, et eas ponere non est bonis peripateticis consentaneum.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 10925

English

Intelligible species are not necessary, and to posit them is not consistent with being a good Peripatetic. unverified draft

Doctrine

The thesis denies that intelligible species are necessary and states that positing them is inconsistent with being a good Peripatetic (farmer1998:10925). Farmer's note observes that this reverses Albert's accustomed view, apparently for polemical reasons (farmer1998:10995). The same note reports that the thesis opens a long series on the origin of intelligible images tied to Pico's planned reconciliation of Plato and Aristotle (farmer1998:10995).

Historiography

Edelheit cites the thesis within a discussion of Pico's ownership of Albert's works, cross-referencing Kibre's catalogue of Pico's library (edelheit2022:8929). The same note directs readers to Leen Spruit's history of the concept of intelligible species for further orientation (edelheit2022:8929).
