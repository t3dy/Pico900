Latin

Rectius foret illud Becadmin, quod ponit glosa chaldaica super dictione Bresit, exponere de sapientialibus ideis_quam de trigintaduabus uiis, ut dicunt alii Cabalistae; utrunque tamen est rectum in Cabala.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 27633

Apparatus (Farmer): 1486 Cabalistae.utrunque

English

It would be more correct to interpret that 'Becadmin,' which the Chaldean gloss on the term 'Bresit' sets down, as referring to the sapiential ideas, rather than to the thirty-two paths, as other Cabalists say; both, however, are correct within the Kabbalah. unverified draft

Farmer's apparatus records a minor spelling variant in the 1486 print at this point (farmer1998:27652); the entry follows Farmer's base text. 'Becadmin' and 'Bresit' are left transliterated: per Farmer's note, Pico glosses 'Becadmin' as meaning 'with' or 'through eternal things,' and 'Bresit' ('Bereshit') is the opening word of the Torah, 'in the beginning' (paraphrase; farmer1998:27669). 'sapientialibus ideis' (the sapiential ideas) are, per the same note, the Platonic ideas, for Pico originating at the highest level in Christ or 'Wisdom'; the 'thirty-two paths' are the combination of the twenty-two Hebrew letters and the ten sefirot (paraphrase; farmer1998:27669).

Doctrine

Farmer's note glosses 'Becadmin' as 'with' or 'through eternal things' and 'Bresit' as the opening word of the Torah, 'in the beginning'; it associates 'Bresit' with wisdom, the second sefirah and, for Pico, a symbol of Christ, and it identifies the 'thirty-two paths' as the combination of the twenty-two Hebrew letters with the ten sefirot from which, in this tradition, the Wisdom of God created the world (paraphrase; farmer1998:27669). The thesis thus concerns the eternity of the ideas, or of Christ, or of both, and holds both rival Cabalistic readings of the Chaldean gloss to be correct (farmer1998:27633).
