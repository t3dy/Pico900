Latin

Ordo librorum naturalis philosophiae ab Aristotele tradite est iste: Liber Physicorum, coeli et mundi, de generatione, metheororum, mineralium, de plantis, de generatione animalium, de partibus animalium, de progressu animalium, de anima, tum libri qui dicuntur parui naturales.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 19442; 1486 print folio <16v/17r>

Apparatus (Farmer): 1486 ab Aristotle est iste | Emendationes errorum, interpone: ab Aristotle tradite est

The sheet's line-break slash in 'metheororum,/mineralium' is repaired to a comma-and-space. 1486 reads 'ab Aristotle est iste' (omitting 'tradite'); the Emendationes errorum inserts 'tradite', giving the reading followed here, which (unlike other emendations in this stretch) the 1487 reprint adopted.

English

The order of the books of natural philosophy handed down by Aristotle is this: the book of the Physics, on the heaven and the world, on generation, the Meteorology, on minerals, on plants, on the generation of animals, on the parts of animals, on the progression of animals, on the soul, and then the books called the minor natural works. unverified draft
