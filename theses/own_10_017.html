Latin

Ex eisdem dictis potest intelligi cur in Symposio a Diotima Porus consilii filius, et Iesus in sacris litteris angelus magni consilii, nominetur.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 25900

Apparatus (Farmer): 1486 nominetur:

The 1486 apparatus records a variant at the sentence's close: '1486 nominetur:' (farmer1998:25913).

English

From the same sayings one can understand why, in the Symposium, Porus is named by Diotima the son of Counsel, and why Jesus, in the sacred writings, is named the angel of great counsel. unverified draft

'consilii filius' and 'angelus magni consilii' are kept close to the Latin word order to preserve the parallel Pico draws between the two epithets (farmer1998:25900).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch18, from Latin at farmer1998:25900

Attribution: Pico's own opinion. · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

The thesis draws a parallel between Diotima's naming of Porus as 'son of Counsel' in Plato's Symposium and the scriptural naming of Jesus as 'angel of great counsel', presenting the latter identification as following 'from the same sayings' as the two preceding Orphic-Kabbalistic theses (farmer1998:25900).

unverified draft

Context

Busi and Ebgi's commentary situates the thesis within a Christological reading tradition, citing Pseudo-Dionysius the Areopagite's Celestial Hierarchy as background to the identification of Jesus with the 'angel of great counsel' (busi2014:15985).

Reception

Busi and Ebgi quote the thesis's Latin text in full in their edition and gloss it as continuing the reasoning of the two preceding sources cited in the thesis (busi2014:15985).
