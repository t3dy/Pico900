Latin

Ratitudo formaliter cuiuslibet creati est respectus.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 12610

English

The formal ratitude of anything created is a relation. unverified draft

'ratitudo' is retained as a loanword ('ratitude'): a technical term in Henry of Ghent's vocabulary for the settled, confirmed formal reality of a created thing, roughly its being formally 'ratified' or established as what it is; 'respectus' is rendered 'relation' in the technical sense of a real relation (esse ad, a being-toward another) (farmer1998:12610).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-swarm-batch6, from Latin at farmer1998:12610

Attribution: Reports Henry of Ghent (farmer1998:12610). · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

Edelheit groups this thesis with theses 12 and 13 as together dealing with Henry's notion of relation, 'a crucial point in Henry's thought which can be found in many different parts of his works' (edelheit2022:15721). He states that thesis 11 corresponds to Henry's Quodlibet V, q. 2, where Henry discusses 'the double respect of relation as one of the accidental categories transferred from a created being towards God: with regard to its own foundation and with regard to its opposite' (edelheit2022:15738). Edelheit also connects theses 11 and 13 to Henry's discussion, in Quodlibet III, question 4, of whether a relation in divine matters remains a relation, and to the claim that every relation concerning the existence of created beings is founded or based on some accident (paraphrase, edelheit2022:15731).

Context

Farmer's note ties the series to theses 4.16-17 (farmer1998:12655); thesis 4.16, attributed to Scotus, holds that the relation of a creature to God is really the same as its foundation but formally distinct and distinct in the nature of the thing (Pico's Latin, farmer1998:12361), giving a rival, non-Henrician account of the same relation of creature to God against which the Henry series can be read.
