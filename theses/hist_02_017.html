Latin

Licet defendi possit quoquomodo creaturam posse creare, rationabilius tamen est credere potentiam creandi creaturae communicari non posse.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 11530

English

Although it can be defended, in some way, that a creature is able to create, it is nevertheless more reasonable to believe that the power to create cannot be communicated to a creature. unverified draft

Doctrine

The thesis concedes a qualified sense in which a creature might be said to create, while holding it more reasonable that the power of creation proper cannot be communicated to any creature (edelheit2022:11064). Farmer's note identifies the thesis as touching emanationism, a doctrine he says Thomas regarded as heretical, and states that Pico's own opposing view is set out in the Apology (Opera, 189) (farmer1998:11564).

Context

Farmer's note lists a wide network of related historical theses on emanation and causation (7.5, 8.7-8, 11.1, 13.5, 26.3) and records that Pico planned to treat aspects of emanationism through his 'way of numbers' at 7a>7 and 7a>26 (farmer1998:11564).

Historiography

Edelheit is the only scholar found discussing this thesis in the sources consulted; he describes it as 'somewhat complex in its structure', turning on two distinct senses of the concept of creation (edelheit2022:11064).
