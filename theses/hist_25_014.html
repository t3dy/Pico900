Latin

Quilibet numerus linearis symbolizat deos.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 17025

Apparatus (Farmer): 1486 Qilibet

The 1486 print reads 'Qilibet' for 'Quilibet' (farmer1998:17034).

English

Every linear number symbolizes the gods. unverified draft

Farmer glosses 'numerus linearis' as 'prime number', literally a number divisible only by itself, hence a symbol for indivisible divine powers (farmer1998:17106).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch9, from Latin at farmer1998:17025

Attribution: Reports Pythagorean mathematics (farmer1998:17025). · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

Farmer glosses 'linear number' as meaning a prime number, and, without claiming it as Pico's actual source, compares Theon of Smyrna's Mathematics Useful for Understanding Plato 1.6 (farmer1998:17106).

Context

Farmer's cross-reference network links the thesis to 1.6 from Albert the Great (farmer1998:17025).

Historiography

Allen (2017) cites this thesis together with 25.13, from Pico's Opera Omnia, Basel 1572, page 79, associating linear numbers in Ficino's Timaeus commentary with souls and minds mediating between center and circumference (allen2017:2688).
