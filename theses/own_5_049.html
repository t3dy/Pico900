Latin

Ex eo nomine quo dei nominant amorem, patet diligenter aduertenti quod in actu amoris non est foelicitas.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 22994; 1486 print folio <24v/25r>

English

From the name by which the gods name love, it is clear to one who attends carefully that happiness does not consist in the act of love. unverified draft

'foelicitas' is Farmer's transcription of 'felicitas' (happiness, beatitude); Busi and Ebgi's apparatus identifies the two sources behind 'the name by which the gods call love' (busi2014:6542).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch15, from Latin at farmer1998:22994

Attribution: Pico's own opinion, in the doctrine of Plato (section 5>, farmer1998:22132). · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

The thesis holds that from the name by which the gods call love, it is clear to a careful observer that happiness does not consist in the act of love (farmer1998:22994). Farmer's note places it in the series he begins discussing at thesis 2.12, together with 3>48-49, and starts to gloss 'the name which the gods call love' as a reference to the Phaedrus, though the note is truncated at the page citation in the material consulted (farmer1998:23063).

Historiography

Busi and Ebgi's apparatus identifies the two sources for 'the name by which the gods call love' as Plato, Phaedrus 252b, and Pseudo-Dionysius the Areopagite, De divinis nominibus I (589a) (busi2014:6542), and lists the thesis in their index of citations (busi2014:26620).
