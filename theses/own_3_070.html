Latin

Cum tres fuerint qui dicerent omnia esse unum: Zenophanes, Parmenides, et Melissus, uidebit qui diligenter eorum dicta perscrutabitur, Zenophanis unum illud esse quod est simpliciter unum. Vnum Parmenidis non unum absolute ut creditur, sed ens unum. Vnum Melissi esse unum habens ad unum Zenophanis extremalem correspondentiam.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 21353

Apparatus (Farmer): 1486 perscrutabitur.Zenophanes

English

Since there were three who said that all things are one -- Xenophanes, Parmenides, and Melissus -- whoever carefully examines their sayings will see that Xenophanes's one is that which is simply one; Parmenides's one is not, as is commonly believed, one absolutely, but a one-being; and Melissus's one has an extreme correspondence to Xenophanes's one. unverified draft
