Latin

Superior angelus illuminat inferioremnon quia ei uel obiectum presentet luminosum, uel quod in se est unitum illi particulariset et diuidat, sed quia inferioris intellectum conífortat et fortificat.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 12693; 1486 print folio <3v/4r>

The transcription at farmer1998:12693 runs 'inferioremnon' together and prints 'conífortat' for 'confortat'. A quotation of the printed text at edelheit2022:16671 confirms the wording 'inferiorem non', 'particulariset et dividat', and 'confortat et fortificat', showing that 'particulariset' stands in the printed text rather than being an artifact of this transcription.

English

A higher angel does not illuminate a lower one because it presents to it a luminous object, or because what is united in itself it particularizes and divides for the other, but because it strengthens and fortifies the lower one's understanding. unverified draft

Doctrine

Edelheit reports that the thesis declares that a superior angel illuminates an inferior one not because it presents to it a luminous object, or because it particularizes and divides what is in itself united, but rather because it strengthens and fortifies the inferior angel's intellect, citing Giles's Opera omnia III.2, page 234, on communication between superior and inferior angels by particularizing one universal species into many (paraphrase, edelheit2022:16616, edelheit2022:16671).

unverified draft

Context

Edelheit observes, in discussing theses 7-10, that a standard Aristotelian account is here attributed by Pico to Giles, and suggests this may be another indication of the haste with which Pico composed the 900 Theses, noting a similar incident he had already found among the theses attributed to Albert the Great (paraphrase, edelheit2022:16760).
