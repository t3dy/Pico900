Thesis 4.5

Latin

Praxis est operatio alterius potentiae ab intellectu, apta nata conformiter elici rationi rectae ad hoc: ut sit recta.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 12156

English

Practice (praxis) is an operation of a faculty other than the intellect, naturally suited to be elicited in conformity with right reason, so that it may be right. unverified draft

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch5, from Latin at farmer1998:12156

Attribution: Reports Duns Scotus, fifth of the theses drawn from his work, continuing a series that begins at 2.12 (farmer1998:12135, 12225). · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

The thesis defines 'practice' (praxis) as an operation of a faculty other than the intellect -- for Scotus, the will -- whose nature fits it to be elicited in conformity with right reason so that the act may itself be right (edelheit2022:13959). Farmer's note ties the thesis to the series beginning at 2.12 and observes that Pico adopts similar technical language in his own-opinion thesis 2>25 (farmer1998:12225).

unverified draft

Context

Edelheit quotes Scotus's own formulation, that praxis 'is an act of a power other than the intellect, naturally posterior to intellection, born to be elicited in conformity with right reason so that it may be a right act' (edelheit2022:14065), and separately quotes Scotus stating the premise, conceded 'by all,' that a practical habit extends in some way to praxis (edelheit2022:14065).

Historiography

Edelheit locates the discussion in Scotus's Lectura, Prologue, part 4, questions 1-2, and in a variant Wadding-edition formulation of the same doctrine, Opera omnia volume 5.1 page 112, noting a wording difference that 'might be the result of manuscript readings which were known to Pico but were rejected by the editors of more recent editions' (edelheit2022:14016).
