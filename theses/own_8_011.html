Latin

Dictum illud Zoroastris, ne exeas cum transit Lictor < >, / perfecte intelligetur per illud Exodi, quando sunt prohibiti Israhelite exire domos suas in transitu angeli interficientis primogenita aegyptiorum.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 24951; 1486 print folio <28r/28v>

Apparatus (Farmer): 1486 ne ex eas | 1486 trasit | 1487 omits blank space

The angle brackets mark a blank space left in the editio princeps, apparently for an Aramaic word never inserted; Farmer reports the break recurs nowhere else in the 1486 edition and reads it as evidence Pico had some Aramaic text in hand, most likely a Mithridates forgery (farmer1998:25017). Corazzol quotes the same conclusion with the reading 'exeat' where Farmer's text has 'exeas' (corazzol2019:644).

English

That saying of Zoroaster's, do not go out when the Lictor passes, will be perfectly understood through that passage of Exodus in which the Israelites are forbidden to leave their houses while the angel who kills the firstborn of the Egyptians passes by. unverified draft

'Lictor' is left untransliterated, as in Corazzol's translation of the same conclusion (corazzol2019:644).

Attribution: Pico's own opinion · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

The conclusion glosses an oracle of Zoroaster's forbidding one to go out while 'the Lictor' passes as a figure for the Exodus narrative in which the Israelites are commanded to stay in their houses during the night the angel of death kills the firstborn of Egypt (farmer1998:24951). Farmer reads the blank space left after 'Lictor' in the editio princeps as evidence that Pico had some Aramaic text in hand when he compiled this section of conclusions, most likely supplied by Mithridates (farmer1998:25017).
