Doctrine

The thesis states baldly that the rational soul is immortal, attributed to Alexander of Aphrodisias (farmer1998:14716). Farmer's note calls this a reversal of the usual reading of Alexander's views as reported by Averroes, and cross-references thesis 7.4, on the Averroist question of individual survival after death (farmer1998:14753; farmer1998:12947). Farmer suggests Pico may have been drawing on the spurious second book of Alexander's commentary on De anima, including a section entitled De intellectu (farmer1998:14753).

unverified draft

Context

Farmer's note connects the thesis to a later scholastic dispute at the University of Padua between Nicoletto Vernia and Agostino Nifo, both of whom, Farmer states, were linked to Pico, citing Mahoney's 1969 study for analysis of that later conflict (farmer1998:14753).

unverified draft

Historiography

Black treats this thesis as 'a pertinent example of the difficulty in interpreting the Conclusiones', citing it under the first of the Conclusiones secundum Alexandrum Aphrodiseum in the Schefer edition, page 62 (black2006:10082). He states that ascribing to Alexander the view that the rational soul is immortal is 'contrary to common understanding of Alexander's position', and that 'we have no information as to how Pico would have presented his case', referring to Nardi's study of Simplicius' commentary on De anima for further background (black2006:10082).
