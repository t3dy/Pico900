Latin

Qui ordinem hebraicae linguae profunde et radicaliter tenuerit, atque illum proportionabiliter in scientiis seruare nouerit, cuiuscunque scibilis perfecte inueniendi_normam et regulam habebit.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 21095

Apparatus (Farmer): 1486 linguae profundae | 1487 lingue profunde

Farmer records a variant reading between printings: '1486 linguae profundae | 1487 lingue profunde' (farmer1998:21104).

English

Whoever has grasped the order of the Hebrew language deeply and at its root, and knows how to preserve it proportionally in the sciences, will have the norm and rule for perfectly finding whatever is knowable. unverified draft

'radicaliter': foundational grasp. 'proportionabiliter': proportional extension. 'scibilis': the knowable.

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-swarm-batch3, from Latin at farmer1998:21095

Attribution: Pico's own opinion (farmer1998:21095). · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

Thesis 3>55 claims that a deep, foundational grasp of the order of the Hebrew language, applied proportionally to the sciences, yields the norm and rule for perfectly discovering any knowable thing (farmer1998:21095). Farmer's note connects the thesis to 'the correlative foundations of Pico's revolutio alphabetariae', referring to his own discussion, and directs the reader also to the cluster of theses gathered at his note to 28.33 (paraphrase; farmer1998:21169).

unverified draft

Context

Farmer's cross-reference apparatus ties the thesis to 11>2, Pico's fourfold division of speculative Kabbala including the 'alphabetariae revolutio' (farmer1998:26383), and to 3>56, on a third kind of distinction Pico calls 'inadequationis' (farmer1998:21181). Farmer's own note additionally directs the reader to the theses cross-referenced at 28.33 (paraphrase; farmer1998:21169), namely 28.47, 2>80, and 9>22.

unverified draft

Reception

Mandosio quotes the thesis's Latin directly alongside 28.33 and 9>22 in a discussion of Pico's views on Hebrew and combinatorial method (mandosio2012:474). A German study of Pico's kabbalistic paradigm notes the thesis in connection with the concepts of 'Cabala practica' and 'Kawwanah', pointing to Wirszubski's discussion at pages 140-144 and 147-148 (paraphrase; kabbalah_paradigma:2240).

unverified draft

Historiography

Wirszubski treats the thesis with explicit caution: "Unless I am mistaken, Kabbala was also in Pico's mind when he wrote the fifty-fifth thesis of the Conclusiones paradoxae" (wirszubski1989:9650) — a hedge that marks this 'paradoxical' (non-Cabalistic-section) thesis as one whose Kabbalistic content is inferred rather than asserted outright by the section heading.
