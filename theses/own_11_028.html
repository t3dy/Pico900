Latin

Per dictionem « IN2, quae bis ponitur in illo textu, in principio creauit deus Caelum et Terram, ego credo significan a Moyse creationem naturae intellectualiset naturae animalis, quae naturali ordine praecessit creationem caeli et terrae.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 27006

The bracketed sigil '« IN2' in the corpus OCR stands for a short Hebrew word (the particle appearing twice in Genesis 1:1) that Farmer's own note identifies only as '<et>' (farmer1998:27064); the OCR of Farmer's Hebrew type is corrupted at this point and the exact Hebrew has not been re-collated against the printed edition; 'significan' and 'naturae intellectualiset' likewise show OCR run-together spacing in the corpus copy (farmer1998:27006), against the properly spaced 'signiflcari' and 'naturae intellectualis et' of Wirszubski's transcription (wirszubski1989:8962).

English

By the term that is placed twice in that text, 'In the beginning God created heaven and earth,' I believe Moses signifies the creation of an intellectual nature and an animal nature, which in the order of nature preceded the creation of heaven and earth. unverified draft

'natura animalis' is rendered 'animal nature' rather than 'animate nature' to keep it distinct from 'natura intellectualis'; both are technical terms in the thesis's exegesis of Genesis 1:1, denoting created natures Pico takes as antecedent, in the order of nature though not of narrated time, to the heavens and the earth (farmer1998:27006).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-swarm-batch6, from Latin at farmer1998:27006

Attribution: Pico's own opinion (farmer1998:27006). · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

The thesis reads a Hebrew particle repeated twice in Genesis 1:1 as pointing to two prior creations, an intellectual nature and an animal nature, preceding in the order of nature the creation of heaven and earth (farmer1998:27064). An unsigned German study, *Erkenntnistheorie und christliche Kabbalah: Das Paradigma des Pico della Mirandola* (author not identified in the material opened), confirms this reading and cites Wirszubski's *Encounter* page 176 and, as a further source, a passage from *De secretis legis* page 217 (paraphrase, kabbalah_paradigma:1511).

Historiography

Wirszubski identifies the thesis as 'Conclusio xxviii' of the Cabalistic set and states he is considering it among 'the remaining nine' theses of the set 'to see if they shed some light on what Kabbala meant to Pico' (wirszubski1989:8960), quoting the Latin verbatim (wirszubski1989:8962) and again later in a discussion of a different, related hypothesis for the conversion of the Jews (wirszubski1989:12068).
