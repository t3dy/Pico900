Two accidents differing only in number are not in the same subject. unverified draft

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch4, from Latin at farmer1998:11777

Attribution: Reports Thomas Aquinas (farmer1998:11777). · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

Farmer explains that, apart from special cases such as the Eucharist, Thomas held that accidents draw their existence and unity from the substance in which they inhere, so that positing two numerically distinct but otherwise identical accidents in one subject would be contradictory (farmer1998:11859). Edelheit reports Thomas's Sentences commentary arguing that things 'mutually united and separated while remaining the same in number' [cannot be so related] (edelheit2022:12452, 12480).

unverified draft

Context

Farmer notes the Eucharist as the standing scholastic exception to the rule that accidents depend on their substance, since there the accidents of bread are held to remain without their substance (farmer1998:11859).

unverified draft

Historiography

Edelheit (2022) situates thesis 36 as running counter to a view he finds elsewhere in Pico's engagement with Ockham as mediated by Thomas (edelheit2022:12452); Farmer (1998) reads it as governed by the general Thomist principle that accidents' unity derives from their substantial subject, the Eucharist apart (farmer1998:11859).
