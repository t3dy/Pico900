Latin

Per nomen <N\N>, id est Vir, quod tribus litteris scribitur he, uau, et aleph, quod nomen deo propriissime attribuitur, et maxime conuenienter non solum ad Cabalistas, qui hoc expresse sepius dicunt, sed etiam ad theologiam Dionysii Areopagitae, per uiam Cabalae trinitatis mysterium, cum possibilitate incarnationis, nobis declaratur.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 27122

Apparatus (Farmer): 1486 V (=Vir) | 1487 U

The bracketed siglum '<N\N>' in the corpus OCR represents the Hebrew word the Latin itself glosses as 'Vir' (man), spelled with He, Vau, and Aleph (farmer1998:27122). The 1486 print reads the initial letter as V, for Vir; the 1487 reprint has U instead (farmer1998:27145).

English

By the name <N\N> — that is, 'Man' — spelled with the three letters He, Vau, and Aleph, a name most properly given to God, and most fittingly so not only among the Cabalists, who often say this outright, but also in the theology of Dionysius the Areopagite: by way of the Cabala the mystery of the Trinity, together with the possibility of the Incarnation, is declared to us. unverified draft

Doctrine

The thesis argues that a Hebrew word for 'Man', spelled with He, Vau, and Aleph and applied to God, discloses by Cabala both the Trinity and the possibility of the Incarnation, agreeing, Pico says, with the Cabalists' own explicit statements and with the theology of Dionysius the Areopagite (farmer1998:27122). The immediately following thesis, 11>35, holds that God considered strictly in himself, as infinite and one, is on that consideration understood to proceed into nothing outside himself, but only to be enclosed within himself (farmer1998:27129).
