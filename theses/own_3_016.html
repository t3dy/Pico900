Thesis 3>16

Latin

Rationabiliter posuit Aristoteles in suis scientiis primum principium de quolibet dici alterum contradictoriorum, et de nullo simul.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 20455

English

Aristotle reasonably laid down, as the first principle of his sciences, that of any subject one of a pair of contradictories is asserted, and of none are both asserted at once. unverified draft

Restates, in compressed form, the principle of non-contradiction together with the law of excluded middle (farmer1998:20455).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch13, from Latin at farmer1998:20455

Attribution: Pico's own opinion · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

The thesis affirms, as reasonably posited by Aristotle, the logical axiom that of any subject one member of a pair of contradictory predicates holds, and never both at once (farmer1998:20455). Farmer's note on the group 3>13-18 glosses the phrase "the first principle of everything"/"of nothing" in this thesis as referring, in Pico's usage, to God and prime matter, and cross-references the same wording at 3>52 and 3>70 (farmer1998:20526).

unverified draft

Context

The thesis opens a short run, 3>13 through 3>18, on whether contradictories can coincide (farmer1998:20445-20462). Farmer's note assigns the immediately preceding thesis, 3>15 ("Contradictoria coincidunt in natura uniali", farmer1998:20452), to the debate over Nicholas of Cusa's coincidence of opposites, and states that the idea was in fact a scholastic commonplace rather than evidence of direct dependence on Cusanus (farmer1998:20526). 3>16 restates the ordinary Aristotelian rule before the following theses, 3>17 ("In intellectu est hoc et illud, sed non est hoc extra illud", farmer1998:20459) and 3>18, argue for an exception in the intellectual and rational nature.

Historiography

The one scholarly source located for this thesis, an unsigned German study titled Erkenntnistheorie und christliche Kabbalah: Das Paradigma des Pico della Mirandola, lists 3>16 in a footnote alongside 3>15 and 3>18 without separate discussion of its content (kabbalah_paradigma:1705).
