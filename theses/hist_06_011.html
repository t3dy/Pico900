Thesis 6.11

Latin

Dato uacuo, si aliquid in eo moueatur, in instanti mouebitur.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 12790

English

Given a vacuum, if something were moved within it, it would be moved instantaneously. unverified draft

'dato uacuo': counterfactual vacuum. 'in instanti': instantaneously.

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-swarm-batch3, from Latin at farmer1998:12790

Attribution: Reports Giles of Rome (farmer1998:12790). · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

Thesis 6.11 holds that, given a vacuum, any motion within it would occur instantaneously (farmer1998:12790). Farmer situates the thesis in a debate opposed to Aquinas, 'who claimed that if a vacuum existed a heavy body would move at a finite speed to its "natural place"', against Giles's Aristotelian view that velocity is proportional to force and inversely proportional to resistance, so a vacuum's lack of resistance implies instantaneous motion (paraphrase; farmer1998:12838).

unverified draft

Context

Farmer's cross-reference apparatus ties the thesis to 2.37 of Thomas Aquinas, that heavy and light bodies are moved only by a generating mover or one that removes an impediment (farmer1998:11780). Edelheit groups thesis 11 with the natural-philosophical theses of the Giles series (8, 9, 11), against the theological cluster of theses 1-7 (edelheit2022:15969).

unverified draft

Reception

Edelheit discusses the thesis's scholastic background, quoting Giles's own question-titles from his Physics commentary: "utrum corpus in vacuo positum successive moveatur ad ubi" (whether a body placed in a vacuum moves successively to a place) (edelheit2022:4085), and notes it is also discussed by Giles in his "Reportatio of his commentary on the Sentences, 1, q. 6, entitled: 'whether the movement of an angel is measured by time'" (edelheit2022:16900).

unverified draft

Historiography

Edelheit reports a scholarly disagreement over the thesis's Aristotelian pedigree: "According to Caroti, this thesis is commonly held as a doctrine of Aristotle; however, he provides a citation from the Auctoritates Aristotelis which is not identical to what we have in Pico", citing 'Caroti, "Note sulle fonti medievali di Pico della Mirandola", pages 68-69' (edelheit2022:16921).
