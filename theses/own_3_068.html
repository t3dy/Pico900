Latin

Omnes animae coelestes in primo intellectu unum sunt.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 21345

English

All the celestial souls are one in the first intellect. unverified draft

'Primus intellectus' is left untranslated as a technical term rather than rendered 'first intellect' loosely; Farmer's note identifies 'celestial souls' here as the rational souls of the heavenly bodies (farmer1998:21373).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch14, from Latin at farmer1998:21345

Attribution: Among the conclusions Pico titles 'paradoxical, according to his own opinion, introducing new teachings in philosophy'; grouped by Farmer's note with 3>67 and 3>69 (farmer1998:21373). · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

Where the preceding thesis assigns one pure intellect to each series of souls, this one asserts that all celestial souls specifically are unified in a single, 'first' intellect (farmer1998:21345); Farmer's cross-reference apparatus again links the cluster 3>67-69 to the Averroist unity-of-intellect theses 7.2 (farmer1998:12937), 7.3 (farmer1998:12940), and 7.4 (farmer1998:12947).

Historiography

Dougherty's note tying 3>67-69 as a group to the Averroist thesis 7.2, 'Vna est anima intellectiua in omnibus hominibus' (dougherty2008:9367), applies to this thesis as one of the three in that cluster; an unauthored German-language study likewise quotes 3>67 and this thesis together in a note on Pico's Neoplatonic and Chaldean citations (kabbalah_paradigma:1442).
