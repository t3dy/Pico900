Doctrine

The thesis states that the organ of hearing is a nerve extended toward the hollow of the ear (farmer1998:11050; edelheit2022:9572). Edelheit calls it the ninth of a run of theses (7-10) on sense perception drawn from Albert, and finds its source in Albert's account of air held in the ear's inner part, the tympanum, where the audible nerve is expanded and moved by the surrounding air (edelheit2022:9548; edelheit2022:9579). He concludes that in both of his theses on hearing (7 and 9), Pico 'accurately reflects Albert's views, although he uses a slightly different terminology' (edelheit2022:9579).

Context

Farmer's note relates the thesis to Aristotle, De anima 2.8, and to the broader dispute between Aristotelians and followers of Galen over the location of the sensory organs, cross-referring to thesis 8.11 (from Avicenna) on the organ of smell, and to 2>76, where Pico posits a kind of double-truth reconciling Aristotle and the physicians on where the sensitive faculties reside (farmer1998:11121; farmer1998:13680; farmer1998:20108). Edelheit remarks: 'It is hard to tell though why Pico has chosen to dedicate several theses, for instance theses 7–10, to some very standard and rather limited accounts of sense perception' (edelheit2022:10159).

Historiography

Edelheit is the only scholar in the sources consulted to discuss this thesis; he treats it as one of a closely matched pair with thesis 7 in accurately restating Albert's physiology of hearing, while questioning the rationale for including such standard natural-philosophical material among the 900 theses (edelheit2022:9579; edelheit2022:10159).
