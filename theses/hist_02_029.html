Latin

In generatione substantiali fit resolutio usque ad materiam primam.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 11665

English

In substantial generation, dissolution occurs all the way down to prime matter. unverified draft

'resolutio' is glossed by Edelheit as tracing the effect back to its cause, not 'resolution' in a modern sense (edelheit2022:12020); rendered 'dissolution' to keep this technical force.

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-swarm-batch7, from Latin at farmer1998:11665

Attribution: Reports Thomas Aquinas (farmer1998:11665). · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

Farmer's note supplies the Thomist background: Thomas held that matter cannot exist without form and posited only one substantial form per created nature, so that generating a new substance required destroying any form previously in its matter; 'Pico upheld the opposing view that matter had a distinct form of its own preceding the substantial form,' directing comparison to 16.1-2, 2>12, and 2>70 (farmer1998:11743). Edelheit confirms that 'Pico in thesis 29 according to Thomas Aquinas deals with substantial generation, using the same standard terminology but stating that in substantial generation the resolution does take place all the way down to prime matter,' citing Pico's Latin at 900 Theses, page 226 (edelheit2022:4076, 4089).

unverified draft

Context

Edelheit contrasts the thesis with an Averroist-tradition view (his thesis 12, according to Averroes) in which substantial corruption need not proceed all the way to prime matter (edelheit2022:4076), and finds Pico's thesis 'in agreement with Thomas's view' as expressed in Thomas's commentary on Metaphysics, Book 8, Lecture 4 (edelheit2022:12020).

unverified draft

Historiography

Edelheit (2022), citing Caroti, notes that the idea behind this thesis 'can be found in many commentaries on De generatione et corruptione from the fourteenth and fifteenth centuries' and 'can also be found in the Auctoritates Aristotelis' (edelheit2022:12040).
