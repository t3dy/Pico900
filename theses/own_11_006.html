Latin

Trıa magna dei nomina quaternaria, quae sunt in secretis cabalistarum, per mirabilem appropriationem tribus personis trinitatis ita debere attribui, ut nomen <N?NN> sit patris, nomen «nmm» sit filii, nomen «?YTN» sit spiritus sancti, intelligere potest qui in scientia cabalae fuerit profundus.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 26487

Apparatus (Farmer): 1486 sit patris. Nomen ... filu. Nomen ... sancti. intelligere

Farmer's OCR renders the three four-letter Hebrew names as garbled sigla (and 'Trıa' for 'Tria' shows a dotless-i OCR artifact), and the 1486 print apparatus records the readings 'sit patris', 'Nomen … filii', 'Nomen … sancti', 'intelligere' (farmer1998:26509); Farmer's note identifies the three names as Ehyeh (Father), YHVH (Son) and Adonai (Holy Spirit) (farmer1998:26546).

English

Anyone deeply versed in the science of the Cabala can understand that the three great four-letter names of God hidden in the secrets of the Cabalists are, by a striking correspondence, to be assigned to the three Persons of the Trinity: one name to the Father, one to the Son, and one to the Holy Spirit. unverified draft

The three names themselves (Ehyeh, YHVH, Adonai, per farmer1998:26546) are omitted from the main translation because Farmer's transcription of the Hebrew is garbled in the OCR; see latin_note.

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-swarm-batch2, from Latin at farmer1998:26487

Attribution: Pico's own opinion (farmer1998:26487). · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

The thesis assigns three four-letter divine names to the three Persons of the Trinity by 'miraculous appropriation'; Wirszubski treats the thesis as inseparable from 'the equation of the Tetragrammaton with the second person of the Trinity' (wirszubski1989:8456), and gives the three names as aleph-he-yod-he (Ehyeh) for the Father, yod-he-waw-he (YHVH) for the Son, and aleph-daleth-nun-yod (Adonai) for the Holy Spirit (wirszubski1989:8458; wirszubski1989:10081).

Historiography

Busi's edition gives an Italian translation with the three Hebrew names reconstructed as ['hyh] for the Father, [Yhwh] for the Son, and ['dny] for the Holy Spirit (busi2014:21989). The study cited here as kabbalah_paradigma associates the three names with three sefirot, citing Greive's Die christliche Kabbalah for a parallel treatment (kabbalah_paradigma:1557).

Not established

  • Two records in this packet nominally attributed to 'Conclusio vi' in Wirszubski (wirszubski1989:9061, wirszubski1989:9767) quote Latin text unrelated to this thesis's wording (one on the three letters of a different divine name 'Is', one on the Chaldean interpreters and 'double wine', which belongs to 8>6); the packet itself flags both as unresolved between sections (28.n or 11>n), and neither is used here.
  • The precise sefirotic correspondences claimed by the kabbalah_paradigma study (kabbalah_paradigma:1557) are given in German and are reported here only in general terms, without endorsing a specific sefirah-to-Person mapping beyond what is stated.
