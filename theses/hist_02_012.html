Beatitudo est essentialiter in actu intellectus.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 11419

English

Beatitude is essentially in an act of the intellect. unverified draft

Edelheit quotes the printed thesis as 'Beatitudo est essentialiter in actu intellectus' (edelheit2022:10700). He also quotes its printed corollary, absent from Farmer's inventory line, as 'Correlarium: Nec fruitio, nec aliquis actus voluntatis, est essentialiter beatitudo' (edelheit2022:10700).

Doctrine

The thesis makes beatitude (final human happiness) consist essentially in an act of intellect rather than of will, with a printed corollary denying that fruition or any act of will is essentially beatitude (edelheit2022:10700). Edelheit reads it as opening a series concerned with the relative powers of intellect and will in achieving beatitude (edelheit2022:10678). Farmer's note ties the thesis to the same intellect-versus-will axis that runs through the 'unity of intellect' series beginning at thesis 7.2, and states that Pico again represents Aquinas as an intellectualist at theses 2.19 and 2.28 (farmer1998:11497).

Context

The thesis stands among a run of theses reported under Aquinas's name in the historical part of the 900 (section 2), the section through which Pico surveys positions before arguing his own view elsewhere in the work (farmer1998:11419).
