Latin

Possibilis est regressus a causa ad effectum negatione quam somniat Burleus.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 19327

Apparatus (Farmer): 1486 absque negatioe | 1486 Emendationes errorum, ubi scribitur : absque negatôe (sic) : Corrige: negatione | 1487 absque negotiatione

The 1486 text and its own Emendationes errorum disagree over this word, correcting it to 'negatione'; the 1487 reprint instead prints 'negotiatione' (farmer1998:19342). Farmer's note treats the 1487 correction as doubtful, since 'negotiatione' does not appear in Burley's commentary on the Posterior Analytics; 'negatione', as given in the sheet, is followed here (farmer1998:19354).

English

A regress from cause to effect is possible, even by the negation that Burley dreams of. unverified draft

'Burleus' = Walter Burley, an English scholastic who died around 1343 (farmer1998:19354). The crux over 'negatione'/'negotiatione' is Farmer's own and is not resolved here beyond following the sheet's reading 'negatione' (farmer1998:19354-19362); worth a human Latinist's eye.
