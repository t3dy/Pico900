Latin

Opus ab anima charitate formata elicitum meretur aeternam gloriam decondigno.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 11334

English

A work elicited by a soul formed by charity merits eternal glory by condign merit. unverified draft

'decondigno' (de condigno) is the technical term for merit measured by an equality between service and return, as against merit 'de congruo'; Edelheit cites the Catholic Encyclopedia's account of the distinction (edelheit2022:10539).

Attribution: Reports Thomas Aquinas (farmer1998:11334). · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

The thesis holds that an act elicited by a soul formed by charity merits eternal glory de condigno, by strict equivalence of merit and reward (farmer1998:11334). Edelheit reports that thesis 9 states that an act performed by a soul formed with charity is worthy of eternal glory due to condign merit, tracing it to Thomas's commentary on the Sentences, 2, distinction 28, question 1, article 2 (edelheit2022:10518). Farmer groups this thesis with 2.2-9 under the divine omnipotence/free will problem (farmer1998:11348).

Historiography

Edelheit (2022) suggests that Pico is here pushing a notion found in Thomas somewhat beyond its original scope, in response to a challenge from Ockham (edelheit2022:10518).
