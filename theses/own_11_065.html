Latin

Rectius est ut AMEN tipheret dicat et regnum, ut per uiam numeri ostenditur, quam quod dicat regnum tantum, ut quidam uolunt.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 27830

Apparatus (Farmer): 1487 Amen. thipheret

English

It is more correct that AMEN signify both Tiferet and Kingdom, as is shown by the way of number, than that it signify Kingdom alone, as some hold. unverified draft

Farmer's apparatus records a minor spelling variant in the 1487 print at this point (farmer1998:27852); the entry follows Farmer's base text. 'tipheret' and 'regnum' are left transliterated/glossed as the sixth and tenth sefirot respectively; Farmer's note explains that Pico intended to show AMEN's convertibility with both terms by some form of gematria, his 'way of number,' and that his identification of Tiferet with 'the great Adam,' and so with Christ, would carry further symbolic implications here (paraphrase; farmer1998:27858).

Doctrine

The thesis holds, against 'some' unnamed interpreters, that the word AMEN corresponds numerically (by gematria) to both Tiferet, the sixth sefirah, and Kingdom (Malkhut), the tenth, rather than to Kingdom alone (farmer1998:27830). Farmer's note observes that Pico's association of Tiferet with 'the great Adam,' and so with Christ, would extend this numerical claim into further symbolic equations, though the note does not specify what these would be (paraphrase; farmer1998:27858).
