Thesis 1.16

Latin

Potentia respectiua materiae non addit supra materiam rem, sed rationem.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 11159

English

The relative potency of matter adds to matter, over and above matter itself, not a thing, but a concept [ratio]. unverified draft

The res/ratio contrast distinguishes something existing concretely (res) from its formal or conceptual possibility (ratio), per Farmer's note (farmer1998:11224).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch2, from Latin at farmer1998:11159

Attribution: Reports Albert the Great (farmer1998:11159). · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

The thesis holds that a 'respective' potency in matter adds to matter nothing real, only a conceptual distinction (farmer1998:11159). Edelheit states that in thesis 16 potency regarding matter does not add anything to matter beyond reason (ratio), and traces this to Albert's commentary on the Physics, Book 1, Tract 3, Chapter 13, on matter understood as intelligible without privation and without form (edelheit2022:10060). Farmer's note reads the thesis as qualifying thesis 1.4, on the model of how Pico elsewhere used a sect leader's final thesis to qualify an earlier one, comparing 4.22 (from Scotus) and 7.41 (from Averroes) (farmer1998:11224).
