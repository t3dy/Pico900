Latin

Angeli intelligunt per habitum scientialem sibi connaturalem.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 12502

English

Angels understand through a scientific habit connatural to them. unverified draft

'Habitus scientialis... connaturalis': Farmer glosses 'habitus connaturalis' as a habit or infused property created simultaneously with a nature but not included in its formal definition or essence (paraphrase, farmer1998:12580).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch7, from Latin at farmer1998:12502

Attribution: Reports Henry of Ghent (farmer1998:12502; edelheit2022:15595). · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

Edelheit continues his joint treatment of theses 6 and 7 on angelology, reporting that thesis 7 holds that angels understand through a scientific habit which is natural to them, a doctrine he finds at Henry's Quodlibet V, question 14, where Henry discusses whether a separated intellect has its own proper natural habit (paraphrase, edelheit2022:15595). Farmer's note groups this thesis with a series of closely related theses on the knowledge of angels, intelligences and the intellectual nature, cross-referencing thesis 18.7 and Pico's own theses 5>41 and 7a>28-29, which use the 'way of numbers' (paraphrase, farmer1998:12580, farmer1998:12586).
