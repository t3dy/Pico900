Thesis 9>25

Latin

Sicut caracteres sunt proprii operi magico, ita numeri sunt proprii operi cabalae, medio existente inter utrosque et appropriabili per declinationem ad extrema usu litterarum.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 25475

English

Just as characters are proper to the work of magic, so numbers are proper to the work of Cabala, with a middle existing between the two, appropriable to either extreme by declination through the use of letters. unverified draft

'caracteres' (characters) means magical words in general rather than figures on astrological talismans, as Farmer argues against Frances Yates's reading (farmer1998:25541).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-swarm-batch1, from Latin at farmer1998:25475

Attribution: Pico's own opinion (farmer1998:25475). · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

Farmer glosses 'characters' as magical words in general, explicitly rejecting Frances Yates's view that the term refers to words inscribed on astrological talismans (farmer1998:25541). Pico's point, per Farmer, is that magical words can be translated into numbers and numbers into magical words, apparently through the word-number equations of gematria, and that the 'works' in view are those of contemplative or prophetic magic, as in the two preceding theses (farmer1998:25541).

unverified draft

Context

Farmer cross-references the thesis to five others: 11>12, on the impossibility of operating through pure Cabala without a rationally intellectual nature; 1>6, on the Thomist-Scotist distinction ex natura rei; 9>16 through 9>18, on the nature that is the 'horizon' of time and eternity and its relation to the magician; and 9>26, contrasting the effect of a pure and immediate Cabalistic work with what no magic can attain (farmer1998:26604; farmer1998:18596; farmer1998:25354; farmer1998:25358; farmer1998:25362; farmer1998:25480).

Historiography

Akopyan quotes the thesis together with its immediate predecessor (9>24) in a discussion of the relation between formal number and magical and Cabalistic operation (akopyan2018:618; akopyan2021:1347). Busi's edition and commentary situate the thesis among the magical conclusions and their restriction to natural magic (busi2014:1667; busi2014:9639). Wirszubski cites the thesis's Latin as evidence that "linguistic symbolism to numerical symbolism which makes itself felt throughout the translations of Mithridates is reflected in Pico's theses" (wirszubski1989:4353), and elsewhere calls it Pico's "twenty-fifth magical thesis" (wirszubski1989:7158). Howlett quotes and translates it, citing Farmer's edition directly (howlett2021:6198).
