Latin

Quod dicunt interpretes super dictum secundum Zoroastris, de duplici aere, aqua, et terra, nihil aliud sibi uult nisi quodlibet elementum quod potest diuidi per purum et impurum habere habitatores rationales_et irrationales; quod uero purum est tantum, rationales tantum.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 24826

English

What the interpreters say on Zoroaster's second saying, concerning the twofold air, water, and earth, means nothing other than that any element which can be divided into pure and impure has both rational and irrational inhabitants; but what is pure only has rational inhabitants only. unverified draft

The underscore printed in 'rationales_et' reproduces an artifact of Farmer's transcription as given at farmer1998:24826, and is not corrected here.
