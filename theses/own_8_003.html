Latin

Quod dicunt interpretes super dictum secundum Zoroastris, de duplici aere, aqua, et terra, nihil aliud sibi uult nisi quodlibet elementum quod potest diuidi per purum et impurum habere habitatores rationales et irrationales; quod uero purum est tantum, rationales tantum.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 24826

The sheet's OCR text joined 'rationales' and 'et' as 'rationales_et'; restored as two words. No other correction needed.

English

When the interpreters comment on Zoroaster's second saying, about the twofold air, water and earth, they mean only this: any element that can be divided into pure and impure has both rational and irrational dwellers, whereas an element that is wholly pure has rational dwellers alone. unverified draft
