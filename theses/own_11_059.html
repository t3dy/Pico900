Latin

Qui profunde considerauerit quadruplicem rerum statum: Primo unionis et stabilitae mansionis, Secundo processionis, Tertio reuersionis, Quarto beatificae reunionis, videbit litteram Beth cum prima littera_primum, cum media_medium, cum ultimis_ultima, operari.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 27638

Apparatus (Farmer): 1486 reumonis. Videbit

English

Whoever will have deeply considered the fourfold state of things -- first, of union and stable abiding; second, of procession; third, of reversion; fourth, of beatific reunion -- will see that the letter Bet operates the first with the first letter, the middle with the middle letter, the last with the last letters. unverified draft

Farmer's apparatus records a minor spelling variant in the 1486 print at this point (farmer1998:27655); the entry follows Farmer's base text. 'Beth' (the Hebrew letter Bet) is left transliterated (farmer1998:27638). Farmer's note reports that Wirszubski's reading of this obscure thesis (cited by Farmer to his own 1974 and 1989 studies) treats a Hebrew alphabet of twenty-seven letters, counting as two letters the five consonants that change form at the end of a word, in which the first letter is alef, the middle nun, and the last two shin and tav; combining Bet with the first, middle, and last letters is said to yield the Hebrew words for Father, Son, and Sabbath respectively, corresponding to the thesis's stated 'indwelling,' 'procession,' and 'reversion and beatific reunion' (paraphrase; farmer1998:27714).

Doctrine

The thesis proposes a fourfold scheme of union, procession, reversion, and beatific reunion, and claims that combining the Hebrew letter Bet with the first, middle, and last letters of the alphabet reflects this scheme (farmer1998:27638). Farmer's note reports Wirszubski's reading of the obscure combinatorics involved as plausible, connecting the resulting words for Father, Son, and Sabbath to the thesis's four terms, and cross-refers the association of Christ with 'procession' to thesis 2.1 (paraphrase; farmer1998:27714).

Historiography

Farmer's note credits Wirszubski's studies of 1974 and 1989 with the plausible decipherment of the thesis's letter-combinatorics (paraphrase; farmer1998:27714). Wirszubski's Pico della Mirandola's Encounter with Jewish Mysticism itself groups the thesis with two others as 'three interrelated theses' and quotes its Latin in full (wirszubski1989:8352, 8353).
