Latin

Opifex mundi est supermundana anıma.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 15442

Farmer's OCR renders the final word with a dotless 'ı' ('anıma'); read as 'anima' (soul) (farmer1998:15442).

English

The maker of the world is a supramundane soul. unverified draft

'Opifex' (craftsman/maker) renders the Platonic demiurge; 'supermundana anima' names a soul-level principle above the sensible world, per Farmer's note locating the thesis among a set on the world's demiurge (farmer1998:15526).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch9, from Latin at farmer1998:15442

Attribution: Reports Porphyry (farmer1998:15442). · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

Farmer identifies this thesis, together with 22.1 and 22.3, as drawn from Proclus's commentary on the Timaeus (Diehl 1:300), a set Farmer suggests reading alongside 23.2 from Iamblichus on the same theme of the world's craftsman (farmer1998:15526). Steiris reads the thesis as Pico's report, following Porphyry, that the demiurge of the world is to be identified with a soul rather than with a higher hypostasis (steiris2014:465).

unverified draft

Context

The thesis belongs to the run of conclusions 'secundum Porphyrium' (farmer1998:15442).

unverified draft

Historiography

The only modern discussion located is Steiris (2014), who cites the thesis as evidence of Pico's engagement, via Anaxagoras, with the lower Neoplatonic hypostases of Intellect and Soul (steiris2014:465).
