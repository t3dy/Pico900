English

There is one intellective soul in all human beings. unverified draft

'anima intellectiua' is the intellective soul; the thesis states in bare form the doctrine of the unity of the intellect associated with Averroes (farmer1998:12937).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch8, from Latin at farmer1998:12937

Attribution: Reports Averroes (farmer1998:12937). · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

The thesis asserts flatly that there is one intellective soul in all human beings (farmer1998:12937). Farmer's note groups it together with the two theses that follow, 7.3 and 7.4, under the problem of the unity of the intellect, and directs the reader to Pico's own theses 3>67-69 (farmer1998:12967). A footnote in the Dougherty anthology quotes the Latin of this thesis verbatim and likewise cross-references Conclusions 3>67-9 (dougherty2008:9366).

unverified draft

Context

Farmer's cross-reference network for this run of theses also includes 18.1, attributed to Alexander of Aphrodisias, which asserts that the rational soul is immortal (farmer1998:14716), and 20.7, attributed to Plotinus, on the soul's final happiness in union with the first intellect (farmer1998:15105); both bear on the same question of the individuality and fate of the intellect that this thesis raises in Averroes' name.
