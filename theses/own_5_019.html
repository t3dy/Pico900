Plato · thesis 5>19

Thesis 5>19

Work in progress. This entry is a draft awaiting verification: every claim carries a locator, but no second reader has yet re-found each one.

Latin

Possibile est ut pars rationalis animae nostrae, quam secundum peripateticos possibilem intellectum uoco, ad hoc perueniat, ut sine coniunctione ad phantasmata discurrat et operetur.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 22458

English

It is possible for the rational part of our soul, which I call, following the Peripatetics, the possible intellect, to reach the point where it reasons and acts without conjunction to phantasms. unverified draft

'possibilem intellectum' is the Peripatetic (Aristotelian/Averroist) 'possible' or receptive intellect, capable in principle of becoming all things; 'phantasmata' are the sense-derived mental images that, on the standard Aristotelian account, the intellect must turn to in order to think; 'coniunctio' ('conjunction') is the technical term for the intellect's dependence on, or union with, such images, which the thesis claims the rational soul can reach a state of operating without (farmer1998:22458).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-swarm-batch6, from Latin at farmer1998:22458

Attribution: Pico's own opinion (farmer1998:22458). · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

Farmer's note ties the thesis to Proclus, In Timaeum 2 (see Taylor's translation, 1:207-8), and calls it "pertinent to Pico's syncretic fusion of the Platonic and Aristotelian theories of knowledge" (farmer1998:22531). Farmer cross-refers it to thesis 1.6 (Albert the Great), which holds that the separated soul understands through species concreated with it from the beginning of its existence, species it uses rarely or never while still in the body (farmer1998:11040) — a parallel claim that the rational soul can operate independently of what it draws from the body and the senses.

Historiography

Dougherty's volume cites thesis 5>19 in a footnote alongside 1.6 as instances of Pico's claim that the possible intellect can operate without turning to phantasms, referring the reader to Farmer's commentary at 102-5 (dougherty2008:9528). An unsigned German study, *Erkenntnistheorie und christliche Kabbalah: Das Paradigma des Pico della Mirandola* (author not identified in the material opened), lists 5>19 among the few theses drawn on for Pico's doctrine of Plato, noting that the bulk of Plato's doctrine is instead claimed for discussion by the first paradoxical conclusion (kabbalah_paradigma:1326). Mandosio cites 5>19 together with 1.6 and thesis 31 ("Non dari species intelligibiles a phantasmatibus abstractas") as evidence for Pico's position on intelligible species, referring likewise to Farmer's commentary at 102-105 (mandosio2012:309).
