Doctrine

The thesis holds that the three divine Persons could together have taken up a single nature as their common supposit (farmer1998:11412). Copenhaver explains that everyone agreed there are three supposits in the Trinity, one for each Person, so that Pico's thesis raises the counterfactual of a single supposited nature shared among the three (copenhaver2022:5494). Edelheit reports that thesis 10 states that the three divine Persons can at once assume one nature, and locates it in Thomas's Summa contra gentiles, 4, chapter 39, number 3, where hypostatic union and union in supposit in Christ are discussed (edelheit2022:10573). Farmer's note treats this thesis under the limits of divine power, and reports that Thomas, in his Sentences commentary, 3, distinction 1, question 2, article 4, held that while all three Persons assuming human nature as a single Person would be contradictory, no contradiction follows from God having assumed one nature as three Persons (farmer1998:11445). Farmer adds that Pico himself dispensed with the principle of noncontradiction with respect to the divine nature (farmer1998:11445).

Historiography

Both Copenhaver (2022) and Edelheit (2022) treat this thesis as a Trinitarian speculation on shared supposit, with Edelheit specifying Thomas's Summa contra gentiles passage as the source and noting Ockham's parallel doctrine of condign merit in his gloss on the neighboring thesis 2.9 (edelheit2022:10604).
