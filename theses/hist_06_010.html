Thesis 6.10

Latin

Ad hoc, quod aliqua scientia alteri non subalternetur, sufficit quod faciat reductionem ad per se nota in suo genere abstractions.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 12786

English

For some science not to be subordinated to another, it suffices that it perform a reduction to what is known per se within its own genus of abstraction. unverified draft

'subalternetur': subordination of sciences. 'genere abstractionis': abstraction-degree classification.

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-swarm-batch3, from Latin at farmer1998:12786

Attribution: Reports Giles of Rome (farmer1998:12786). · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

Thesis 6.10 holds that a science escapes subordination to another provided it can reduce its inquiries to principles self-evident within its own genus of abstraction (farmer1998:12786). Farmer calls the thesis 'the premodern antecedent of Gödel's second theorem', tracing it to commentary on Aristotle's Posterior Analytics 1.9-10, and glosses the underlying picture as an interlocked hierarchy of sciences, each operating independently of its superiors only when restricting its questions to a 'derivative sort' (paraphrase; farmer1998:12806). Edelheit places the thesis within Giles's own discussion 'of the subordination of one science to another, in regard to demonstrations' (edelheit2022:16848), and finds a parallel discussion in Giles's Reportatio on the Sentences (edelheit2022:16848).

Context

Farmer's cross-reference apparatus ties the thesis to 1.9 of Albert the Great, on the organ of hearing (farmer1998:11050); to 12.2 of Moses of Egypt (Maimonides), on metaphysics not being a single science (farmer1998:14143); and to 7.14 of Averroes, that no science proves the existence of its own subject (farmer1998:13145). Edelheit groups thesis 10 with the 'last 4 theses' of the Giles series, stating: "The last 4 theses are focused on natural philosophy (theses 8, 9, and 11), and on the subordination of sciences (thesis 10)" (edelheit2022:16746).

Reception

Edelheit quotes the Latin of the thesis directly, citing 'Pico, 900 Theses, page 248' (edelheit2022:16870), and identifies Giles's own Super libros Posteriorum Analyticorum (Venice, 1488; repr. Frankfurt, 1967), ff. f6v-f8r, as the corresponding scholastic locus (edelheit2022:16870).

unverified draft

Historiography

Edelheit assesses the relation between Pico's thesis and Giles's source text with a qualified judgment: "In thesis 10, on the subordination of sciences, a contrast seem to be emerging between what we have in Pico and what is to be found in Giles" (edelheit2022:16982). "But in most cases the very same ideas can be found in Giles, even if Pico uses his own formulations and terminology" (edelheit2022:16982).
