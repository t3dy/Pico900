Latin

Si foelicitas sit in speculatiua perfectione, mathematicae non faciunt ad foelicitatem.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 23616

English

If happiness consists in speculative perfection, the mathematical sciences do not conduce to happiness. unverified draft

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch16, from Latin at farmer1998:23616

Attribution: Pico's own opinion · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

This is the second of five conclusions on mathematics that Pico states as his own opinion (7>1-7>5), a set Farmer reads as directed against the fourteenth-century calculatores, who applied mathematical treatment to physical questions traditionally handled qualitatively in Aristotelian physics (farmer1998:23698). Farmer adds that the wider claim that Renaissance Platonism promoted the mathematical physics of the later scientific revolution finds little support in this group of theses (farmer1998:23698).

Historiography

Black quotes the Latin of this conclusion alongside conclusions 4 and 6 of the same series in a discussion of the numerical questions Pico promises to answer in the Conclusiones (black2006:10041). Cassirer's 1942 study cites the conclusion together with 7>1 and 7>3 as evidence for Pico's early view that a supersensible knowledge alone can disclose the obscure, supersensible nature of things (cassirer1942:1134).
