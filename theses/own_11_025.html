Thesis 11>25

Latin

Quilibet cabalista habet concedere_quod messias eos a captiuitate Diabolica et non temporali erat liberaturus.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 26917

The underscore in 'concedere_quod' reproduces an OCR artifact for a word-space in the corpus copy of Farmer's edition (farmer1998:26917); a modern-spelling quotation of the same clause in kabbalah_paradigma prints it as two separate words, 'concedere quod' (kabbalah_paradigma:2110).

English

Every Cabalist has to concede that the Messiah was going to free them from diabolical, and not temporal, captivity. unverified draft

The unstated antecedent of 'eos' ('them') is not supplied by this thesis alone; the point at issue is the kind of captivity — a spiritual, diabolical one rather than the Jews' political subjection — from which the Cabalists' own Messiah was to free his people (farmer1998:26917).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch19, from Latin at farmer1998:26917

Attribution: Pico's own opinion (farmer1998:26917). · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

Farmer's note groups this thesis with the next as views 'that could be drawn directly from a wide range of kabbalistic, Midrashic, or Talmudic texts' (farmer1998:26992). Thesis 11>26 makes a structurally similar move immediately afterward, holding that Cabalist authorities themselves concede that original sin will be expiated at the Messiah's coming (farmer1998:26921), and 11>22 similarly argues, from Cabalistic exegesis of the redness of Esau, to a claim about Christ (farmer1998:26817).
