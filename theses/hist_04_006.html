Vnumquodque indiuiduum est indiuiduum per propriam differentiam indiuidualem, quae dicitur hecheitas.

English

Each individual is an individual through its own individual difference, which is called haecceity. unverified draft

Doctrine

The thesis states the Scotist principle of individuation: each individual is individual through 'a special individual difference which is called haecceity or thisness' (edelheit2022:14090). Farmer's note glosses haecceity as the 'thisness' of a thing, a formal distinction differentiating each individual from every other (farmer1998:12269).

Context

Edelheit contrasts Pico's wording with the Scotist source, noting that where the Scotist discussion he cites uses actus ('act'), Pico's thesis prefers operatio ('operation'), which he judges makes Pico's phrasing 'a little more obscure' (edelheit2022:14090).

Historiography

Edelheit locates the doctrine of individuation in Scotus's Ordinatio 2, distinction 3, part 1, question 4, where 'Scotus wishes to clarify' what he means by individuation, quoting Scotus's opening statement of the question (edelheit2022:14090, 14114).
