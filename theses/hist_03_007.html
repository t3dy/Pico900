Doctrine

The thesis asserts that a 'plurality of formalities' -- the Scotist compromise between a real and a merely rational distinction -- can coexist with 'real identity' in one and the same thing (farmer1998:12090). Farmer's note explains that formalities were held to be metaphysically real yet inseparable, even by God, from the objects in which they were found, and that later scholastics invoked them to reconcile texts that stressed a thing's unity with texts that stressed its internal distinctions (farmer1998:12090). Edelheit identifies the source as the Tractatus formalitatum secundum doctrinam Francisci Mayronis (edelheit2022:13399), quoting its opening question, 'whether those things which are distinguished really are [also] distinguished formally' (edelheit2022:13426).
