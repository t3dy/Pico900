Latin

Habitus actum producit ut causa partialis effectiua.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 12382

English

A habit produces an act as a partial efficient cause. unverified draft

'Causa partialis effectiua': a partial efficient cause, that is, one that contributes to, but does not alone account for, the production of an act (farmer1998:12382).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch6, from Latin at farmer1998:12382

Attribution: Reports Scotus (farmer1998:12382). · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

The thesis holds that a habit produces an act as a partial efficient cause, a question Farmer notes 'involves the issue of free will' and locates in Scotus's Ordinatio, book 1, distinction 17, part 1, questions 1-2 (farmer1998:12463). Edelheit confirms that thesis 22 holds that habit produces an act as a partial and effective cause, found in the same Ordinatio question, titled 'on the habit of charity' (De habitu caritatis), and observes a terminological difference between Pico's and Scotus's wording (edelheit2022:15153, 15161).

unverified draft

Historiography

Edelheit notes that where Pico writes of habit as a cause 'ut causa partialis effectiva', Scotus himself writes 'habitus est causa partialis activa'; this is the only scholarly comment on thesis 22's wording identified in the material consulted, and Edelheit does not assess whether the difference is substantive (edelheit2022:15161).

Pico's sources

  • John Duns Scotus, Ordinatio 1, d. 17, pars 1, q. 1-2 ('De habitu caritatis'; Opera omnia): Edelheit identifies this question as the source of thesis 22's claim about habit as a partial efficient cause (edelheit2022:15153)

Quotations cited

Not established
