Quodcunque contingens deus sciuit esse futurum, necessario sciuit illud esse futurum.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 11321

English

Whatever contingent thing God knew would be future, he knew necessarily that it would be future. unverified draft

Doctrine

The thesis holds that whatever contingent future thing God knew, he knew it necessarily (farmer1998:11321). Edelheit states that thesis 5 holds that whatever future contingency God knows is known to him in a necessary fashion, and that this necessity is emphasized again by Thomas a little later in the same discussion as thesis 4, in the Summa, 1, question 14, article 13 (edelheit2022:10331). Farmer groups this thesis with 2.2-9 under the divine omnipotence/free will problem (farmer1998:11348).

Historiography

Edelheit (2022) reads theses 4 and 5 as a pair restating, in Pico's compressed form, the same page of Thomas's Summa, 1, question 14, article 13 (edelheit2022:10331).
