Porphyry · thesis 22.7

Thesis 22.7

Latin

Sicut Apollo est intellectus solaris, ita Aesculapius est intellectus lunaris.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 15459

English

Just as Apollo is the solar intellect, so Aesculapius is the lunar intellect. unverified draft

Compare Busi and Ebgi's own Italian rendering of the same thesis, 'Come Apollo è l'intelletto solare, cosi Asclepio è l'intelletto lunare' (busi2014:7251), which independently supports this reading.

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch9, from Latin at farmer1998:15459

Attribution: Reports Porphyry (farmer1998:15459). · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

Farmer places the thesis in a set (22.4-8) adapted from Proclus's Timaeus commentary that pits Porphyry's reading against Iamblichus's, the first of a series of theses involving astrological correspondences that Farmer stresses do not endorse the horoscopic astrology Pico attacks elsewhere (farmer1998:15531). Farmer glosses 'Apollo'/solar intellect as Pico's 'total' or 'angelic' intellect, and 'Aesculapius'/lunar intellect as the passive intellect or reason, comparing thesis 5>19 (farmer1998:15531).

unverified draft

Context

Busi and Ebgi likewise trace the thesis to Proclus's commentary on the Timaeus, reading it as Pico's Porphyrian identification of Apollo with the solar intellect (busi2014:7251).

unverified draft

Historiography

Busi and Ebgi (2014) cite the thesis, in a discussion of the Oration's Apolline solar symbolism, as evidence of Pico's derivation of the Apollo/solar-intellect identification from Proclus (busi2014:7251).
