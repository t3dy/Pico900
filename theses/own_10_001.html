Latin

Sicut secretam magiam a nobis primum ex Orphei hymnis elicitam_fas non est in publicum explicare, ita nutu quodam, ut in infrascriptis fiet conclusionibus, eam per amphorismorum capita demonstrasse, utile erit ad excitandas contemplatiuorum mentes.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 25569

English

Just as it is not permitted to set forth publicly the secret magic first drawn out by us from the hymns of Orpheus, so it will be useful, by a kind of hint, as will happen in the conclusions written below, to have shown it through the headings of aphorisms, so as to arouse the minds of contemplatives. unverified draft

'a nobis primum...elicitam' stakes a claim of originality; Farmer's note reads this claim of priority as aimed at Ficino, whose own commentary on the Orphic Hymns predates Pico's (farmer1998:25645). On Pico's technical use of 'aphorism' Farmer cross-refers to 8>6 and 11>57 (farmer1998:25645).

Doctrine

The thesis states Pico's method for the Orphic Hymns theses that follow: rather than publish the secret magic he claims first to have drawn from the Hymns, he will demonstrate it obliquely, by a 'kind of hint', through the headings of aphorisms (farmer1998:25569).

Context

Farmer's note reads Pico's stress on his own originality here as directed against Ficino, 'who years earlier had composed his own commentary on the Hymns' (farmer1998:25645).

Historiography

Wirszubski quotes the opening of this thesis verbatim from Pico's Opera, pages 106-107, connecting it to Pico's stated method of interpreting the Hymns 'secundum Magiam' (wirszubski1989:9987). Busi and Ebgi likewise print the thesis's Latin from the Conclusiones, page 504 (busi2014:17236).
