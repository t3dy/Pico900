Latin

Dato quocunque obiecto practicabili, nobilior est operatio quae eum practicat quam quae eum contemplatur, si caetera sint paria.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 20976

Apparatus (Farmer): 1486 coetera

The 1486 print read "coetera" for the corrected "caetera" ("other things") (farmer1998:20996).

English

Given any object capable of being acted upon, the operation that acts on it is nobler than the operation that only contemplates it, other things being equal. unverified draft

Doctrine

The thesis holds that, given any object capable of being acted upon, the operation that acts on it is nobler than the operation that only contemplates it, other things being equal (farmer1998:20976). Farmer's note connects the thesis to his discussion of Pico's magic, pages 130-31 (farmer1998:21009).

unverified draft

Context

Jean-Marc Mandosio quotes the thesis while glossing Pico's definition of natural magic, in the separate Magical Conclusions, as "the practical part of natural science" and its noblest part, and reports that Pico infers the nobility of magic from this general principle together with the corresponding Magical Conclusion (mandosio2012:565).

unverified draft

Historiography

Mandosio's article is the only scholarly discussion of the thesis located in the sources consulted, and treats it as the general principle underlying Pico's claim for the nobility of magic (mandosio2012:565).
