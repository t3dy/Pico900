Latin

Secundum precedentem conclusionem ex Procli mente potest exponi unum ex dictis Zoroastris secundum quod apud Graecos legitur, quanquam apud Chaldeos aliter et legitur et exponitur.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 16470

Wirszubski's transcription shows minor orthographic differences from Farmer's text: 'praecedentem' for 'precedentem', 'quamquam' for 'quanquam', and 'Chaldaeos' for 'Chaldeos' (wirszubski1989:12236).

English

According to the preceding conclusion, one of the sayings of Zoroaster can be explained, in Proclus's understanding, as it is read among the Greeks — although among the Chaldeans it is both read and explained differently. unverified draft

Doctrine

Farmer reads the thesis as reflecting Pico's claim to have possessed the Chaldean Oracles in an original Chaldean-language text, differing from the Greek transmission (farmer1998:16508). Corazzol situates the thesis within a set of 'Zoroastrian Conclusions' (8>5, 8>11, 8>14) drawing on a source he labels 'M', containing Latin translations of three Chaldean oracles, and reads 24.31 as a further reference to that same source (corazzol2019:192).

Context

Farmer's cross-reference network links the thesis to 24.13, 24.14, 24.30 and 24.4 on the trinities of the supermundane gods (farmer1998:16470).

Historiography

Corazzol (2019) glosses the thesis as Pico's claim that, in their original version, the Chaldean oracles not only read differently but also evinced a different meaning from the Greek tradition (corazzol2019:192). Wirszubski (1989) transcribes the thesis in an appendix (wirszubski1989:12236).
