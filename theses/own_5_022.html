Amor de quo in Symposio loquitur Plato, in deo nullo modo esse potest.

English

In no way can the love of which Plato speaks in the Symposium exist in God. unverified draft

Farmer's note reads the thesis as trading on a distinction between acquisitive and non-acquisitive love (eros and agape) (farmer1998:22584).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch15, from Latin at farmer1998:22553

Attribution: Pico's own opinion, in the doctrine of Plato (section 5>, farmer1998:22132). · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

The thesis denies that the love Plato discusses in the Symposium can exist in God in any way (farmer1998:22553). Farmer reads it as trading on a distinction between acquisitive and non-acquisitive love, eros and agape, and reports that Pico argues the same point against Ficino in his own Commento, that God can desire nothing beyond himself, comparing again Plotinus's Enneads 3.5 (farmer1998:22584).

unverified draft

Historiography

Busi and Ebgi print the Latin and an Italian translation of the thesis and connect it to Pico's reading of the Symposium myth elsewhere in his commentary on the Commento (busi2014:24377, busi2014:6596). Howlett quotes the Latin together with a passage from the 1986 English translation of Pico's own Commentary, in which Pico states that love is the cause of the production of everything that proceeds from God (howlett2021:7679).
