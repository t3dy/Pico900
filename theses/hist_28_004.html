Doctrine

The thesis reads Adam's sin kabbalistically, as the severing of 'the kingdom' — the tenth sefirah — from the other 'shoots', the higher sefirot figured as a tree, a standard symbol for the origin of sin found in the Zohar and in Recanati (farmer1998:17617). Farmer directs the reader to Scholem and to Wirszubski's own discussion of the same image (farmer1998:17617).

Historiography

Wirszubski identifies this as the fourth conclusion of Pico's Cabalistic set and quotes it twice in his discussion of its background (wirszubski1989:1390, 1393).

Pico's sources

  • the Zohar and Menahem Recanati's commentary (as cited via Scholem and Wirszubski): Farmer's note traces the 'severing of the shoots' image, here applied to Adam's sin, to this common kabbalistic association of the sefirot with the tree of paradise (farmer1998:17617)
