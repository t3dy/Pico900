Latin

Per Syrenam apud Zoroastrem nihil aliud intelligas quam partem animae rationalem.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 24956

English

By the Siren in Zoroaster, understand nothing other than the rational part of the soul. unverified draft

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch16, from Latin at farmer1998:24956

Attribution: Pico's own opinion · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

The conclusion is the first of a pair (8>12-8>13) that Farmer connects to 8>10's reading of Zoroaster's dog as the irrational part of the soul, extending the same interpretive method to the Siren as the rational soul and, in 8>13, the boy as the intellect (farmer1998:25026). Busi and Ebgi place the Siren, so understood, within a wider Homeric allegory in which Circe stands for the power superior to sense-knowledge and Calypso for fantasy, with the Siren corresponding to reason (busi2014:9112).

Historiography

Busi and Ebgi quote the Latin of the conclusion with an Italian translation in their edition, glossing the Siren as reason within this Homeric allegorical scheme (busi2014:9112).
