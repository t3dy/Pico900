Latin

Qui nouerit proprietatem meridionalem in dextrali coordinatione, sciet cur omnis profectio Abraam semper fit uersus austrum.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 17745; 1486 print folio <13r/13v>

English

Whoever knows the southern property within the right-hand ordering will know why every journey of Abraham is always made toward the south. unverified draft

Rendered directly from the Latin at farmer1998:17745, checked against Farmer's sense at farmer1998:17804.

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch10, from Latin at farmer1998:17804

Attribution: the Hebrew Cabalists · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

The thesis reads Abraham's journeys as symbols keyed to the 'right-hand order', which distinguishes the emanation of the true sefirot from the evil 'left-hand order' that mirrors it; 'southern property' and 'Abraham' are standard symbols of the fourth sefirah, called 'love' or 'piety' in Pico (farmer1998:17827). Farmer reports and disputes Wirszubski's further claim that the phrase 'right-hand order' derives from Mithridates' translation of Gikatilla's Portae Iustitiae, objecting that Pico's own phrase 'dextralis coordinatio' has no counterpart in that translation, which uses only 'dextra' (farmer1998:17827).

Historiography

Wirszubski quotes the Latin and proposes two possible source-texts without resolving between them in the passage consulted; Farmer's note reports, and contests, Wirszubski's further claim that the phrase 'right-hand order' derives from Mithridates' translation of Gikatilla (farmer1998:17827; wirszubski1989:1867).

Pico's sources

  • Genesis 12:9 (Abraham's journeys): the biblical narrative allegorized in the thesis as a figure of the fourth sefirah (farmer1998:17827)
  • Joseph Gikatilla, Portae Iustitiae, in Flavius Mithridates' Latin translation: proposed by Wirszubski, and disputed by Farmer, as the source of the phrase 'right-hand order' (farmer1998:17827)
