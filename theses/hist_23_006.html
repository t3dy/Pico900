Latin

Cum excellenter ad intellectum assimilatur anima, fit in uehiculo motus perfecte circularis.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 15750

English

When the soul is assimilated to intellect in a superior way, a perfectly circular motion occurs in the vehicle. unverified draft

'Vehiculum' renders the Neoplatonic okhema, the soul's subtle vehicle, per Farmer's cross-reference to his note on 5>45 (farmer1998:15816).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch9, from Latin at farmer1998:15750

Attribution: Reports Iamblichus (farmer1998:15750). · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

Farmer draws the thesis from Proclus's Timaeus commentary on the soul's 'vehicle', directing the reader to his note on thesis 5>45 for the concept, and reads this thesis as deliberately set against 20.11 from Plotinus (farmer1998:15816).

unverified draft

Context

The thesis stands opposite 20.11 (Plotinus) in Farmer's cross-reference network, and is linked by Farmer to 22.4 and 5>7 on related themes of the soul and the heavens (farmer1998:15816).

Historiography

Howlett (2021) cites the Latin of the thesis, from Farmer's edition, alongside passages of Pico's Heptaplus, in her study of Aristotelian, Kabbalistic and Platonic strands in Pico's philosophy (howlett2021:9242).
