Latin

Verum erit omnia pendere ex fato, si per fatum, fatum supremum intellexerimus.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 17737

English

It will be true that all things depend on fate, if by 'fate' we understand the supreme fate. unverified draft

Doctrine

The thesis qualifies the claim that all things depend on fate by restricting the term to 'supreme fate', implying that other, lower modes of fate are to be distinguished, which Farmer presumes correspond to different sefirot (farmer1998:17818). Farmer cross-references this thesis to the network of related conclusions gathered under his note to thesis 24.2 of Proclus (farmer1998:17818).

unverified draft

Historiography

Wirszubski quotes the thesis as the twelfth conclusion of the Cabalistic set and states that its source can be either of two closely similar texts, without identifying them in the passage consulted (wirszubski1989:1783, 1786).
