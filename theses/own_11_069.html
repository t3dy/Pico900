Thesis 11>69

Latin

Ex fundamento praecedentis conclusionis sciri pariter potest secretum quinquaginta portarum intelligentiae, et millesimae generationis, et regni omnium saeculorum.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 27927

English

From the foundation of the preceding conclusion can likewise be known the secret of the fifty gates of understanding, and of the thousandth generation, and of the kingdom of all ages. unverified draft

'Portarum intelligentiae' (gates of understanding): Farmer's note identifies 'intelligentia' here with Binah, which he calls the most common name of the third sefirah, and cites Scholem (1974: 113) (farmer1998:28007). Farmer's note treats 'thousandth generation' and 'kingdom of all ages' as further millennial and eschatological symbols tied to the numerology of the preceding thesis, 11>68 (farmer1998:28007).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch21, from Latin at farmer1998:27927

Attribution: Pico's own opinion (farmer1998:27927). · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

The thesis states that the numerological foundation laid in thesis 11>68 also yields three further secrets: the 'fifty gates of understanding,' the 'thousandth generation,' and the 'kingdom of all ages.' Farmer's note glosses all three as millennial and eschatological symbols continuing the numerology of thesis 68, and identifies 'intelligentia' with Binah, the third sefirah (farmer1998:28007).

Historiography

Black's study of the Heptaplus's biblical hermeneutics quotes a passage containing the phrase 'secretum quinquaginta portarum intelligentie' (black2006:11985), and Wirszubski's book contains a matching passage, on the Hebrew letter nun and its numerical value of fifty, using the same phrase (wirszubski1989:1851).
