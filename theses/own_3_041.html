Latin

Sicut se habet sensus communis in cognitione accidentalium qualitatum_et quantitatis materialis, ita se habet ratio in cognitione substantialium qualitatum et quantitatis formalis, seruata proportione, quod ille sensualiter, haec rationaliter, agit.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 20875

The underscore in Farmer's transcription stands for a word break ('et quantitatis'); reproduced verbatim (farmer1998:20875).

English

As the common sense stands in the cognition of accidental qualities and material quantity, so reason stands in the cognition of substantial qualities and formal quantity, the proportion being preserved, such that the former acts sensibly and the latter rationally. unverified draft
