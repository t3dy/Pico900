Latin

Si ponamus deum cognoscere creaturas ut obiectum secundarium suae intuitionis, ut communiter ponitur, dico quod pater prius producit uerbum quam creaturas cognoscat.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 21549; 1486 print folio <21r/21v>

English

Supposing, as is commonly held, that God knows creatures as a secondary object of his intuition, I say that the Father produces the Word before knowing created things. unverified draft

Argued conditionally from the common view's own premise; Pico's own account of divine cognition departs from an ordinary act of intellect altogether (cf. 3>43).
