Latin

Licet fiat multiplex coordinatio curruum, tamen inquantum attinet ad phylacteriorum mysterium duo sunt currus ordinandi, ita ut ex secunda, tertia, quarta, quinta, fiat unus currus, et sunt quatuor phylacteria quae induit Vau; et ex sexta, septima, octaua, et nona fit secundus currus, et sunt phylacteria quae induit ultima He.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 17959

Apparatus (Farmer): 1486 phylatteriorum ... phylatteria ... phylatteria | 1487 philatteriorum ... philatteria ... philatteria

The apparatus records 1486 'phylatteriorum ... phylatteria ... phylatteria' and 1487 'philatteriorum ... philatteria ... philatteria' for the orthography of 'phylacteriorum'/'phylacteria' (farmer1998:17978).

English

Although the chariots may be arranged in many ways, still, so far as concerns the mystery of the phylacteries, two chariots are to be set in order: from the second, third, fourth, and fifth [sefirot] one chariot is formed, and these are the four phylacteries that Vav puts on; and from the sixth, seventh, eighth, and ninth a second chariot is formed, and these are the phylacteries that the final He puts on. unverified draft

'currus' ('chariot') translates the merkabah of Ezekiel's vision (Ezek. 1:4ff.), on which Farmer directs comparison to Scholem (farmer1998:17995); 'Vau' and the 'final He' are letters of the Tetragrammaton, each here said to be 'invested with' (induere) a set of four sefirot as its phylacteries.

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch11, from Latin at farmer1998:17959

Attribution: Reports Hebrew Cabalists (farmer1998:17959). · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

Farmer glosses 'chariots' (chanot, merkabah in Hebrew) as the animated chariot of Ezekiel's vision (Ezekiel 1:4 and following), rendered plural in antiquity through centuries of attempts to harmonize earlier commentaries on that text with each other and with other Hebrew and pagan chariot myths, including the charioteer myth of the Phaedrus, and directs the reader to Scholem for further discussion, though the note as consulted breaks off after naming Pico (farmer1998:17995).
