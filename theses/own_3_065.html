Thesis 3>65

Latin

Quia intellectus animalis differt ab intellectu intellectuali ut pars media, non ut pars tantum, ideo intellectuali totalitati magis parificatur.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 21274; 1486 print folio <20v/21r>

English

Because the animal intellect differs from the intellectual intellect as a middle part, and not merely as a part, it is therefore the more made equal to the intellectual totality. unverified draft

'Pars media' (a mediating, not merely a subordinate, part) continues the part/whole vocabulary of the immediately preceding thesis, 3>64 (farmer1998:21270).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch14, from Latin at farmer1998:21274

Attribution: Among the conclusions Pico titles 'paradoxical, according to his own opinion, introducing new teachings in philosophy' (kabbalah_paradigma:1322). · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

The thesis refines the preceding one's part/whole account of intellect by distinguishing a merely subordinate part from a 'middle' or mediating part, holding that the animal intellect, because it mediates, is thereby more nearly equalized to the intellectual totality than a plain part would be (farmer1998:21274).
