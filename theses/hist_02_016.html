Latin

Christus in ultimo iudicio iudicabit non solum in natura humana, sed

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 11525

Farmer's inventory line breaks off mid-sentence at 'sed', but Edelheit quotes the complete printed thesis, citing 'Pico, 900 Theses, page 224', as 'Christus in ultimo iudicio iudicabit non solum in natura humana, sed etiam secundum naturam humanam' (edelheit2022:11023).

English

At the last judgment Christ will judge not only in human nature, but also according to human nature. unverified draft

Farmer's own note (paraphrased, not reproduced) glosses the thesis in the same terms: Christ in the Last Judgment will judge not only in human nature but also according to human nature (farmer1998:11589).

Doctrine

The thesis distinguishes Christ judging 'in' human nature (as possessing it) from judging 'according to' human nature (as its exercised standard), a distinction Edelheit locates in Thomas's commentary on the Sentences, book 4, distinction 48, question 1, article 1 (edelheit2022:11000). Edelheit remarks that, as it stands, the thesis 'does not make much sense without an explanation', while also judging it 'an accurate conclusion' relative to Thomas's discussion (edelheit2022:11000).

Context

Edelheit frames the thesis as part of a set that does not exhaust the theological nuance found in Thomas, adding that the project of the 900 Theses was not wholly a work of scholastic theology and that Pico was not a professional theologian (edelheit2022:11000).

Historiography

Edelheit is the only scholar found discussing this thesis in the sources consulted; he judges Pico's formulation compressed to the point of needing explanation, yet substantively accurate to Thomas's own discussion (edelheit2022:11000).
