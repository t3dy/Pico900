Latin

Ex precedenti conclusione habetur quae sit uirga Mercurii soporifera.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 22341

English

From the preceding conclusion it is established what the sleep-inducing wand of Mercury is. unverified draft

'habetur' is here rendered as the impersonal 'it is established' (habeo, 'to have, to hold'); the thesis is elliptical, presupposing the content of the preceding conclusion 5>9 (farmer1998:22335).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch15, from Latin at farmer1998:22341

Attribution: Pico's own opinion, in the doctrine of Plato (section 5>, farmer1998:22132). · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

The thesis is elliptical: it states only that the preceding conclusion yields the identity of Mercury's 'sleep-bringing wand,' without independently restating what that identity is (farmer1998:22341). Farmer groups it with 5>9, 5>11 and 5>12 as a set correlating the soul's several lives and operations with the seven planets, in which the sun and moon stand for the intellect and its lower reflection in reason, and in which, he states, no predictive or horoscopal astrology is at issue (farmer1998:22372).
