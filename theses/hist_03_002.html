The essence can be seen without the Persons, and one Person without another. unverified draft

Attribution: Reports Francis of Meyronnes, the second of the eight theses drawn from his commentary (farmer1998:11969, 11978). · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

The thesis holds, on Trinitarian logic, that the divine essence 'can be seen without the Persons, and one Person without another' (farmer1998:11978). Edelheit paraphrases it directly: 'According to Pico it is possible that essence could be seen without Persons, and that one Person could be seen without another Person' (edelheit2022:13130). Farmer's shared note on 3.1-3.2 glosses 'the essence' as the abstract divine nature considered independently of the Persons (farmer1998:12005).

Context

Edelheit places the question in Francis's commentary on the Sentences, and records that Francis 'goes on and demonstrates further different components of this proposition,' a discussion Edelheit judges to go beyond what the thesis itself states (edelheit2022:13130).

Historiography

Edelheit reads the thesis as continuing the Trinitarian logic of thesis 3.1, remarking that it 'brings us of course to the subtleties of Trinitarian theology' (edelheit2022:13130), and identifies Francis of Meyronnes, Scriptum super Primum Sententiarum, pp. s9v-t1v, as the passage Farmer had already indicated (edelheit2022:13155).
