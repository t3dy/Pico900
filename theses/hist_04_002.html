Thesis 4.2

Latin

Idea lapidis non est aliud quam lapis productus a diuino intellectu in esse intelligibili, quod est esse secundum quid, existens in mente diuina sicut cognitum in cognoscente.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 12144

English

The idea of a stone is nothing other than the stone produced by the divine intellect in intelligible being -- which is a qualified [secundum quid] being -- existing in the divine mind as the known [thing exists] in the knower. unverified draft

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch5, from Latin at farmer1998:12144

Attribution: Reports Duns Scotus, second of the theses drawn from his work, opposed to 3.5 from Francis of Meyronnes (farmer1998:12135, 12173). · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

The thesis states that the divine idea of a stone is identical with the stone as produced by the divine intellect in 'intelligible being,' a merely qualified (secundum quid) mode of being that exists in the divine mind as a known thing exists in a knower (farmer1998:12144). Edelheit paraphrases the same doctrine of divine intellection: the idea of a stone 'is nothing but the production of a stone by the divine intellect on an intelligible level of existence,' existing in the divine mind 'just as something known (cognitum) exists in the mind of someone who knows it' (edelheit2022:13758).

unverified draft

Context

Farmer records that Pico set this thesis in explicit opposition to 3.5 from Francis of Meyronnes, on the eternal formal being of quiddities, and cites Scotus's Ordinatio 1, distinction 35 as the passage at issue (farmer1998:12173).

unverified draft

Historiography

Edelheit treats the thesis as continuing thesis 1's theme into 'the process of divine intellection,' and quotes Scotus's Latin as transmitted through Pico's own thesis rather than offering an independent gloss beyond the paraphrase (edelheit2022:13787).
