Thesis 11>72

Work in progress. This entry is a draft awaiting verification: every claim carries a locator, but no second reader has yet re-found each one.

Latin

Sicut uera astrologia docet nos legere in libro dei, ita Cabala docet nos legere in libro legis.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 28032

English

Just as true astrology teaches us to read in the book of God, so the Cabala teaches us to read in the book of the Law. unverified draft

'libro legis' (the book of the Law) is the Torah; the thesis states a hermeneutic analogy between astrology's reading of the created world and Cabala's reading of the Law, glossed by Dougherty as meaning that astrology and natural magic contemplate the works of God in a way analogous to how Cabala interprets the text of the Law (dougherty2008:2961).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-swarm-batch2, from Latin at farmer1998:28032

Attribution: Pico's own opinion (farmer1998:28032). · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

The thesis states an analogy between astrology's reading of the created world as 'the book of God' and Cabala's reading of the Torah as 'the book of the Law'; Dougherty glosses the analogy as meaning that astrology and natural magic contemplate the works of God in a way analogous to how Cabala interprets the text of the Law (dougherty2008:2961).

unverified draft

Context

Dougherty describes the thesis as Pico's 'concluding thesis (11>72)' (dougherty2008:2961). Wirszubski gives an entire chapter of his study the thesis's own words as its title, 'Cabala Docet Nos Legere in Libro Legis,' explaining that it is 'with its principal function that Kabbala is said to teach us how to read the Law' (wirszubski1989:8638; wirszubski1989:8919).

unverified draft

Historiography

Beyond Wirszubski's and Dougherty's treatments above, Akopyan cites the thesis in both his 2018 and 2021 studies as a parallel to the immediately preceding thesis on the power of Hebrew names in magic, situating both within Pico's account of astrology (akopyan2018:637; akopyan2021:1357). Howlett cites the thesis in a footnote alongside her discussion of Wirszubski's and Craven's differing characterizations of Cabala as a science distinct from revealed theology, and alongside a reference to the Heptaplus (howlett2021:6088).
