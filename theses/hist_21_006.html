Thesis 21.6

Work in progress. This entry is a draft awaiting verification: every claim carries a locator, but no second reader has yet re-found each one.

Latin

Quia sicut dixit Abdala, videre somnia est fortitudo imaginationis, intelligere ea est fortitudo intellectus, ideo qui uidet ea ut plurimum non intelligit ea.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 15358

English

For, as Abdala said, seeing dreams is a matter of the imagination's strength, and understanding them a matter of the intellect's strength; and so whoever sees them mostly fails to understand them. unverified draft

'Abdala'/'Abdallah': Farmer's note states that the identity of this 'Abdallah' is uncertain (farmer1998:9277). 'Fortitudo' is rendered 'strength' rather than the later, narrower moral sense of 'fortitude' (farmer1998:9264).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch8, from Latin at farmer1998:9264

Attribution: Reports Adeland the Arab, citing 'Abdala' (farmer1998:9264). · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

The thesis holds, citing a saying attributed to 'Abdala', that seeing dreams is a matter of the strength of the imagination while understanding them is a matter of the strength of the intellect, so that whoever sees dreams mostly fails to understand them (farmer1998:9264). Farmer states that the identity of 'Abdala'/'Abdallah' is uncertain, and remarks, in a passage cut off at its start in the material consulted, that the famous opening line of the Oration, 'Nothing is more miraculous than man!', 'is similarly ascribed to an unidentified "Abdallah the Saracen"' (farmer1998:9277-9280).

unverified draft

Context

This uploaded copy of Busi and Ebgi discusses, in its introduction, the same 'Abdalla' figure as he appears at the opening of Pico's Oration ('Nothing is more miraculous than man'), independently of this thesis: it records that his identity has never been established and that the passage is so mysterious as to raise the possibility that Pico invented the figure, perhaps with Flavio Mitridate's help — 'Da piu di cinque secoli, il mistero di Abdalla rimane irrisolto' ('For more than five centuries, the mystery of Abdalla remains unsolved') (busi2014:782-783); whether this is the same discussion Busi and Ebgi give of the 'Abdala' cited in this thesis specifically (on dreams, rather than on man's dignity) is not established by the sources consulted (see not_established).

unverified draft

Historiography

Busi and Ebgi's introduction raises the possibility that the Oration's 'Abdalla' is Pico's own invention, and suggests 'lo zampino di Mitridate' ('Mitridate's hand') may be behind it, since Flavio Mitridate, Pico's tutor in Arabic and other languages, supplied Pico with bibliographic leads 'a proposito e a sproposito' ('whether relevant or not') (busi2014:786-788).

Not established

  • Farmer's note on the comparison between this thesis and the opening line of the Oration is cut off at its start in the material consulted (farmer1998:9277); what precedes 'is uncertain' -- almost certainly a statement that Abdala's/Abdallah's identity is unknown -- is not established.
  • Whether Busi and Ebgi's introduction, discussing the Oration's 'Abdalla' figure at busi2014:782-788, is describing the same 'Abdala' cited in this thesis (on dreams) or treats the two as distinct is not established by the sources consulted; Farmer's own cross-reference (farmer1998:9277-9280) suggests they may be the same invented or unidentified figure, but this is Farmer's inference, not Busi and Ebgi's.
  • The identity of 'Abdala'/'Abdallah' -- whether a real medieval Arabic author, an invention of Pico's, or (per Busi and Ebgi's speculation) supplied by Flavio Mitridate -- is not established by the sources consulted.
  • A prior draft attributed to Busi and Ebgi (busi2014:1241) the claim that they 'propose identifying "Abdala" with Abdallah ibn Salam, a Jew of Medina to whom tradition attributes Qur'an 46:10', and quoted them as saying (in Italian) 'Per quanto noto, nessuna fonte attribuisce pero a tale personaggio un detto simile a quello riportato qui da Pico' ('As far as is known, no source attributes to this figure any saying like the one Pico reports here'). On verification against this session's uploaded copy of Busi and Ebgi, neither the Italian sentence nor any discussion of 'Abdallah ibn Salam' or of Qur'an 46:10 appears anywhere in the file; the book's index does list an entry 'Abdallah ibn Salam, xxviii' alongside 'Abdalla arabo, xxviii-xxxi' and 'Corano: xxviii', suggesting the identification is made somewhere on those introduction pages, but the surrounding discussion actually present in this uploaded copy at that page range (busi2014:770-810) is about the Oration's Abdalla and does not use either the quoted Italian sentence or the ibn-Salam/Qur'an identification. Removed as unverifiable/likely fabricated; replaced with the genuinely-verified discussion of the same figure's uncertain identity, from both Busi/Ebgi and Farmer, that this uploaded corpus does contain.
