Latin

Si dictum illud Prophetae: Vendiderunt iustum argento, cabalistice exponamus, nihil aliud nobis significat quam hoc, scilicet, Deus ut redemptor uenditus fuit argento.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 26801

English

If we expound that saying of the Prophet, 'They sold the just man for silver,' in Kabbalistic fashion, it signifies nothing else to us than this: namely, that God, as redeemer, was sold for silver. unverified draft

Doctrine

The thesis subjects a prophetic verse, 'they sold the just man for silver', to a Kabbalistic exposition yielding a Christological reading: God as redeemer sold for silver (farmer1998:26801). Farmer's note identifies the verse as Amos 2:6, read together with Matthew's account of Judas's betrayal at Matthew 26:14-16, and explains that 'the just'/'redeemer' function as alternate terms for the ninth sefirah in Pico's Cabalistic symbolism, establishing a typological bond between the Old and New Testaments (farmer1998:26833).

Context

The thesis belongs to the Cabalistic own-opinion series (farmer1998:26227), and is directly followed by two further theses on the same typological cluster: thesis 11>20, on the letter combination that signifies 'then' and the mystery of the Trinity (farmer1998:26806), and thesis 11>21, linking the sefirah called 'the just and redeemer' to a Talmudic saying about Isaac carrying his cross (farmer1998:26810).
