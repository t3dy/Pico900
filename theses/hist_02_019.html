Doctrine

The thesis makes sin in the will depend on a prior defect in reason, denying that error could arise from the will independently of reason (edelheit2022:11198). Edelheit places it at the center of the intellect-will debate and the problem of evil, and notes that an opposing view -- that error in reason itself comes from a failure to control the passions -- is the alternative the thesis rules out (edelheit2022:11198).

Context

Farmer's note cross-refers the thesis to his note on 2.12 and to thesis 5>46 among Pico's own opinions, which restates the same Thomistic position in commenting on Plato (farmer1998:11578; farmer1998:22978).

Historiography

Edelheit is the only scholar found discussing this thesis in the sources consulted; he reads it as continuous with the intellect-will debate he treats at length elsewhere, and points to his own separate study of the 1474 controversy between Marsilio Ficino and Vincenzo Bandello as further background on that debate (edelheit2022:11225).
