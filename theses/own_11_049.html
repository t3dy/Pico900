Thesis 11>49

Latin

Qui sciuerit correspondentiam decem preceptorum ad prohibentia per coniunctionem ueritatis astrologicae cum ueritate theologica, uidebit ex fundamento nostro praecedentis conclusionis, quicquid alii dicant Cabalistae, primum preceptum primae numerationi correspondere, Secundum secundae, Tertium tertiae, Quartum septimae, Quintum quartae, Sextum quintae, Septi-

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 27430

Farmer's Latin, as given in data/inventory/theses.json, breaks off mid-word at 'Septi-'; this is an OCR defect in the copy-text, not a feature of Pico's text (farmer1998:27430). A differently spelled transcription of the same thesis, quoted in Akopyan's work ('correspondenciam', 'numeracioni' for Farmer's 'correspondentiam', 'numerationi'), continues further — 'quartum septime, quintum quarte, sextum quinte, septimum none, octauum octaue,' — before that quoted excerpt is itself cut off (akopyan2018:746; akopyan2021:1459). Neither excerpt gives the thesis's full text; the correspondences for the seventh through tenth commandments are not established here (akopyan2018:746; akopyan2021:1459). Farmer's own English translation of the whole thesis exists at farmer1998:27482 but is not reproduced, being in copyright.

English

Whoever will have known the correspondence of the ten commandments to the prohibitions, through the conjunction of astrological truth with theological truth, will see — from the foundation we set out in the preceding conclusion, whatever other Cabalists may say — that the first commandment corresponds to the first numeration, the second to the second, the third to the third, the fourth to the seventh, the fifth to the fourth, and the sixth to the fifth. [The source breaks off here, mid-word, in the copy-text consulted.] unverified draft

'numerationi' (numeration) renders the Hebrew sefirah, left untranslated by Pico's Latin source Mithridates for the same reason given for thesis 28.13 (black2006:11922, cited there). The translation stops where the Latin available in this packet stops; no further commandment-to-sefirah correspondences are given here (farmer1998:27430).

Doctrine

Farmer's note on this cluster of theses (11>48-50) glosses 'numerations' as the sefirot and 'edifice' as the fourth through tenth sefirot (farmer1998:27453). The thesis correlates each of the Ten Commandments with one of the ten sefirot by a stated conjunction of astrological and theological truth, following directly from thesis 11>48, which correlates the ten planetary spheres to the ten sefirot ('ego decem spheras sic decem numerationibus correspondere dico...', farmer1998:27423; quoted in full at akopyan2018:746).

Historiography

Akopyan (2018, 2021) quotes the thesis together with 11>48 in his studies of Pico's astrology, in each case reproducing the Latin of both theses in sequence without further discursive claim preserved in the material consulted (akopyan2018:746; akopyan2021:1459). Dougherty's anthology includes an English rendering of the thesis's opening alongside a paraphrase of the correspondence with 11>48 ('to the ten numerations [seﬁrot] like this: so that starting from the edifice, Jupiter corresponds to the fourth, Mars to the fifth...'), not reproduced further here as it is a copyrighted translation (dougherty2008:7894). Howlett quotes the phrase '…primum preceptum primae numerationi correspondere…' from Pico's text (1998, 540-1) in a discussion crediting Wirszubski with pointing to 'a strong relationship between Platonism and Kabbalism at the beginning of the Heptaplus' (howlett2021:6154). The 'Erkenntnistheorie und christliche Kabbalah' study cross-references 11>49 with thesis 5>3, on the illumination of higher things upon intermediate ones (kabbalah_paradigma:1643).
