Latin

Contingens rerum quae sunt, erunt, aut fuerunt, existentia ideo deo ab aeterno fuit infallibiliter nota, quia fuit aeternitati eius praesentialiter praeposita.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 11315

English

The contingent existence of things that are, will be, or have been was for that reason infallibly known to God from eternity, because it was set before his eternity as present. unverified draft

Attribution: Reports Thomas Aquinas (farmer1998:11315). · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

The thesis argues that God knows contingent things infallibly from eternity because their existence, though successive for us, stands present before his eternity (farmer1998:11315). Edelheit reports that thesis 3 states that the contingent existence of things which are, will be and were, since they were known to God's eternity in a present fashion, were infallibly and eternally known by him, and finds this in Thomas's Summa theologiae, 1, question 14, article 13 (edelheit2022:10290). Farmer groups this thesis with 2.2-9 under the divine omnipotence/free will problem (farmer1998:11348).

Historiography

Edelheit (2022) remarks that Pico's thesis is accurate to Thomas's formulation via the phrase 'a filio', but that its brevity as a single conclusion cannot reflect all of the theological nuance and implications found in Thomas (edelheit2022:10290).
