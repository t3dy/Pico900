Latin

Qui sciuerit quid sit denarius in Arithmetica formali, et cognouerit naturam primi numeri spherici, sciet illud quod ego adhuc apud aliquem Cabalistam non legi, et est quod sit fundamentum secreti magni Iobelei in Cabala.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 27921

Apparatus (Farmer): 1486 formabili. | Emendationes errorum, corrige: formali | 1487 text emended sic

The 1486 print reads 'formabili'; the errata list corrects it to 'formali', and the 1487 reprint carries the emendation (farmer1998:27945).

English

Whoever shall have known what the denarius is in formal arithmetic, and shall have recognized the nature of the first spherical number, will know something that I myself have not yet read in any Kabbalist, namely that it is the foundation of the great secret of the Jubilee in the Kabbalah. unverified draft

'Denarius' is the number ten, and Farmer cross-references 'formal arithmetic' to thesis 7>9, where Pico holds that non-material, formal arithmetic gives 'the best route to natural prophecy' (farmer1998:23724). Farmer's note identifies the 'first spherical number' as five, a spherical number being one whose powers always end in that same digit, and compares Theon of Smyrna's Mathematics Useful for Understanding Plato 1.24 (farmer1998:27954).

Doctrine

The thesis links three technical objects: the denarius, the 'first spherical number' of formal arithmetic, and the Kabbalistic 'great Jubilee.' Farmer's note glosses the 'first spherical number' as five and cites Theon of Smyrna's Mathematics Useful for Understanding Plato 1.24 for the class of spherical numbers, whose powers always end in the same digit as the number itself (farmer1998:27954). The same note identifies the 'great Jubilee' as a standard Kabbalistic symbol of the third sefirah, one of whose names was Teshuvah ('penitence' or 'return'), and states that the great Jubilee carried an eschatological significance that Pico intended this thesis to emphasize (farmer1998:27954).
