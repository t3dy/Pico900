Latin

Ideo haec est falsa: Essentia generat, quia essentia est ultimate abstracta, et generat formaliter praedicatur. u

English

Therefore this is false: 'The essence generates'; because the essence is utterly abstract, and 'generates' is predicated of it [only] formally. unverified draft

Edelheit's paraphrase gives the same sense: the proposition is false 'since essence is completely abstract, but it is formally predicated as ‘generating’' (edelheit2022:13078).

Doctrine

The thesis declares false the claim 'the essence generates,' on the ground that the essence is 'ultimately abstract' while 'generates' is predicated of it only formally (farmer1998:11974). Edelheit locates the source discussion in Francis of Meyronnes's commentary on the Sentences, Book 1, distinction 5, question 1, titled 'whether the divine essence generates' (edelheit2022:13078). Farmer's note glosses 'the essence' in this and the following thesis as the abstract divine nature considered apart from the three Persons, and records that Pico meant to set this essence alongside the Kabbalistic Ein-Sof and comparable concepts in other traditions (farmer1998:12005).

Context

Farmer's note ties 3.1-3.2 to his comments on 2.1 (Trinitarian procession, from Thomas) and 11>4 (the Kabbalistic Ein-Sof), which suggests Pico read Francis's logic of the divine essence alongside both the Thomist and Kabbalistic material (farmer1998:12005).

Historiography

Edelheit treats the thesis as the first of a run he attributes to Francis of Meyronnes's commentary on Sentences Book 1, and observes that Pico's opening word 'ideo' ('therefore') implies a continuation from a preceding discussion, 'possibly the previous set of theses, those dedicated to Thomas, and probably the last one, thesis 45' (edelheit2022:13099).
