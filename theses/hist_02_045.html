Latin

Non sunt ponendae ideae generum.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 11898

English

Ideas of genera are not to be posited. unverified draft

'ideae' is used in the technical Platonic sense taken up by scholastic authors, of separate exemplar forms; the thesis denies that genera (as distinct from species or individuals) require their own such Ideas (farmer1998:11898).

Attribution: Reports Thomas Aquinas (farmer1998:11898). · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

Edelheit states that 'Thesis 45 states that we should not assume ideas of genera' (edelheit2022:12987) and situates it in the context of Thomas's commentary on Aristotle's Metaphysics, Book 1, Lect. 14, where Plato 'multiplied the genera of things' in trying to account for sensible things alone (paraphrase, edelheit2022:12987). Edelheit quotes Thomas's Latin from that commentary, distinguishing ideas taken strictly (of things that can exist separately) from ideas taken broadly, as a likeness or notion, which can belong even to things that cannot exist separately, including prime matter (paraphrase, edelheit2022:13012).

Historiography

Edelheit asks why the thesis 'opens with the adverb ideo (therefore), suggesting a continuation from a previous discussion, possibly the previous set of theses, those dedicated to Thomas, and probably the last one, thesis 45,' and raises, without resolving, whether this is 'another case of concatenatio, this time between theses of two different philosophers, belonging to two competing schools' (edelheit2022:13103). He quotes the Latin of thesis 45 verbatim on two further occasions (edelheit2022:13012, 13103).
