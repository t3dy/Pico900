The claim classifies psychology (scientia de anima) as an intermediate science between natural philosophy and theology, rather than simply a branch of natural philosophy (farmer1998:14890).

Doctrine

The thesis classifies the science of the soul as intermediate between the natural sciences and the divine sciences (farmer1998:14890). Farmer's note glosses the reasoning as resting on the soul's mediating position between the intellectual and material worlds (farmer1998:14964). Edelheit reports that this contrasts with the position he attributes to the commentator he calls Hennon, discussing Book One of Aristotle's De anima, that 'the science of the soul belongs mainly to natural science' (edelheit2022:4080), and, via Bakker's edition, with the Parisian 'conclusio responsalis' that de anima is a natural science having the ensouled body as its first subject (edelheit2022:4101). He also notes a related account by Johannes le Damoisiau, in the same Parisian tradition, on the subject of Book One of De anima (edelheit2022:4101).

Historiography

Edelheit is the only scholar in the material opened to discuss this thesis; he places it in contrast with the mainstream Parisian scholastic view he documents via Bakker's edition, that the science of the soul is simply a part of natural science (edelheit2022:4080; edelheit2022:4101).
