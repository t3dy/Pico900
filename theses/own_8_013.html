Latin

Per puerum apud interpretes nihil aliud intellige quam intellectum.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 24960

English

By the boy, among the interpreters, understand nothing other than the intellect. unverified draft

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch16, from Latin at farmer1998:24960

Attribution: Pico's own opinion · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

Paired with 8>12, this conclusion assigns the intellect to the figure of the boy in the interpreters' reading of Zoroaster, continuing the scheme in which 8>10's dog stands for the irrational soul and 8>12's Siren for its rational part (farmer1998:25026). Farmer notes the more general pattern of designating the intellectual nature as 'firstborn,' 'child,' or 'boy' in this interpretive tradition (farmer1998:25026).

Historiography

Wirszubski quotes the conclusion and states his own uncertainty about the context in which puer was used by the interpreters of Zoroaster's sayings, though he judges the term likely connected to a further source he goes on to discuss (wirszubski1989:9845). A German-language study of Pico's Kabbalah cites the conclusion alongside Wirszubski's discussion and a conclusion drawing on Menahem Recanati (kabbalah_paradigma:1877).
