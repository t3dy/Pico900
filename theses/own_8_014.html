Thesis 8>14

Latin

Per dictum illud Zoroastris, adhuc tres dies sacrificabitis et non ultra, apparuit mihi per arithmeticam superioris merchiaue illos computandi dies, esse in eo dicto expresse praedictum aduentum Christi.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 25041

Wirszubski quotes the conclusion with the editio princeps reading 'computandos' where Farmer's text has 'computandi' (wirszubski1989:9858).

English

Through that saying of Zoroaster's, you shall sacrifice for three more days and no longer, it has appeared to me, by reckoning those days through the arithmetic of the higher chariot, that the coming of Christ is expressly foretold in that saying. unverified draft

'merchiaue' is read as merkabah, the 'chariot', the term Farmer glosses as the speculative part of the Cabala concerned with divine matters (farmer1998:25053).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch16, from Latin at farmer1998:25041

Attribution: Pico's own opinion · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

The conclusion applies a numerological method to a saying attributed to Zoroaster, finding in it, by way of the 'superior chariot' (merkabah), an arithmetical prediction of the coming of Christ (farmer1998:25041). Farmer reports a conjectural reading by Wirszubski: since 'days' can symbolize particular sefirot elsewhere in Pico's Cabalistic conclusions, counting from the first sefirah downward would put the third day at Binah, though Farmer's note breaks off before completing the identification (farmer1998:25053).
