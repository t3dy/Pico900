Magic · thesis 9>17

Thesis 9>17

Latin

Illius naturae quae est orizon temporis et aeternitatis propria est magia, inde est petenda per modos debitos, notos sapientibus.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 25358

Apparatus (Farmer): 1487 per modos debitos notus

The apparatus records a 1487 variant, 'per modos debitos notus', for the copy-text's 'notos' (farmer1998:25385).

English

To that nature which is the horizon of time and eternity, magic properly belongs; from it, magic is to be sought by the due methods, known to the wise. unverified draft

'orizon' is kept as in the neighboring theses 9>16 and 9>18, which place natures at the same kind of threshold but assign them, respectively, to the magus's proximity alone and to Cabala (farmer1998:25354; farmer1998:25362).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch17, from Latin at farmer1998:25358

Attribution: Pico's own opinion, section 9> (Magic) (farmer1998:25358). · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

This is the middle term of the 'horizon' triad begun at 9>16 and completed at 9>18; it assigns magic, as opposed to the Cabala assigned in 9>18, as the discipline proper to the nature standing at the horizon of time and eternity (farmer1998:25358).
