Doctrine

The thesis holds that a quality remains numerically one and the same throughout an alteration, from its beginning to its end (edelheit2022:11868). Edelheit locates the discussion in Thomas's commentary on Aristotle's Physics, book 7, lecture 8, and reports that, according to Caroti, the thesis reflects a view common among medieval commentators on Aristotle, citing Caroti's 'Note sulle fonti medievali di Pico della Mirandola', page 68 (edelheit2022:11892). Farmer's note explains that the thesis follows from Thomas's denial of 'natural minima' or limits to divisibility in qualities, from which he concluded that alteration occurs in a single movement without passing through a successive series of discrete states (farmer1998:11736).

Context

Farmer's note cross-refers the thesis to 1.5 and to Pico's own thesis 2>34 among the theses against the common view, and records that Pico's opposing views are suggested there, without further detail given in the excerpt consulted (farmer1998:11736).

Historiography

Edelheit is the only scholar of this thesis found in the sources consulted directly; he in turn reports Caroti's independent judgment that the thesis reflects a commonplace among Aristotle's medieval commentators rather than a distinctively Thomistic or Pichian claim (edelheit2022:11892).
