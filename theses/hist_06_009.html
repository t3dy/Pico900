Latin

Calor, si sit etiam separatus, ignem generare poterit.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 12783

Apparatus (Farmer): 1486 seperatus

The 1486 print reads 'seperatus' for 'separatus' (farmer1998:12795).

English

Heat, even if it were separated [from its subject], would be able to generate fire. unverified draft

'separatus' is glossed by Edelheit against the scholastic question of nature and supposit — whether a nature can exist or act apart from the individual substance ('supposit') that normally bears it (edelheit2022:16791; edelheit2022:16819).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-swarm-batch4, from Latin at farmer1998:12783

Attribution: Reports Giles of Rome (farmer1998:12783). · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

The thesis states that heat, even if separated from its ordinary subject, would still be able to generate fire (farmer1998:12783). Edelheit places it among four theses (8, 9, 10 and 11) drawn from Giles that treat natural-philosophical issues, several of them examples Giles himself uses within theological discussions (edelheit2022:15969). He identifies the conceptual background as the relation between nature and supposit that Giles discusses in his Reportatio on the Sentences, book 3, question 1, there disguised as the question of whether God could be incarnated in a nature separated from its supposit (edelheit2022:16791). He calls thesis 9 a case in which 'an example from natural philosophy discussed in a theological context by Giles becomes a thesis on natural philosophy by Pico' (edelheit2022:16980).

unverified draft

Context

Edelheit reads theses 7-10 as material Giles used within theological discussions, so that Pico's extraction of thesis 9 as a stand-alone natural-philosophical proposition changes its function (edelheit2022:16760; edelheit2022:16980). Noting that a thesis on taste is placed between two on hearing, he suggests this order may reflect 'the haste with which Pico was working on the theses' (edelheit2022:16760).

Historiography

Edelheit is the only scholar in the sources consulted to discuss this thesis; he treats it as evidence of how Pico converts a theological illustration in Giles into an independent natural-philosophical thesis (edelheit2022:16980).
