Latin

Qui intellexerit cur sit dictum quod Moyses abscondit faciem suam, et quod Ezechias uertit facies suas ad parietem, sciet quae esse debeat orantis habitudo et dispositio.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 18190

English

Whoever understands why it is said that Moses hid his face, and that Hezekiah turned his face to the wall, will know what the bearing and disposition of one who prays ought to be. unverified draft

The two scriptural allusions are Exodus 3:6 (Moses hiding his face) and 2 Kings 20:2-3 (Vulgate 4 Kings/Malachim 20:2-3, Hezekiah turning to the wall) (farmer1998:18220).

Doctrine

Farmer glosses the thesis by its two scriptural allusions, Exodus 3:6 and 2 Kings 20:2-3, and reads it as one of several theses suggesting that God at his highest lies beyond human understanding, implying the need, at the highest stages of the mystical ascent, to set aside ordinary forms of knowledge; he cross-references 26.5 and 11>35 among others (farmer1998:18220).

unverified draft

Context

Farmer cross-references 11>35, on nothing being understood to proceed from God considered in himself as infinite and one (farmer1998:27129), and 26.5, on the intelligible order lying above every intellectual hierarchy (farmer1998:17143) (farmer1998:18220).

unverified draft

Historiography

Wirszubski transcribes the thesis in full as his 'Conclusio XXXIV' (wirszubski1989:2636, 2639) and elsewhere states that it was in Recanati, and not by his own wits, that Pico found the combination of texts that brought out the proper attitude at prayer, adding that it was because he read the Zohar through Recanati's eyes that Pico understood it so well (wirszubski1989:3140).
