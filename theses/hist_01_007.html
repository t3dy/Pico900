Thesis 1.7

Latin

Sonus fertur secundum esse reale usque ad principium nerui auditiui.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 11044

English

Sound is carried, according to real being, as far as the beginning of the auditory nerve. unverified draft

'esse reale' is the mode of existence Farmer's note contrasts with light's greater 'intentionality' in the companion thesis 1.8 (farmer1998:11115).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-swarm-batch7, from Latin at farmer1998:11044

Attribution: Reports Albert the Great (farmer1998:11044). · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

Farmer's note (shared with 1.8) situates the pair in commentary on Aristotle's De anima 2.7-8 and the Albertist contrast of intentional and real being in sensation (farmer1998:11115). Edelheit finds that 'in thesis 7 we find that sound, according to its real existence, is brought to the beginning of the nerve responsible for hearing,' citing Pico's wording at 900 Theses, page 214, and Albert's Opera omnia commentary on De anima (edelheit2022:9488, 9515).

unverified draft

Context

Edelheit groups theses 7 and 9 as 'both theses dedicated to the sense of hearing,' concluding that Pico 'accurately reflects Albert's views, although he uses a slightly different terminology' (edelheit2022:9579).

unverified draft

Historiography

Edelheit (2022) traces the doctrine to Albert's Opera omnia commentary on De anima and to the discussion in De homine (edelheit2022:9515), within his broader treatment of theses 7-10 as a set on sense perception whose rationale within the 900 Theses he finds unclear (edelheit2022:10159).
