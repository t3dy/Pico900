Thesis 4>9

Latin

Licet ita mihi uideatur probabile, non est tamen pertinaciter asserendum quod anima Christi per alium modum nobis ignotum non potuerit in infernum descendere.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 21661

Apparatus (Farmer): 1486 ignotum potuerit | Emendationes errorum, corrige: non potuerit | 1487 text emended sic.

The 1486 print reads 'ignotum potuerit'; Pico's own Emendationes errorum corrects this to 'non potuerit', the reading the 1487 print and the text given here follow (farmer1998:21683).

English

Although this seems probable to me, one should not, however, insist obstinately that the soul of Christ could not, by some other way unknown to us, have descended into hell. unverified draft

The double negative ('non...non potuerit') leaves open a possibility rather than denying one; my rendering follows the corrected 1487 reading (farmer1998:21683).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch14, from Latin at farmer1998:21661

Attribution: Pico's own opinion, in the theological conclusions 'against the common mode' (farmer1998:21661); Dougherty calls the immediately preceding thesis, which this one qualifies, 'the first questionable thesis' (dougherty2008:2264). · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

Where the immediately preceding thesis, 4>8, had denied that Christ 'truly and as to real presence descended into hell as Thomas and the common way hold, but only as to effect' (farmer1998:21657), this thesis immediately qualifies that denial, holding only probably, and refusing to assert obstinately, that Christ's soul could not have descended by some other, unknown way (farmer1998:21661); Farmer reads it as evidence that Pico recognized the theological danger already present in 4>8 as he had originally proposed it, adding that the papal commission passed over this qualification when it examined the preceding thesis (farmer1998:21742).

Reception

Dougherty observes that this thesis, 'immediately following the first questionable thesis, was not disputed, even though it may put all rational theology into jeopardy' (dougherty2008:2264); it is not among the thirteen theses the 1487 commission censured, unlike the thesis on the descent it qualifies (farmer1998:21657).
