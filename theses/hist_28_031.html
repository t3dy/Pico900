Latin

Data est circumcisio ad liberationem a uirtutibus immundis quae in circuitu ambulant.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 18175

English

Circumcision was given for liberation from the unclean powers that walk about in a circle. unverified draft

'in circuitu ambulant' alludes to Psalm 12:9 (Vulgate 11:9), 'in circuitu impii ambulant' ('the impious walk in a circle'), the passage Wirszubski (1989: 44) quotes from Recanati in connection with circumcision (farmer1998:18207).

Doctrine

Farmer explains that the passage Wirszubski (1989: 44) quotes from Recanati associates the rite of circumcision with Psalm 12:9 (Vulgate 11:9), 'in circuitu impii ambulant' ('the impious walk in a circle'), and calls the thesis a fine example of Pico's Christian Cabalism and of the systematic links between widely separated theses in his text, directing the reader to read it together with 11>22 (farmer1998:18207, note incomplete in the material consulted).

Context

Farmer's cross-reference network links the thesis to 11>22, on the Cabalists' teaching concerning the redness of Esau (farmer1998:26817); 11>27, on what is evidently elicited from Cabalistic principles concerning the Messiah's coming (farmer1998:26926); 28.47, on the order of the sefirot's influx expressed through the word AMEN (farmer1998:18410); 2>80, on Hebrew as the first, non-arbitrary language (farmer1998:20213); 3>55, on mastering the order of the Hebrew language as a norm for finding any knowable thing (farmer1998:21095); and 9>22, on Hebrew names alone having efficacy in magical operation (farmer1998:25378).

Historiography

Wirszubski transcribes the thesis in full as his 'Conclusio XXXI' and gives Recanati, fol 47va, as its source (wirszubski1989:2543).
