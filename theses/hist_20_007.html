Latin

Foelicitas hominis ultima est cum particularis intellectus noster totali primoque intellectui plene coniungitur.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 15105; 1486 print folio <7v/8r>

English

Man's final happiness is when our particular intellect is fully conjoined to the total and first intellect. unverified draft

'totali primoque intellectui' (the total and first intellect) reflects the Plotinian universal Intellect (Nous); the thesis casts humanity's final happiness as full union of one's own particular intellect with that universal intellect, phrased in scholastic Latin that Farmer notes is syncretically imposed on Plotinus by Pico (farmer1998:15135).

Doctrine

The thesis holds that man's final happiness consists in the full conjunction of one's own particular intellect with the total and first intellect (farmer1998:15105). Farmer's note states that this uses Latin scholastic terminology syncretically imposed on Plotinus by Pico (farmer1998:15135). The wording closely parallels thesis 7.3, attributed to Averroes, which holds that man's final happiness is when the agent intellect is conjoined to the possible intellect as its form (farmer1998:12940), suggesting a deliberate structural pairing of the Averroist and Plotinian accounts of intellectual beatitude.

Context

Black quotes the thesis's Latin using Pico's own cross-reference formula, page 66 of the Schefer edition, thesis 7 according to Plotinus, in the same footnote sequence as thesis 7.3 and a third thesis on attaining God by act of intellect, cited there as thesis 11 in the theology series according to Pico's own opinion (black2006:10027).
