Latin

Sicut hymni Dauid operi Cabalae mirabiliter deseruiunt, ita hymni Orphei operi uerae, licitae, et naturalis magiae.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 25671

Wirszubski's 1989 quotation of this conclusio prints 'Dauid open Cabalae' rather than 'Dauid operi Cabalae' (wirszubski1989:9973), evidently an OCR or typesetting artifact of Wirszubski's own printed page rather than a substantive variant.

English

Just as the hymns of David wonderfully serve the work of Cabala, so the hymns of Orpheus [serve] the work of true, licit, and natural magic. unverified draft

'hymni Dauid' are the Psalms (farmer1998:25697); the verb 'deseruiunt' is elided in the second clause and understood from the first, a standard Latin economy; 'uerae, licitae, et naturalis magiae' distinguishes the magic endorsed here from the illicit, demonic magic Pico elsewhere rejects (cf. 9>1, in the research packet for 10>2).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-swarm-batch7, from Latin at farmer1998:25671

Attribution: Pico's own opinion (farmer1998:25671). · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

Pico draws an explicit parallel between the Psalms ('hymns of David') as an instrument of Cabalistic work and the Orphic Hymns as an instrument of 'true, licit, and natural' magic (farmer1998:25671). Farmer's note glosses 'hymns of David' as the Psalms and observes that discussion of magic in the Psalms conventionally turned on the episode (1 Samuel 16:14-23) of David curing Saul's affliction through music (farmer1998:25697).

Context

Farmer's cross-reference network links the thesis to two further Orphic-Hymn theses on the hymns' numerology (10>5, on the number of Orphic hymns matching the number by which 'the triple God created the world' under a Pythagorean quaternary, farmer1998:25675; 10>25, on the quaternary of hymns designating 'the formable nature' of the first form's attributes, farmer1998:26114) and to two Mathematics-section theses comparing how medicine and music each move the spirits, one to govern the body and one to serve the soul (7>7, 7>8, farmer1998:23717, 23721).

Historiography

Wirszubski (1989) numbers this conclusio 'Conclusio iv' and groups it among 'three theses, namely' a set he goes on to enumerate (wirszubski1989:9973).
