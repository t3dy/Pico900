Latin

Per extremorum et medii rationem cognoscere possumus conuenienter uniuersi gradus sic in quinque posse diuidi: In super ens, uere ens, non uere ens, non uere non ens, uere non ens.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 22437

Apparatus (Farmer): 1486 diuidi.In super ens.uere ens.non uere ens. etc.

The 1486 print abbreviates the list after the first two terms: 'diuidi.In super ens.uere ens.non uere ens. etc.' (farmer1998:22465).

English

By the reasoning of the extremes and the middle we can fittingly recognize that the grades of the universe can be divided in this way into five: into that which is above being, that which truly is, that which is not truly being, that which is not truly not-being, and that which truly is not. unverified draft

The five coinages render Pico's 'super ens' / 'uere ens' / 'non uere ens' / 'non uere non ens' / 'uere non ens'; Howlett cites the Latin of the thesis in a sequence of notes on Pico's De ente et uno (howlett2021:7627).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch15, from Latin at farmer1998:22437

Attribution: Pico's own opinion, in the doctrine of Plato (section 5>, farmer1998:22132). · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

The thesis proposes that 'the reasoning of the extremes and the middle' allows the grades of the universe to be divided into five: that which is above being, that which truly is, that which is not truly being, that which is not truly not-being, and that which truly is not (farmer1998:22437). Farmer glosses the method of the extremes and the middle by reference to theses 3>52 and 3>70 and his note to 3>53-54, and states that the five grades run from God, 'that-above-being,' to prime matter, 'truly-not-being' (farmer1998:22475).

Historiography

Howlett quotes the Latin of the thesis in a sequence of notes connecting Pico's Conclusiones to his treatise On Being and the One, situating the five-grade scheme within that treatise's metaphysics (howlett2021:7627).
