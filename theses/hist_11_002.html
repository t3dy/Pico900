Latin

Intellectus agens nihil aliud est quam deus.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 14058

English

The agent intellect is nothing other than God. unverified draft

'Intellectus agens' is the agent (or active) intellect of Aristotelian-Averroist psychology; the thesis simply identifies it with God (farmer1998:14058).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch8, from Latin at farmer1998:14058

Attribution: Reports Abumaron (farmer1998:14058). · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

The thesis identifies the agent intellect outright with God (farmer1998:14058). Farmer's note states that it is opposed to thesis 10.1, attributed to Isaac of Narbonne, which holds that positing an agent intellect is not necessary (farmer1998:13969), and that the same underlying issue, of whether God himself or the intellectual nature is the source of the soul's illumination, recurs in Heptaplus 4.2, though the note is cut off in the material consulted before it can be followed further (farmer1998:14111). Cassirer treats the thesis as evidence that Pico 'completed the equating of God and the "intellectus agens"', quoting its Latin, and connects it to Pico's notion of intellectual vision, the 'visio intellectualis', which for Cassirer carries for Pico 'an independent theoretical meaning and content' rather than coinciding with mere mystical feeling (cassirer1942:874).

Historiography

Cassirer is the only scholar in the material opened to discuss the thesis' content and significance (cassirer1942:874); Black's footnote merely juxtaposes its Latin with that of thesis 7.4 without further comment (black2006:10046).
