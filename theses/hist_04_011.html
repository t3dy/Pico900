Gratia est subiectiue in uoluntate, non in essentia animae.

English

Grace is present, as in its subject, in the will, not in the essence of the soul. unverified draft

'Subiectiue': as in a subject, the scholastic technical sense (that in which an accident inheres), not the modern sense of 'subjectively' (farmer1998:12252).

Doctrine

The thesis addresses the ontological seat of grace: whether the supernatural quality of grace inheres in the will or in the essence of the soul itself (farmer1998:12252). Farmer locates the Scotist discussion in the Ordinatio, book 2, distinction 26, question unica, and notes that Thomas holds 'the opposite view' in his commentary on the Sentences, without specifying the passage (farmer1998:12337). Edelheit confirms that according to thesis 11 grace is located in the will and not the soul's essence, and traces the claim to Scotus's Lectura, book 2, distinction 26, question unica, where Scotus determines the relations of the soul to grace and glory while refuting an opinion that the form constituting the soul in supernatural being resides in the soul's essence (edelheit2022:14427, 14455).

Historiography

Edelheit is the only scholar identified as treating thesis 11 in the material consulted; his contribution is the identification of the Scotist source rather than an assessment of Pico's own position (edelheit2022:14427).
