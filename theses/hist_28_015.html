Thesis 28.15

Latin

Nisi nomen Abraam < DINNIAN>, id est he addita, fuisset, Abraam non generasset.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 17847

The bracketed '<DINNIAN>' in Farmer's transcription stands for Hebrew letters that did not survive transliteration; Wirszubski's own quotation of the same conclusio likewise prints the Hebrew as a garbled bracketed string, '<0Π13Κ>' (wirszubski1989:1919), confirming the corruption is in the transmitted text or its reproduction rather than an error introduced by Farmer alone.

English

Had the name Abraham not been [the form given in Hebrew] — that is, with a he added — Abraham would not have procreated. unverified draft

'generasset' rendered 'procreated' here.

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-swarm-batch7, from Latin at farmer1998:17847

Attribution: Reports Hebrew Cabalists (farmer1998:17847). · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

The thesis turns on the addition of the letter he (ה) to Abram's name (Genesis 17:5), making it 'Abraham', as the condition of his capacity to procreate — a piece of Hebrew name-mysticism for which Wirszubski identifies a close textual parallel in Menahem Recanati's commentary, itself quoting the Bahir (wirszubski1989:1919).

Historiography

Wirszubski (1989) transcribes the Latin of this conclusio (his 'Conclusio XV' among the 'Conclusiones Cabalisticae secundum opinionem sapientum Hebraeorum Cabalistarum') and directs comparison to Recanati's commentary, folio 46rb, which quotes the Bahir §6 in Scholem's edition (wirszubski1989:1919). Wirszubski's book elsewhere numbers other, textually unrelated conclusions 'Conclusio xv' in different series (wirszubski1989:9894, 9913, 10001); those excerpts do not share this thesis's Latin and are not established as pertaining to 28.15.
