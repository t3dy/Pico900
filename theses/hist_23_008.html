Latin

Qui finalem causam diluuiorum incendiorumque cognouerit, haec potius «καθάρσεις», id est purgationes, quam corruptiones uocabit.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 15757

Wirszubski, citing Bohdan Kieszkowski's edition of the Conclusiones (Geneva, 1973), records the manuscript reading as 'licet potius' with the correction 'hec' [haec] added in the margin, differing from Farmer's printed 'haec potius' (wirszubski1989:10689).

English

Whoever has recognized the final cause of floods and conflagrations will call them rather 'katharseis' — that is, purgations — than corruptions. unverified draft

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch9, from Latin at farmer1998:15757

Attribution: Reports Iamblichus (farmer1998:15757). · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

Farmer identifies the thesis as quoting Proclus's account of the destruction of Atlantis in his Timaeus commentary, noting that in Diehl's edition the words are spoken not by Iamblichus, whose own reading of the Atlantis story appears earlier in Proclus, but by the Egyptian priest of Plato's Timaeus (farmer1998:15822).
