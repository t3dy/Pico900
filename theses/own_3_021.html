Latin

Per praedictas conclusiones intelligi potest quae sit omiomeria Anaxagorae, quam opifex intellectus distinguit.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 20551

Farmer's cumulative number for this thesis, in his continuous count across the historical and paradoxical sections, is 520 (farmer1998:20601).

English

From the conclusions stated above, one can understand what Anaxagoras's homoeomeria is, which the maker-intellect distinguishes. unverified draft

'omiomeria' transliterates the Greek homoiomereia, Anaxagoras's doctrine of 'like-parted' elemental stuffs (farmer1998:20551). 'opifex intellectus' ('maker-' or 'craftsman-intellect') renders Anaxagoras's cosmic Nous as the ordering, distinguishing principle (farmer1998:20551; steiris2014:108). Steiris's own rendering, 'demiurge of the intellect' (steiris2014:108), is a related but distinct interpretive choice and is not reproduced here.

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-swarm-batch4, from Latin at farmer1998:20551

Attribution: Pico's own opinion (farmer1998:20551). · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

The thesis asserts that the preceding conclusions of the same paradoxical section allow one to understand Anaxagoras's doctrine of homoeomeria, the 'like-parted' elemental mixtures, and that it is the 'maker-intellect' that distinguishes them (farmer1998:20551). Steiris quotes the thesis as direct evidence that Pico comments on Anaxagoras within the Conclusiones (steiris2014:108). He identifies a source for it in Simplicius's commentary on Aristotle's Physics, calling the similarity between the two passages 'obvious' (steiris2014:340).
