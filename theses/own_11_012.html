Thesis 11>12

Latin

Non potest operari per puram Cabalam, qui non est rationaliter intellectualis.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 26604

English

One who is not rationally intellectual cannot operate by means of pure Cabala. unverified draft

'rationaliter intellectualis' echoes the graded psychological vocabulary of thesis 9>14, which grades a nature's magical power by whether it is 'rational' or 'rationally rational' (farmer1998:25283).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch18, from Latin at farmer1998:26604

Attribution: Pico's own opinion. · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

The thesis restricts the practice of 'pure Cabala' to those who are 'rationally intellectual', matching the graded psychology of thesis 9>26, which distinguishes pure, immediate Cabala from magic operating through the mediation of causes (farmer1998:25480), and thesis 9>14, which grades a nature's magical power by whether it is rational or 'rationally rational' (farmer1998:25283). Farmer's note states that 'pure Cabala' is discussed further at his note on 9>26, and that it operates in the intellectual part of the soul while natural magic operates in the rational part, per thesis 9>14 (farmer1998:26679).

unverified draft

Context

The thesis belongs to the Cabalistic own-opinion series (farmer1998:26227), and is followed immediately by thesis 11>13, which describes the mortal dangers of operating in the Cabala without purification (farmer1998:26608).

unverified draft

Reception

The study of Pico's Kabbalistic paradigm quotes the thesis's Latin text, pairing it in a note with the 'famous and contested' magical conclusion 9>9 on magic and Cabala certifying Christ's divinity, and with conclusion 11>63 on Aristotle's veiled philosophy, citing Wirszubski's Pico's Encounter at pages 164f as background (kabbalah_paradigma:2175). Wirszubski himself quotes the thesis — calling it 'the twelfth thesis' — in asking what kind of Kabbalistic operation produces the intellectual rapture that begins the soul's union with God (wirszubski1989:8153).

unverified draft

Historiography

Two works discuss the thesis in the sources consulted: the Kabbalistic-paradigm study treats it as one of a cluster of conclusions, with 9>9 and 11>63, illustrating Pico's linkage of magic, Cabala, and philosophy (kabbalah_paradigma:2175), while Wirszubski reads it specifically as bearing on the nature of the Death of the Kiss and the contemplative ascent to union with God (wirszubski1989:8153); no disagreement between the two readings is recorded in the material examined (kabbalah_paradigma:2175; wirszubski1989:8153).
