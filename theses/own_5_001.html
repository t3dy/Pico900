Latin

Per numeros triplares, qui a Platone in Timeo ponuntur in triangulo animam significante, admonemur quousque in formis numerandis sit progrediendum per naturam illius quod est prima forma formans. Per numeros uero duplares ibidem positos, admonemur quatenus, positis duobus extremis terminis, coordinanda sunt media per naturam eius, quod est medium in uniuerso.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 22141

English

Through the triple numbers that Plato, in the Timaeus, places in the triangle signifying the soul, we are advised how far one must proceed in numbering forms, according to the nature of that which is the first forming form. Through the double numbers likewise placed there, we are advised, once the two extreme terms are set, how the middle terms are to be coordinated, according to the nature of that which is the mean in the universe. unverified draft

Farmer's note identifies the 'first forming form' with the world soul and the 'two extreme terms' with intellectual nature and prime matter, situating the thesis in commentary on Timaeus 35c-36a and what late-ancient and medieval commentators called 'Crantor's diagram', the so-called Platonic lambda (farmer1998:22188).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch14, from Latin at farmer1998:22141

Attribution: Pico's own opinion, in the conclusions 'according to the doctrine of Plato' (farmer1998:22141). · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

The thesis treats the triple and double numbers of the Timaeus's world-soul triangle as instructions for two distinct operations: numbering forms downward from the first forming form, and coordinating middle terms between two extremes according to the universe's own mean (farmer1998:22141); Farmer's cross-reference apparatus links it to an earlier thesis on 'the wonderful saying of that barbarian, companion of nymphs and demons,' concerning a triangular figure of 184 worlds with three unities at the corners and one in the middle (3>39, farmer1998:20779).
