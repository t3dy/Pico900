Doctrine

The thesis states that even if every individual member of the human species were destroyed, it would remain true that man is an animal (farmer1998:10929). Farmer's note reads this as a standard scholastic formula affirming the independence of species and genera from their individual members, traced to Aristotle's attack on the Platonic theory of ideas in Metaphysics 7.14-15 (farmer1998:11003). The same note reports that Pico held species and genera could legitimately be viewed as independently existing from the limited standpoint of human knowledge, though from a higher perspective they are mere beings of reason (farmer1998:11003).

Historiography

Edelheit's chapter on Pico and Albert introduces the thesis under the heading 'Thesis 2', framing it as a question about the relation between particulars and universals (edelheit2022:9058). Edelheit's accompanying note reproduces the Latin from the printed edition of the 900 Theses (edelheit2022:9086).
