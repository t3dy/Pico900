Quiddities have their formal being from eternity of themselves, not from something extrinsic. unverified draft

Attribution: Reports Francis of Meyronnes, on quiddities as ideas in the divine mind (farmer1998:11969, 12056). · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

The thesis holds that 'quiddities' -- for Francis, the ideas in the mind of God -- 'formally hold their existence by themselves and from eternity, not by anything external' (edelheit2022:13351), and so do not 'require an external cause' (edelheit2022:13379). Farmer's note glosses 'quiddities' here as the divine ideas, distinct from the material-essence sense used elsewhere in the 900, such as thesis 2.42, and from the sense applied to ideas reflected in the separate intelligences, such as thesis 7.36; he adds that Pico meant to rank these senses hierarchically in his planned Roman debate (farmer1998:12056).

Context

Farmer records that the thesis is 'opposed to' 4.2 from Scotus and 'strongly linked' to 5.9 from Henry of Ghent, and that 11>58 may bear further on Pico's own view of the same issue (farmer1998:12056).

Historiography

Edelheit identifies the source discussion as Francis of Meyronnes's commentary on Sentences 1, distinction 42, question 2, citing Scriptum super Primum Sententiarum, pages x9v-y1r, and notes that this identification 'was indicated by Farmer' (edelheit2022:13379).
