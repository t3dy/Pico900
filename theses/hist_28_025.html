Latin

Idem est bresith, id est in principio creauit, ac si dixisset in sapientia creauit.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 18066

English

'Bresit' -- that is, 'he created in the beginning' -- amounts to the same thing as if he had said, 'he created by means of wisdom.' unverified draft

'bresith' (Bereshit, Gen. 1:1) is here glossed, per Farmer, through its association with Hokhmah, the second sefirah, conventionally rendered 'Wisdom' (farmer1998:18096).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch11, from Latin at farmer1998:18066

Attribution: Reports Hebrew Cabalists (farmer1998:18066). · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

Farmer glosses 'bereshit'/'wisdom' as common symbols for Hokhmah, the second sefirah, and notes that in Pico's second set of Cabalistic conclusions this sefirah is frequently associated with Christ, the 'Wisdom of the Father' of the New Testament and the Second Person of the Trinity in scholastic tradition; he cross-references 28.9 (farmer1998:18096).

unverified draft

Historiography

Howlett quotes the thesis in full in Latin, citing Farmer's edition (howlett2021:7698). Wirszubski transcribes the thesis, together with 28.26, as his 'Conclusiones XXV and XXVI' (wirszubski1989:2365, 2368), and elsewhere calls it 'an obvious sequel' to itself and the following conclusio, arguing that it presupposes the equation of 'Beginning' with 'Wisdom,' which links the symbolism of the ten sefirot with Pico's Kabbalistic conception of the first Platonic or Philonic ideas rather than the thirty-two paths of wisdom of the Sefer Yetzirah (wirszubski1989:9123).
