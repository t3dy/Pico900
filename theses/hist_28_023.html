Latin

Supra proprietatem penitentiae non est utendum uerbo dixit.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 17966

English

Above the property of penitence, the word 'he said' is not to be used. unverified draft

'proprietatem penitentiae' ('property of penitence') is, per Farmer, an alternate name for the third sefirah, elsewhere called Teshuvah (penitence) or Binah (intelligence) (farmer1998:18043); 'dixit' ('he said') alludes to the recurring formula of divine speech in the Genesis creation account.

Attribution: Reports Hebrew Cabalists (farmer1998:17966). · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

Farmer glosses 'property of penitence' as an alternate name of the third sefirah, elsewhere named Teshuvah (penitence) or Binah (intelligence), and reads the thesis as alluding to the fact that, in medieval tradition, different sefirot were symbolically associated with different stages of creation in Genesis 1, and that the words 'God said' first appear in that account only with the creation of light -- itself another symbol, for Pico, of 'intelligence.' Farmer adds that Pico would presumably have tied the thesis to his belief that language cannot adequately describe the highest levels of reality, and cross-references 11>31 (farmer1998:18043).

Context

Farmer's cross-reference network links the thesis to three Cabalistic conclusions confirming the Christian religion -- 11>11, on the mode by which rational souls are sacrificed to God by the archangel (farmer1998:26598); 11>31, on formlessness understood as antecedent to, rather than a privation of, formality (farmer1998:27021); and 11>45, on why the Son of God came with the water of baptism and the Holy Spirit with fire (farmer1998:27335) -- and to 28.1, on the priest Michael's sacrifice of rational souls (farmer1998:17532) (farmer1998:18043).

Historiography

Wirszubski transcribes the thesis as his 'Conclusio XXIII' (wirszubski1989:2291) and gives Recanati, fol. 6rb-va, as a parallel (wirszubski1989:2294).
