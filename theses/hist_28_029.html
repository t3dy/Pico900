Latin

Nomen dei quatuor litterarum, quod est ex mem, sade, pe, et sa/de, regno Dauidis debet appropriari.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 18082; 1486 print folio <13v/14r>

English

The four-letter name of God, made up of mem, tsade, pe, and tsade, ought to be appropriated to the kingdom of David. unverified draft

This 'name of four letters' is not the Tetragrammaton YHVH itself but a different combination (mem-tsade-pe-tsade), generated, per Farmer's note, by replacing each letter of YHVH with its counterpart under a reversed Hebrew alphabet — Pico's so-called revolutio alphabetariae (paraphrase; farmer1998:18155). 'regno Dauidis' ('kingdom of David') is identified by Farmer as the tenth sefirah (paraphrase; farmer1998:18155).

Doctrine

Thesis 28.29 assigns the four-letter divine name formed from mem, tsade, pe, and tsade to 'the kingdom of David' (farmer1998:18082). Farmer's note identifies 'kingdom of David' with 'the tenth sefirah', and explains the four-letter name as generated by substituting each letter of the ineffable name YHVH (normally the sixth sefirah) with its counterpart under a reversed Hebrew alphabet, citing 'Anagnine (1937: 160), Wirszubski (1989: 43-44)' (paraphrase; farmer1998:18155).

unverified draft

Context

Farmer's cross-reference apparatus ties the thesis to the immediately preceding 28.28, on the 'volatile' creature of the fifth day understood as worldly angels visible to men (farmer1998:18078).

unverified draft

Reception

Wirszubski quotes the thesis in full as his 'Conclusio XXIX' (wirszubski1989:2460, 2463, 7256) and situates it within a source-critical argument about the Bahir and Recanati.

unverified draft

Historiography

Wirszubski uses the thesis as a test case for distinguishing Pico's direct and indirect sources: "Conclusio xxix, which was inspired by the Bahir, is a case in point" (wirszubski1989:3177). "It treats of the proper application of the name MSPS and is located between two theses both of which treat of angels" (wirszubski1989:3177). He generalizes: "It is certain that Pico read the Bahir: Scholem proved it beyond doubt" (wirszubski1989:3284), while noting that of the theses reflecting the Bahir, 'only one (Conclusio xxix) derives in all likelihood from the Bahir itself', the others being possibly mediated through Recanati's commentary (paraphrase; wirszubski1989:3284).
