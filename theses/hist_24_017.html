Latin

Licet ut tradit theologia distinctae sint diuinae hierarchiae, intelligen-

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 16166

Farmer's own transcription verbatim breaks off mid-word ('intelligen-') at what appears to be an OCR line-join in the inventory extraction (farmer1998:16166). The continuation is not taken from farmer1998 but is independently and identically attested, as the same thesis ('Conclusiones secundum Proclum', no. 17), by Black, '...intelligendum est tamen omnia in omnibus esse modo suo' (black2006:8798), and by Wirszubski, '...intelligendum est tarnen omnia in omnibus esse modo suo' (wirszubski1989:12669), 'tarnen' apparently an OCR misreading of 'tamen'; a VERIFIER should confirm the complete sentence against farmer1998 directly (farmer1998:16166).

English

Although, as theology teaches, the divine hierarchies are distinct, it must nevertheless be understood that all things are in all things, each in its own way. unverified draft

Translates the complete sentence as attested by black2006:8798 and wirszubski1989:12669, since Farmer's own transcription at farmer1998:16166 breaks off before 'intelligendum'.

Doctrine

Farmer cites Proclus's Platonic Theology book 5, page 275, as the thesis's source and points to further discussion of its syncretic background earlier in his own book, without specifying a page in the note itself (farmer1998:16201). Black situates the underlying formula against Syrianus's Commentary on Aristotle's Metaphysics, in Kroll's 1892 edition, pages 81-82, quoting a related Procline dictum on things being present reciprocally within one another, as part of the same tradition (black2006:8798).

Context

Farmer's cross-reference network links the thesis to 22.1-3 and 23.2 on the same theme of the world's craftsman and intellectual hierarchy (farmer1998:16166).

Historiography

Wirszubski (1989) quotes the thesis while identifying it as one of 'two motifs' recurring together elsewhere in Pico's corpus, without the consulted excerpt specifying what the two motifs are (wirszubski1989:12669). Black (2006) quotes the thesis in a discussion tracing the 'omnia in omnibus' formula to the Proclus-Syrianus tradition of commentary on Aristotle's Metaphysics (black2006:8798).
