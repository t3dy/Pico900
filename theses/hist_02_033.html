Materia nullum dicit actum entitatiuum positiuum.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 11767

English

Matter signifies no positive entitative act. unverified draft

'actus entitativus' recurs as Pico's own term for an act constitutive of a thing's positive being (cf. thesis 2.34); matter, on this thesis, contributes none (farmer1998:11767).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch4, from Latin at farmer1998:11767

Attribution: Reports Thomas Aquinas (farmer1998:11767). · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

The thesis denies that matter, considered in itself, constitutes any positive actuality; Farmer notes the underlying question was whether the matter of created beings belongs to their formal essence or definition, on which Pico here follows the negative side, cross-referenced to theses 7.21, 8.6, 18.3 and 2>52 (farmer1998:11807). Edelheit reports that Thomas, commenting on the Sentences (2, d.18, q.1, a.2), discusses how God operates on matter through seminal reasons and concludes that matter is purely [potential] (edelheit2022:12267).

Historiography

Edelheit (2022) connects thesis 33 to Thomas's Sentences commentary on divine action on matter through seminal reasons (edelheit2022:12267); Farmer (1998) situates it within a cluster of cross-referenced theses on whether matter enters into the definition of natural substances (farmer1998:11807).
