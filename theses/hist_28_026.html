Latin

Quod dixit Anchelos chaldeus becadmin, id est cum aeternis uel per

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 18069

Apparatus (Farmer): 1486, 1487 becadmim | 1487 cum eternis vel paterna

Farmer's transcription as extracted for this inventory (farmer1998:18069) breaks off after 'per', apparently at the copy-text's line end; Wirszubski's own transcription of the same conclusio gives the sentence complete: 'Quod dixit anchelos chaldeus becadmin, id est cum aeternis uel peraeterna, triginta duas uias sapientiae intellexit' (wirszubski1989:2370), and the apparatus records the 1486/1487 variant 'becadmim' and the 1487 variant 'cum eternis vel paterna' (farmer1998:18090).

English

What Onkelos the Chaldean said, 'becadmin' -- that is, 'with' or 'through the eternal things' -- he understood [as referring to] the thirty-two paths of wisdom. unverified draft

Translated from the complete sentence as transmitted by Wirszubski's transcription (wirszubski1989:2370), since the locator's own text as extracted stops short of the verb; 'Anchelos' is Onkelos, traditional author of the Aramaic Targum Onkelos, mentioned often by Maimonides and other medieval sources known to Pico (farmer1998:18101); the 'thirty-two paths of wisdom' come from the proto-Kabbalistic Sefer Yetzirah and later texts, on the world's creation through the twenty-two Hebrew letters and the ten sefirot (farmer1998:18101).

Doctrine

Farmer identifies 'Anchelos' as Onkelos the Proselyte, traditional author of the Aramaic Targum Onkelos, frequently mentioned by Maimonides and other medieval sources available to Pico, and traces the 'thirty-two paths of wisdom' to the proto-Kabbalistic Sefer Yetzirah (Book of Creation) and later texts holding that God created the world by the combined power of the twenty-two Hebrew letters and the ten sefirot; he cross-references Pico's magical conclusions at 9>19 and following, and at 11>58 (farmer1998:18101).

Context

Farmer cross-references 9>19, which holds that voices and words have efficacy in magical operation because the voice is the first thing through which nature exercises magic (farmer1998:25366) (farmer1998:18101).
