Latin

Sicut formae substantiales in secundo mundo sunt per modum accidentium, ita formae accidentales sunt in primo mundo per modum substantiarum.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 20561

English

Just as substantial forms exist in the second world in the manner of accidents, so accidental forms exist in the first world in the manner of substances. unverified draft

"primus mundus"/"secundus mundus" (first world/second world) are Pico's own terms, glossed by Farmer as the intellectual nature and the rational or animate nature (or the material world informed by it), respectively (farmer1998:20627).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch13, from Latin at farmer1998:20561

Attribution: Pico's own opinion · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

The thesis proposes a structural inversion between two levels of reality Pico calls the "first world" and the "second world": what functions as substance in one functions as accident in the other, and conversely (farmer1998:20561). Farmer's note glosses "second world" as the rational or animate nature, or the material world informed by that nature, and "first world" as the intellectual nature, and refers the reader to his own discussion, page 98 (farmer1998:20627).

Historiography

The German study Erkenntnistheorie und christliche Kabbalah cites the thesis's Latin text in a footnote that also references Roulier's Jean Pic de la Mirandole and Farmer's Syncretism in the West on the surrounding pages, without stating what conclusion it draws from the citation (kabbalah_paradigma:1768).
