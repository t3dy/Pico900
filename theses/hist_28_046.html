Latin

Non punitur Rex terrae in terra, quin prius humilietur militia coelestis in coelo.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 18406

Apparatus (Farmer): 1487 ponitur

The 1487 reprint misread 'punitur' as 'ponitur', an error Farmer states 'was followed by all later Renaissance editors of Pico's text' (paraphrase of Farmer's note; the apparatus variant itself is recorded at farmer1998:18416).

English

The king of the earth is not punished on earth, unless the heavenly host is first humbled in heaven. unverified draft

Follows the 1486 reading 'punitur' ('is punished') rather than the 1487 misprint 'ponitur' ('is placed'), per Farmer's note (farmer1998:18463).

Doctrine

Farmer's note states that both texts Wirszubski (1989: 51) proposes as potential sources emphasize 'the favorite Pichean theme that proportion exists between higher and lower realms', here expressed in an eschatological register (paraphrase, farmer1998:18463). He also records the transmission error by which the 1487 reprint read 'ponitur' for 'punitur', an error followed, in his words, 'by all later Renaissance editors of Pico's text' (paraphrase, farmer1998:18463).

Historiography

Wirszubski quotes the thesis as his 'Conclusio XLVI' and states that 'what this thesis states is not, strictly speaking, a specifically Kabbalistic' claim, though the excerpt consulted breaks off before he completes the point (wirszubski1989:2939).
