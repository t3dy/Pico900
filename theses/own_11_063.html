Latin

Sicut Aristoteles diuiniorem philosophiam, quam philosophi antiqui sub fabulis et apologis uelarunt, ipse sub philosophicae speculationis facie dissimulauit, et uerborum breuitate obscurauit, ita Rabi Moyses aegyptius, in libro qui a latinis dicitur dux neutrorum, dum per superficialem uerborum corticem uidetur cum Philosophis ambulare, per latentes profundi sensus intelligentias, mysteria complectitur Cabalae.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 27748

English

Aristotle hid the more divine philosophy, which the ancient philosophers had veiled under fables and tales, beneath the look of philosophical speculation and darkened it by the brevity of his words; in the same way Rabbi Moses the Egyptian, in the book the Latins call the 'dux neutrorum', while he seems, through the outer rind of his words, to walk with the philosophers, embraces through the hidden understandings of a deep sense the mysteries of the Cabala. unverified draft

Sense checked against farmer1998:27793. 'dux neutrorum' kept in Latin, being the Latin title of Maimonides' Guide (Farmer's note, farmer1998:27809, names the Guide for the Perplexed). The OCR gives 'dissimulauit_et' at farmer1998:27748, read as a comma.
