Latin

Qui sciet proprietatem Aquilonis in cabala, sciet cur sathan Christo promisit regna mundi, si cadens eum adorasset.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 27343

English

Whoever will know the property of the North Wind in the Kabbalah will know why Satan promised Christ the kingdoms of the world, if, falling down, he would worship him. unverified draft

'Aquilonis' (the North Wind) is glossed as the standard symbol of the fifth sefirah, regularly associated with Satan and the origin of evil but also, for Pico, with magical power; whether 'regna mundi' (the kingdoms of the world) is meant to signify the sefirah Malkhut is left an open question by Farmer's note (paraphrase; farmer1998:27408).

Doctrine

Farmer's note identifies 'Aquilo' (the north wind) as the standard symbol of the fifth sefirah, one of whose properties was power, and reports that the fifth sefirah was regularly associated with Satan and the origin of evil, while Pico elsewhere also associates it with magical power (paraphrase; farmer1998:27408, cross-referring to 10>10). The thesis reads the Gospel temptation narrative, in which Satan offers Christ the kingdoms of the world (farmer1998:27343), as intelligible through this sefirotic property.

unverified draft

Historiography

Wirszubski's Pico della Mirandola's Encounter with Jewish Mysticism identifies the sefirah in question as Might or Judgment (Gevurah) and treats the thesis as an example, stated 'secundum opinionem propriam,' of Pico's independent kabbalistic reasoning (wirszubski1989:9590). A German study of Pico's Kabbalistic epistemology quotes the thesis's Latin in a discussion pairing it with theses 11>22 and 11>44 (kabbalah_paradigma:2106); its further German argument, including a remark relating the sefirah Geburah's effect structurally to original sin, is not established here in its full sense.
