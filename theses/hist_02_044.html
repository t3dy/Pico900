Doctrine

The thesis denies that God has an idea specifically of prime matter considered in itself; Farmer notes a countervailing Thomist position at Summa theologiae 1a.15.3 ad 3, that one can argue an idea of matter exists in God, though not distinctly from the idea of the composite of matter and form (farmer1998:11954). Edelheit reports that Thomas, in the Quaestiones disputatae de veritate (q.3, a.5), holds that since matter is caused by God, 'it is necessary to posit that in some way its idea is in God, since whatever is caused by him retains some likeness to him', which Edelheit calls 'less decisive than Pico' on the question (edelheit2022:12945, 12970).

Historiography

Edelheit (2022) reads thesis 44 as 'strongly connected to thesis 43' and more categorical than its identified Thomistic source, the Quaestiones disputatae de veritate, where Thomas argues for some idea of matter in God via the principle that an effect retains a likeness of its cause (edelheit2022:12945, 12970); Farmer (1998) independently notes the same Thomist qualification at Summa theologiae 1a.15.3 ad 3 (farmer1998:11954).
