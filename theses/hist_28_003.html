Latin

Quamuis nomen ineffabile sit proprietas clementiae, negandum tamen non est quin contineat proprietatem iudicii.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 17540

English

Although the ineffable name is a property of clemency, it must nevertheless not be denied that it contains the property of judgment. unverified draft

'nomen ineffabile' = the Tetragrammaton (YHVH), the ineffable divine name, per the Latin at farmer1998:17540. 'clementiae'/'iudicii' = clemency and judgment; Farmer's note associates these with the sixth and fifth sefirot respectively (paraphrase; farmer1998:17608).

Doctrine

Thesis 28.3 holds that the ineffable divine name, though a property of clemency, must also be granted to contain the property of judgment (farmer1998:17540). Farmer's note identifies the 'ineffable name' as YHVH, 'normally associated with the sixth of the ten sefirot', and 'clemency'/'judgment' as 'usual properties of the sixth and fifth sefirot respectively', glossing the thesis as an instance of Pico's standard view that all divine properties exist in some way on every hierarchical level (paraphrase; farmer1998:17608).

Context

Farmer's cross-reference apparatus ties the thesis to 1>4, on Aquinas, Scotus, and Giles agreeing fundamentally on the subject of theology despite verbal disagreement (farmer1998:18587), and to 24.16-17 of Proclus (farmer1998:16161, 16166).

Reception

Corazzol quotes the thesis in full, identifying it as 'the third of the forty-seven Conclusiones secundum secretam doctrinam sapientum Hebreorum cabalistarum', and asks: "Can we assume that Pico indeed had an interest in Middat ha-Din, the 'Property of Judgment', and its power?" (corazzol2019:919). Wirszubski quotes the thesis in full as his 'Conclusio III' (wirszubski1989:1355, 1358, 7253, 9235).

Historiography

Wirszubski identifies the thesis's direct manuscript source (wirszubski1989:1358): "The direct source is Liber Combinationum, Cod. Vat. Ebr. 190, fol. 41r:" Corazzol frames the thesis as evidence of Pico's specific interest in the Kabbalistic concept of Middat ha-Din, the 'Property of Judgment' (corazzol2019:919).
