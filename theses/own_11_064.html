Latin

In textu, Audi Israhel, dominus deus noster dominus unus, rectius est ut intelligatur ibi collectio ab inferiori ad superius, et a superiori ad inferius, quam ab inferiori ad superius bis.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 27756

English

In the text 'Hear, O Israel, the Lord our God, the Lord is one,' it is more correct that there be understood a gathering from the lower to the higher, and from the higher to the lower, than from the lower to the higher twice. unverified draft

The quoted text is the liturgical Shema, Deuteronomy 6:4, which Farmer's note identifies as, like this thesis, underscoring the oneness of God (paraphrase; farmer1998:27815).

Doctrine

The thesis reads the repeated 'Lord' of the Shema (Deuteronomy 6:4) as pointing not to a double movement from the lower to the higher but to a reciprocal 'gathering,' from lower to higher and from higher to lower (farmer1998:27756). Farmer's note glosses this as pertaining to the underlying unity of the seven inferior and three superior sefirot, while stating that how the thesis bears on Christianity specifically is not clear from the note itself (paraphrase; farmer1998:27815).

Historiography

Wirszubski's Pico della Mirandola's Encounter with Jewish Mysticism situates the thesis within a chapter linking 'the symbolism of the ten sefirot' to 'Pico's Kabbalistic confirmation of Christianity,' and quotes its Latin in full (wirszubski1989:9129, 9131).
