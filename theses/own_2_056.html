Latin

Sonum non ex motu aeris intercepti inter duo corpora se percutientia, ut tenet Aristoteles_et expositores eius, sed ex contactu tali talium uel talium corporum, talem uel talem sonum causari, dico.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 19772

Apparatus (Farmer): 1486 intercoepti

1486 reads 'intercoepti' for 'intercepti'; an orthographic variant, sense unaffected.

English

I hold that sound is caused not by the motion of the air trapped between two colliding bodies, as Aristotle and his commentators maintain, but by the contact, of such-and-such a kind, of such-and-such bodies, producing such-and-such a sound. unverified draft
