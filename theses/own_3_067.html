Latin

Cuilibet seriei animarum unus correspondet purus intellectus.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 21342

English

To each series of souls there corresponds one pure intellect. unverified draft

'Series' renders 'series animarum' as a ranked or ordered chain of souls, the sense required by the parallel theses 3>68-69 (farmer1998:21345, farmer1998:21348).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch14, from Latin at farmer1998:21342

Attribution: Among the conclusions Pico titles 'paradoxical, according to his own opinion, introducing new teachings in philosophy'; Farmer's note treats it with 3>68-69 as a set concerning celestial souls (farmer1998:21373). · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

The thesis assigns one separate, pure intellect to each ordered series of souls (farmer1998:21342); Farmer's note glosses 'celestial souls' in this cluster as the rational souls that inhabit the heavenly bodies, though the further problem his note goes on to raise is not given complete in the material consulted (farmer1998:21373).

unverified draft

Context

Farmer's own cross-reference apparatus links this thesis, together with 3>68-69, to the Averroist theses on the unity of the intellect reported earlier in the Conclusiones: 7.2, 'Vna est anima intellectiua in omnibus hominibus' [there is one intellective soul in all men] (farmer1998:12937), 7.3 (farmer1998:12940), and 7.4, on whether an individuated intellect could survive death (farmer1998:12947).

unverified draft

Historiography

Dougherty's footnote on 7.2 explicitly directs the reader onward: "See Conclusions 3>67–9" (dougherty2008:9367), treating this cluster of Pico's own paradoxical theses as bearing on the Averroist unity-of-intellect question reported in section 7; an unauthored German-language study groups 3>67 with 3>68 in a note on Pico's Neoplatonic and Chaldean sources, alongside conclusions attributed to Proclus (24.45, 24.51) and to the Chaldean theologians (26.5) (kabbalah_paradigma:1441).
