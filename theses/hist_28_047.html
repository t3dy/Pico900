Latin

Per dictionem AMEN ordo habetur expressus quomodo numerationum procedant influxus.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 18410

English

Through the word AMEN an order is expressed of how the influx of the numerations proceeds. unverified draft

'numerationum' is rendered literally as 'numerations', and 'AMEN' left untranslated, following the Latin at farmer1998:18410; the packet consulted does not gloss either term for this thesis specifically.

Attribution: Reports Hebrew Cabalists (farmer1998:18410). · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

Farmer's note reads the thesis as reflecting 'the general idea that mystical secrets lay hidden in the primal language, Hebrew', cross-referring 28.33 (paraphrase, farmer1998:18468). He states that thesis 11>65 'throws further light on Pico's sense in this thesis', and that the underlying views can be correlated with Pico's theses on the powers of the 'leaders' and other henads drawn from Proclus, citing 24.27-29 (paraphrase, farmer1998:18468).

Historiography

Wirszubski quotes the thesis and states that 'Recanati affords all that is needed for the interpretation of this thesis', though the excerpt consulted breaks off before he supplies that interpretation (wirszubski1989:2978). Separately, he quotes the same conclusio again as his 'Conclusio xlvii' in a discussion of the Hebrew term kawwanah, following a passage on the term middah (wirszubski1989:7560, 7562).
