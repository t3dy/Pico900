Latin

Ex praecedenti conclusione potest quis rectius exponere quam exponat Proclus quid sibi uelit illud dictum theologi inducentis opificem mundi noctem consulentem de opificio mundano.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 25895

English

From the preceding conclusion one can expound more correctly than Proclus does what that saying of the theologian means, which represents the craftsman of the world as consulting Night about the making of the world. unverified draft

'theologi' continues the reference of the preceding thesis to the Orphic hymnist as 'theologus' (farmer1998:25892).

Doctrine

The thesis claims that the preceding conclusion, identifying Night in Orpheus with Ein-Sof in the Cabala, yields a more correct reading of the Orphic saying about the world's maker consulting Night than Proclus's own reading of it (farmer1998:25895; farmer1998:25892). Farmer's note explains that Proclus, commenting on the Timaeus, places 'night' below the pinnacle of his intelligible trinity, and takes this thesis to register Pico's disagreement with Proclus on that point (farmer1998:25929).

unverified draft

Context

Busi and Ebgi's edition prints the thesis in a continuous run with the two Orphic-Kabbalistic conclusions flanking it, on Night/Ein-Sof and on Ein-Sof's uniqueness among the numerations (busi2014:24761).

Reception

Busi and Ebgi cite the conclusion by number and quote its Latin text in their commentary, in sequence with the adjoining theses (busi2014:15082; busi2014:24761).
