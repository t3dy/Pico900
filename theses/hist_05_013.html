Doctrine

Thesis 5.13 asserts that a relation is not really distinct from its foundation (farmer1998:12617). Farmer treats theses 5.11-13 as one series continuing from 4.16-17 of Scotus (paraphrase; farmer1998:12655). Edelheit summarizes: "in thesis 13 we find that relation is not really distinguish[able] from a foundation" (edelheit2022:15727), and quotes the Latin directly, citing 'Pico, 900 Theses, p. 244' (edelheit2022:15749).

unverified draft

Context

Farmer's cross-reference apparatus ties the thesis to 4.16 of Scotus, that a creature's relation to God is really identical with, though formally distinct from, its foundation (farmer1998:12361), and to 5.11 of Henry of Ghent, that 'ratitudo' is formally a respectus (farmer1998:12610). Edelheit discusses the thesis alongside the immediately preceding thesis of the same series ('thesis 12', on the mutuality required for a real relation), quoting its Latin as well (edelheit2022:15727).

unverified draft

Reception

Edelheit finds a close parallel in Henry's own Quodlibet III, question 4, stating that there 'we can find some expressions which are close to theses 11 and 13, according to which every relation in what concerns existence in created beings is founded or based on ( fundatur super) some accident' (edelheit2022:15731). He also notes a structurally similar formulation in William of Ockham's Expositio in libros physicorum Aristotelis III, 2, quoting Ockham's Latin: "Nec Philosophus et Commentator utuntur tali modo loquendi, quia relatio non est aliqua res distincta ab illo quod vocant moderni fundamen-" (edelheit2022:15760, the source text breaking off at a page turn), and situates the underlying idea within a modern debate, citing 'Decorte, "Relatio as Modus Essendi"' (edelheit2022:15760).

Historiography

Edelheit gives a cautious final assessment: "It is difficult to determine whether Pico was fully aware of all the philosophical complexities involved here, but the thesis shows at least some familiarity with these issues" (edelheit2022:15874).
