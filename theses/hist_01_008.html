Latin

Lumen non habet in medio nisi esse intentionale.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 11047

English

Light has, in the medium, only intentional being. unverified draft

'esse intentionale' is the weaker, representational mode of existence Farmer's note contrasts with sound's greater 'real' existence in the companion thesis 1.7 (farmer1998:11115).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-swarm-batch7, from Latin at farmer1998:11047

Attribution: Reports Albert the Great (farmer1998:11047). · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

Farmer's note (shared with 1.7) traces theses 1.7-8 to commentary on Aristotle's De anima 2.7-8, reporting that for Albert the greater 'intentionality' (or 'spirituality') of light compared to sound was shown by sound being affected by wind while colour is not, though in medieval debate 'all sensual images become “spiritual” once they pass through the senses' (farmer1998:11115). Edelheit finds that 'thesis 8 states that light in a medium has only intentional existence' (edelheit2022:9579), locating Pico's exact wording at 900 Theses p. 214 and Albert's discussion in his De anima commentary, Book 2, Tract. 3 (edelheit2022:9611).

Context

Edelheit treats theses 7-10 as a set on sense perception drawn from Albert (edelheit2022:9488) and remarks that 'it is hard to tell... why Pico has chosen to dedicate several theses, for instance theses 7–10, to some very standard and rather limited accounts of sense perception,' finding the rationale for their place in the larger project of the 900 Theses unclear (edelheit2022:10159).

Historiography

Edelheit (2022) traces the doctrine to Albert's Opera omnia commentary on De anima, Book 2, Tractatus 3, Chapter 5, and to Albert's further discussion of sight, light, medium and colour in De homine (edelheit2022:9611), while questioning why Pico gave several theses to such standard material (edelheit2022:10159).
