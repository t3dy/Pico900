Thesis 7>11

Latin

Per numeros habetur uia ad omnis scibilis inuestigationem et intellectionem; ad cuius conclusionis uerificationem polliceor me ad infrascriptas xxiii. questiones per uiam numerorum responsurum.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 23732; 1486 print folio <26r/26v>

The copy-text breaks 'intellectionem' across a line ('in/tellectionem'); the break is repaired here rather than reproduced as a word division (farmer1998:23732).

English

Through numbers a way is had to the investigation and understanding of everything knowable; and to verify this conclusion I promise to answer the twenty-three questions written below by the way of numbers. unverified draft

'via' is rendered 'way' rather than more freely as 'method', following Mandosio's argument that the literal rendering is preferable (mandosio2012:166).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch16, from Latin at farmer1998:23732

Attribution: Pico's own opinion · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

This is the last and most sweeping of the mathematical conclusions in the series, claiming for number a route to the investigation and understanding of everything knowable, and committing Pico to defend the claim through the twenty-three numerical questions that follow it in the text (farmer1998:23732). Farmer calls it one of three or four methods the text credits with leading to knowledge 'of everything knowable', a claim he notes de Lubac denies Pico makes anywhere (farmer1998:23794).

Historiography

Wirszubski quotes the conclusion as the last of a series before turning to the Sefer Yetzirah's teaching on the number of ways the world was created, adding no comment of his own beyond the quotation (wirszubski1989:9683). Mandosio quotes the same Latin and argues that via should be translated literally as 'way' rather than more idiomatically as Farmer's 'method' (mandosio2012:166).
