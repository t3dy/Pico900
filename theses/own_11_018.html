Latin

Qui coniunxerit Astrologiam Cabalae uidebit quod sabbatizare et quiescere conuenientius fit post Christum die dominico, quam die sabbati.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 26797

Follows Farmer's text (farmer1998:26797-26798). Akopyan's quotations read 'coniurixerit' for 'coniunxerit' and 'Cabale' for 'Cabalae' in the copies consulted (akopyan2018:695; akopyan2021:1451).

English

One who has joined astrology with the Cabala will see that since Christ, keeping the sabbath and resting falls more fittingly on the Lord's day than on the sabbath day. unverified draft

'sabbatizare' is the Christian Latin verb 'to keep sabbath'. 'dies dominicus' and 'dies sabbati' are Sunday and Saturday (farmer1998:26827). 'post Christum' is taken in the temporal sense, as Akopyan also takes it (akopyan2021:1428).

Doctrine

The thesis claims that a reader who combines astrology with the Cabala will find Sunday, not Saturday, the more fitting day of rest in the Christian era (farmer1998:26797). Farmer glosses the two days as Sunday and Saturday but warns that more lies beneath, since Pico assigns days to particular sefirot and sefirot to particular planets or to Christ (farmer1998:26827). The sefirot are the ten emanated powers of God in theosophical Kabbalah, which the Latin translations Pico read call numerationes (wirszubski1989:7451). In Pico's order they are Crown (Keter), Wisdom (Hokmah), Intelligence (Binah), Piety (Hesed), Judgment or Power (Gevurah), Glory or Beauty (Tiferet), Eternity (Nesah), Splendour (Hod), Foundation (Yesod) and Kingdom (Malkut) (wirszubski1989:7066; wirszubski1989:7478; busi2014:13589; busi2014:15728). Wirszubski states that in Kabbalistic symbolism a 'day' as a rule denotes a sefirah (wirszubski1989:8524). In 11>48 Pico sets Saturn at the seventh numeration and the sun at the sixth (farmer1998:27425). Farmer correlates Jesus, the 'sun' of 11>51, with that sixth sefirah (farmer1998:27508), and he cross-refers 11>46 for the pairing of Christ and the sun (farmer1998:27406). The editor infers one reading from these statements: the Saturday sabbath answers to Saturn, and the Lord's day to the sefirah and the luminary that Pico gives to the Messiah (farmer1998:27425; farmer1998:27508). Farmer himself declines to fix a single reading, because the flexibility of Pico's symbolism yields several plausible ones (farmer1998:26830).

Context

Akopyan places the thesis among those in which Pico justifies Christian practice by Kabbalistic astrology, here an astrological reason for keeping the sabbath on Sunday (akopyan2021:3219; akopyan2018:674). He notes that Pico adds no comment to the proposal (akopyan2021:1423). Pico had already given the sabbath a Christological sense in 11>16, where the three letters of the Hebrew word for sabbath mean that the world keeps sabbath when the Son of God becomes man (wirszubski1989:8405). In 11>61 all things at last keep sabbath in the Son, who is the Wisdom of the Father (wirszubski1989:8368), and Farmer cross-refers that thesis to 11>16 and 11>18 (farmer1998:27766). The German study of Pico's epistemology and Christian Kabbalah (key kabbalah_paradigma; author not confirmed in the corpus copy) reports that the Kabbalistic glossaries can call the ninth sefirah, Yesod, 'Sabbath' and identify the 'great Sabbath' with the third, Binah (kabbalah_paradigma:2095).

Reception

The thesis is not among those the commission censured: Blum observes that the Cabalistic theses according to Pico's own opinion went unchallenged (dougherty2008:2808). In the Disputationes adversus astrologiam divinatricem Pico rejected Roger Bacon's claim that Christians err in not resting on Saturday, the day of Saturn, a star judged unfavourable for action (akopyan2021:5273; akopyan2021:5299). Akopyan observes that Pico there seems to have forgotten his own proposal in the Conclusiones about the sabbath (akopyan2021:5277).

Historiography

Farmer reads the thesis through Pico's correlations of days, sefirot and planets and leaves its precise sense open (farmer1998:26827). Akopyan reads it as an astrological argument for Sunday rest and sets it against the later rejection of Bacon, which he treats as a change of mind (akopyan2021:5277). Wirszubski's account of the sabbath in the Christian theses rests on 11>16 and 11>61 and does not treat this thesis in the passages consulted (wirszubski1989:8405; wirszubski1989:8366). The editor notes the contrast the two texts draw: Bacon's astrology alone pointed to Saturday, whereas Pico's thesis claims that astrology joined to the Cabala points to Sunday, so that the Kabbalistic term is what reverses the astrological verdict (akopyan2021:5299; farmer1998:26797).
