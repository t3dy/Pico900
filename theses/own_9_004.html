Doctrine

The thesis is a syllogism stated in one sentence: magic is the practical part of natural science (9>3), and of any practicable object the operation that acts on it is nobler than the one that contemplates it, other things being equal (3>46); so magic is the noblest part of natural science (farmer1998:25122-25126; farmer1998:20976-20977). Taken together, Farmer writes, 'these theses seem not only to condone but to require us to work magic in the world' (farmer1998:7262-7263). The operation required is not the mastery of nature: the magus as Pico pictured him was not a transformer of nature but its minister, ruled by the lowest order of angels and entrusted in turn with governing the material world (farmer1998:7272-7276). The Oration uses the same image of the magus as 'Nature's minister, not her Artificer', ascribed to Plotinus (copenhaver2019:19627-19628). Copenhaver reads the nobility differently, by the height of the object: because magic and Kabbalah look to the stars and beyond, knowledge of them is nobler than the physics of change below the Moon (copenhaver2019:17648-17653).

Context

The cross-reference is faulty: the thesis cited as the forty-seventh paradoxical dogmatizing conclusion is the forty-sixth in the printed text, which Farmer takes as evidence that one thesis was dropped from that section at a late date (farmer1998:25175-25178). In his note on the editio princeps Farmer argues that at least two theological theses and one from 3>1-71 were excluded while the work was in press, and he cites 9>4 as an example of the faulty cross-referencing (farmer1998:23416-23421). Pico's own copy of the text was numbered even though the printed edition was not, as cross-references like this one show (farmer1998:2133-2135). For Pico natural philosophy, of which magic was the apex and summit, was the third of four preparatory studies leading to the mystical ascent (farmer1998:7220-7223).

Reception

In the Apology Pico repeated the claim, calling magic, in Mandosio's rendering, 'the absolute fulfillment of natural philosophy' and 'the highest point of all philosophy' (mandosio2012:566-568). The editors of the Cambridge Oration record Farmer's correction of the numeral in their commentary (borghesi2012:4531-4537).

Historiography

Farmer uses the pair 9>3-4 against Yates's claim that Pico's magic prepared operational attitudes to nature that led to modern science (farmer1998:7262-7270). The operative side of Pico's magic, he argues, is best read in terms of cosmic fall and redemption, so that the magus functions as a cosmic priest (farmer1998:7280-7289). Copenhaver derives the thesis from the reasoning of the Apology's defence of 9>9, where the certainty that magic gives is ranked by the dignity of what it studies (copenhaver2019:17643-17653). Mandosio states the general principle behind the thesis as 3>46 and quotes both formulas from the Apology (mandosio2012:563-568). The thesis shows Pico building a claim about magic from a thesis in natural philosophy, and it preserves, through its wrong numeral, a trace of the censorship he imposed on his own text in press (editorial inference from farmer1998:25175-25178 and farmer1998:23416-23421).
