Doctrine

Farmer's note treats this thesis as opening a run, 3>13-18, and states that its immediate successor, 3>15, is 'regularly cited (originally by Cassirer and most recently by Wirszubski) as evidence of the influence on Pico of Nicholas of Cusa's coincidentia oppositorum', but argues that the idea common to Pico and Cusanus 'was a scholastic commonplace' rather than distinctively Cusan (paraphrase and quotation, farmer1998:20526). He directs the reader to thesis 2.31 for the implications of this run for traditional logic (paraphrase, farmer1998:20526).

Historiography

Steiris cites this thesis in a footnote on Anaxagoras and Heraclitus, in the same note as references to Cassirer's and Wirszubski's readings of Pico's relation to Cusanus and to Farmer's page 405 (steiris2014:375).
