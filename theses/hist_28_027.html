Latin

Sicut congregatio aquarum est iustus, ita mare ad quod tendunt omnia flumina est diuinitas.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 18074

English

Just as the gathering of the waters is 'the just one,' so the sea to which all the rivers run is 'divinity.' unverified draft

'congregatio aquarum'/'iustus' ('gathering of waters'/'the just') is, per Farmer, a symbol and alternate name of the ninth sefirah, drawing on Proverbs 10:25; 'mare ad quod tendunt omnia flumina'/'diuinitas' ('sea to which all rivers run'/'divinity') symbolizes the Shekhinah, the tenth sefirah, drawing on Ecclesiastes 1:7 (farmer1998:18145).

Attribution: Reports Hebrew Cabalists (farmer1998:18074). · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

Farmer glosses 'gathering of the waters' (Proverbs 10:25) and 'the just' as a symbol and alternate name of the ninth sefirah, and 'sea to which all rivers run' (Ecclesiastes 1:7) and 'divinity' as the Shekhinah, alternate name of the tenth sefirah, directing the reader to the Recanati text quoted by Wirszubski at page 42 of his 1989 study (farmer1998:18145). He compares the thesis's method of correlating isolated texts of Hebrew Scripture to Pico's theses from Proclus, for instance 24.8, which correlate isolated texts of the Platonic corpus, the aim in both cases being to demonstrate secret harmonies within the respective sacred texts (farmer1998:18145).

unverified draft

Context

Farmer cross-references 24.8, on the correspondence in Plato's Phaedrus, Parmenides between 'the back of heaven,' 'the one,' and 'the depth/axis of heaven' (farmer1998:15972); 28.8, on souls descending from the third light to the fourth and fifth days (farmer1998:17645); and 6.7, on how a superior angel illuminates an inferior one (farmer1998:12693) (farmer1998:18145).

Historiography

Wirszubski transcribes the thesis as his 'Conclusio XXVII' and gives Recanati, fol 9va, as its source (wirszubski1989:2404); elsewhere he glosses the underlying scriptural allusion (Ecclesiastes 1:7), explaining that 'all the torrents tend to the sea' because the six extremities called 'torrents' tend, through Tiferet, toward Atheret (the Diadem, the tenth sefirah), which is called 'sea' (wirszubski1989:1705). An unsigned German-language study of Pico's Kabbalistic paradigm (author not established from the material consulted) likewise quotes the thesis in full alongside 5>32, on the Epinomis and the Cabalists' 'world to come' (kabbalah_paradigma:1815).
