Latin

Methaphysicus et dialecticus aeque de omnibus disputant, sed ille demonstratiue, hic probabiliter.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 14833

English

The metaphysician and the dialectician dispute about all things equally, but the former demonstratively, the latter probably. unverified draft

'ille' (the former) refers back to the metaphysician, 'hic' (the latter) to the dialectician; 'probabiliter' carries the technical Aristotelian sense of arguing from reputable premises (dialectic), as opposed to demonstrative syllogism (farmer1998:14833; edelheit2022:4336).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch8, from Latin at farmer1998:14833

Attribution: Reports Alexander of Aphrodisias (farmer1998:14833). · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

The thesis holds that the metaphysician and the dialectician dispute about all the same matters, but the metaphysician demonstratively and the dialectician only probably (farmer1998:14833). Farmer's note traces the background to Aristotle's Prior Analytics 1.1 and Topics 1.1, and to the wording of Pico's own thesis 3>9, and states that Elia del Medigo gathered material on this question for Pico, citing Kieszkowski's 1964 study, pages 52-55 (farmer1998:14863). Edelheit cites the thesis, quoting it in full, as pertinent to the terminology and practice of 'the sceptical Academy' regarding probable argument, in a discussion that also refers the reader to Rudolf Schuessler's account of the later scholastic debate on probable opinions (edelheit2022:4336).

Context

Edelheit's discussion situates the thesis within a broader treatment of scepticism and probabilism in Pico's vocabulary (edelheit2022:4336).

Historiography

Edelheit is the only scholar in the material opened to discuss this thesis; he quotes its Latin verbatim and treats it as evidence for Pico's engagement with the vocabulary of probable versus demonstrative argument (edelheit2022:4336; edelheit2022:4337).
