Latin

Tantus est numerus hymnorum Orphei, quantus est numerus cum quo deus triplex creauit saeculum, sub quaternarii pythagorici forma numeratus.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 25675; 1486 print folio <29v/30r>

Apparatus (Farmer): 1486 qnaternani

The apparatus records a 1486 print variant 'qnaternani' for 'quaternarii' (farmer1998:25691).

English

The number of Orpheus's hymns is as great as the number with which the threefold God created the world, reckoned under the form of the Pythagorean quaternary. unverified draft

The 'Pythagorean quaternary' is the sum of the first four natural numbers (1+2+3+4=10), yielding the decad whose symbolism recurs in 11>56 (farmer1998:13659; farmer1998:14551).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch17, from Latin at farmer1998:25675

Attribution: Pico's own opinion, section 10> (Orphic Hymns) (farmer1998:25675). · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

Farmer explains the 'Pythagorean quaternarius' as the first four natural numbers, the numerical correspondents of point, line, plane, and solid, whose sum (1+2+3+4=10) yields the denarius or 'decad' whose symbolism, he writes, 'permeates Pico's thought', cross-referencing 11>56 (farmer1998:13659). Farmer adds that the specific series of numbers, and what Pico meant by 'the number with which the threefold god created the world,' are unknown (farmer1998:13660-13661); he reports that Wirszubski (in a work Farmer cites as Wirszubski 1989, p. 197, not independently checked here) identifies Orpheus's deus triplex, the 'threefold god', with the Christian Trinity, and reads the thesis through gematria and kabbalistic divine names (farmer1998:13661).

Historiography

Busi and Ebgi quote the Latin and give their own Italian translation, 'Il numero degli Inni di Orfeo è lo stesso con cui il dio trino creò il mondo, calcolato secondo il quaternario pitagorico' (busi2014:17244). Wirszubski also quotes the Latin verbatim, as 'Conclusio v' (wirszubski1989:10061).
