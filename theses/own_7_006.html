Latin

Nihil magis nociuum theologo quam frequens et assidua in mathematicis Euclidis exercitatio.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 23713

English

Nothing is more harmful to the theologian than frequent and continual practice in the mathematics of Euclid. unverified draft

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch1, from Latin at farmer1998:23713

Attribution: Pico's own opinion (farmer1998:23713). · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

The conclusion holds that frequent, continual practice in Euclidean mathematics harms the theologian (farmer1998:23713). Cassirer reads it as Pico's conclusion that such practice is harmful to real and genuine knowledge of God (cassirer1942:1099). The neighboring theses set the arts in parallel: music moves the spirits that serve the soul as medicine moves those that govern the body (7>7, farmer1998:23717). Medicine heals the soul through the body, music the body through the soul, in the next conclusion (7>8, farmer1998:23721). Formal, non-material arithmetic, by contrast, gives the best road to natural prophecy (7>9, farmer1998:23724).

Context

Mandosio's study of Pico's numerology quotes this conclusion alongside the fourth thesis of the Mathematics section, that the objects of the mathematicians, taken absolutely, perfect the intellect not at all (mandosio2012:411). An unsigned set of notes on Pico's sources for self-knowledge judges Pico 'rather contemptuous of Euclid' even while giving Pythagorean and Platonic mathematics 'a significant place' in his project (selfknowledge_notes:1236). The same notes, citing Pico's ownership of a Greek manuscript of Proclus' commentary on Euclid's Elements, conjecture that his target was the moderni's use of Euclid rather than the ancient commentary tradition (selfknowledge_notes:1237).

Historiography

Cassirer quotes the conclusion as evidence that Pico judged mathematical exercise harmful to 'real and genuine knowledge of God' (cassirer1942:1099). Mandosio's article on John Dee's numerology cites the same conclusion within a footnote pairing it with Pico's fourth mathematical thesis (mandosio2012:411). An unsigned set of notes on Pico and self-knowledge is the only source consulted here to comment on the conclusion's target specifically, judging Pico 'rather contemptuous of Euclid' (selfknowledge_notes:1236).
