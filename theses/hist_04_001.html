Latin

Charitas non est distinctus habitus ab habitu gratiae, quo mediante spiritus sanctus animam inhabitat.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 12140

English

Charity is not a habit distinct from the habit of grace, by whose mediation the Holy Spirit indwells the soul. unverified draft

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch5, from Latin at farmer1998:12140

Attribution: Reports Duns Scotus, first of the theses drawn from his work (farmer1998:12135, 12140). · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

The thesis denies that charity is a distinct habit from the habit of grace, holding instead that grace is the very habit 'through the mediation of' which the Holy Spirit indwells the soul (edelheit2022:13746). Farmer's note glosses a 'habit' as a modification of a substance that predisposes it to act in a particular way, and remarks that the thesis's apparent deterministic implications are qualified elsewhere in the 900 (farmer1998:12169).

unverified draft

Context

Farmer cross-references the thesis to 2.9 from Thomas, on the merit of acts elicited by charity, and to 4.22, a later Scotus thesis on habits as partial efficient causes of acts (farmer1998:12169).

unverified draft

Historiography

Edelheit identifies Scotus's Ordinatio 1, distinction 17, part 1, questions 1-2 as the passage behind the thesis, and quotes Scotus's own formulation that charity 'does not seem to be really a different habit from that by which the Holy Spirit indwells the soul,' so that the Spirit does not move the soul to love, as it moves it to believe and hope, through an intermediate habit distinct from indwelling itself (edelheit2022:13770).
