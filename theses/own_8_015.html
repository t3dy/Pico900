Latin

Quid sit intelligendum per capras apud Zoroastrem intelliget qui legerit in libro Bair quae sit affinitas capris et quae agnis cum spiritibus.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 25046

English

What is to be understood by 'goats' in Zoroaster, he who has read in the book Bahir will understand: what affinity goats have, and what affinity lambs have, with spirits. unverified draft

'libro Bair' is the Sefer ha-Bahir; Farmer's note records that the passages Pico draws on are not in the Bahir proper but in a collection of fragments attached, in one manuscript family, to its end (farmer1998:25087).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch17, from Latin at farmer1998:25046

Attribution: Pico's own opinion, section 8> (Zoroaster and Chaldeans) (farmer1998:25046). · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

The thesis promises that a reader of the Bahir will understand what Zoroaster meant by 'goats,' by learning what affinity goats and lambs bear to spirits (farmer1998:25046). Farmer's note states that Scholem (1954: 167) found that the passages referred to are not in the Bahir itself but in a set of fragments appended, in one family of manuscripts, to the end of that text, and that Flavius Mithridates began translating these fragments on the same line where he ended his version of the Bahir, in Vatican Codex 191, which may have contributed to Pico's citation (farmer1998:25087).

Historiography

Wirszubski quotes the thesis's Latin verbatim, as 'Conclusio xv,' matching Farmer's wording, and states that the direct source of the thesis's Kabbalistic component was identified, though the sentence naming that identification is not given complete in the material consulted (wirszubski1989:9896).
