Doctrine

The thesis holds that being (ens), though the first, most universal object of the intellect, is not itself predicated quidditatively of its own attributes and ultimate differences (edelheit2022:14228). Farmer's extended note treats 4.7-4.8 as reflecting 'the most important Thomist-Scotist conflict that Pico planned to resolve at Rome': Scotus read Thomas as confining metaphysics to 'individual quiddities,' a reading that for Scotus undercut metaphysical knowledge of being qua being, and Scotus accordingly held that being is said 'univocally,' not merely 'analogically,' of God and creatures (farmer1998:12275). Farmer adds that this transcendental, maximally indeterminate concept of being is what the formula in 4.8 reflects (farmer1998:12275).

Context

Farmer states that Pico's own rejection of Scotus's reading of Thomas is suggested elsewhere in the 900, at 2.42 and 2>5, and that Pico planned further treatment of the point at 1>14 (farmer1998:12275).

unverified draft

Historiography

Edelheit calls thesis 8 'a conceptual continuation of thesis 7,' both drawn from the same Scotist discussion of the unique status of the concept of being with respect to knowledge of God, an instance of what he terms the concatenatio, or internal connection, linking different theses (edelheit2022:14232). He locates Scotus's own formulation in the Lectura 1, distinction 3, part 1, questions 1-2 (edelheit2022:14259).
