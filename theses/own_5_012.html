English

The Sun and Moon cooperate with the aforesaid lives as universal causes, and by appropriation: the Moon with Mercury and Saturn, the Sun with Venus and Mars, and both together with Jupiter. unverified draft

'per appropriationem' ('by appropriation') is a scholastic term for attributing a shared effect to one cause by special fittingness; 'praedictis uitis' ('the aforesaid lives') refers back to the planetary 'lives' assigned to the soul in the immediately preceding theses (farmer1998:22335).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch15, from Latin at farmer1998:22349

Attribution: Pico's own opinion, in the doctrine of Plato (section 5>, farmer1998:22132). · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

The thesis extends 5>9-11 by making the sun and moon 'universal causes' cooperating with the planetary 'lives' already assigned, and pairs each 'by appropriation' with two of the other planets: the moon with Mercury and Saturn, the sun with Venus and Mars, and both together with Jupiter (farmer1998:22349). Farmer reads the sun and moon here as figures for the intellect and its lower reflection in reason, within a set of theses, 5>9-12, that involves no predictive or horoscopal astrology (farmer1998:22372).
