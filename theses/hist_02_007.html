Latin

Licet dei uoluntas consequens semper impleatur, non tamen necessitatem rebus uolitis generaliter imponit.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 11328

English

Although God's consequent will is always fulfilled, it does not, however, impose necessity on willed things in general. unverified draft

'uoluntas consequens' (consequent will) is the scholastic term for God's will as it bears on things all considered together, contrasted with his antecedent will toward each thing considered in itself, per Edelheit's citation of the parallel Thomistic text (edelheit2022:10386).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch2, from Latin at farmer1998:11328

Attribution: Reports Thomas Aquinas (farmer1998:11328). · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

The thesis holds that although God's consequent will is always fulfilled, it does not thereby impose necessity on willed things in general (farmer1998:11328). Edelheit reports that in thesis 7 the will of God will always be fulfilled, yet it does not impose in a general fashion necessity upon things willed (res volitae), and continues his discussion from thesis 6's citation of Thomas's Summa theologiae, 1, question 23, article 5 (edelheit2022:10386). Farmer groups this thesis with 2.2-9 under the divine omnipotence/free will problem (farmer1998:11348).
