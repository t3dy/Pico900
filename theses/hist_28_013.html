Latin

Qui nouerit in Cabala mysterium portarum intelligentiae, cognoscet mysterium magni lobelei.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 17741

Farmer's OCR prints 'lobelei'; Wirszubski's and Black's transcriptions of the same thesis give 'Iobelei' (wirszubski1989:1836; black2006:11922), the reading followed in translation below.

English

Whoever will have known, in the Kabbalah, the mystery of the gates of understanding, will know the mystery of the great Jubilee. unverified draft

'portarum intelligentiae' ('gates of understanding') is, per Farmer's note, a standard symbol for the third sefirah, Binah ('intelligence'); 'magni Iobelei' ('the great Jubilee') is the eschatological return of the world to Binah, 'mother of the world' (farmer1998:17821, citing Scholem 1974: 336).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-swarm-batch5, from Latin at farmer1998:17741

Attribution: Reports Hebrew Cabalists (farmer1998:17741). · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

Farmer glosses 'gates of intelligence' as a standard symbol for the third sefirah, Binah, and 'great jubilee' as the eschatological return of the world to Binah, 'mother of the world', citing Scholem (farmer1998:17821). He cross-references the theme to 11>68-69, on the 'denarius' in formal arithmetic and 'the secret of fifty gates of intelligence' (farmer1998:17821, 27921, 27927).

unverified draft

Historiography

Black quotes the thesis and glosses Mithridates' rendering, writing that 'Numeratio commonly means "counting"' and that he left it untranslated 'because Mithridates uses it to translate the Hebrew term seﬁrah' (black2006:11922). Wirszubski designates the thesis 'CONCLUSIO XIII' and begins a discussion of 'the notion that the year of jubilee is a...' that is cut off in the material consulted (wirszubski1989:1836).
