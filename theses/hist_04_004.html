Doctrine

The thesis asserts that Christ, in uniting two natures in one Person, possessed 'two esse' -- two acts of existing (farmer1998:12153). Farmer's note explains the underlying problem: since Christ united two distinct natures or essences in a single Person, the question was whether he thereby possessed two acts of existing as well as two natures, and records that Pico planned to reconcile the Scotist and Thomist positions on this point in the separate thesis 1>5 (farmer1998:12220). Edelheit locates the discussion in Scotus's Ordinatio 3, distinction 6, question 1, where 'Scotus distinguishes between duo esse and duo entia and between human and divine existence' (edelheit2022:13918).

Context

Farmer notes that some indication of Pico's own approach to reconciling Scotus and Thomas on this question can be gathered from the Apology, in the Opera edition at page 187 (farmer1998:12220).

Historiography

Edelheit identifies the Scotus passage as Ordinatio 3, distinction 6, question 1 (Utrum in Christo sit aliquod esse aliud ab esse increato), noting Scotus's distinction there between duo esse and duo entia, and states this identification was 'indicated by Farmer' (edelheit2022:13918, 13931).
