Animae a tertio lumine ad quartam diem, et inde ad quintam descendunt, inde exeuntes corporis noctem subintrant.

English

Souls descend from the third light to the fourth day, and from there to the fifth; departing from there, they enter the night of the body. unverified draft

Doctrine

The thesis maps the soul's descent onto a sequence of 'light' and 'day', terms that in medieval Hebrew sources together symbolize the superior three and inferior seven sefirot; the 'third light' corresponds to Binah, 'intelligence', the sefirah Pico most often associates with the intellectual nature (farmer1998:17706). Farmer records Wirszubski's argument, built on Mithridates' translations, that Pico idiosyncratically identifies the 'fifth day' of this thesis with the tenth sefirah, and directs comparison to theses 28.6 and 28.41 on the emanation of souls (farmer1998:17706).

Historiography

A study of Pico's Kabbalistic epistemology quotes this conclusion in full and connects it to Wirszubski's discussion, citing Menahem Recanati's Pentateuch commentary and Archangelus's Interpretationes in dogmata as further points of reference (kabbalah_paradigma:1515). Wirszubski quotes the same Latin as the eighth conclusion of the Cabalistic set (wirszubski1989:1529, 1532).
