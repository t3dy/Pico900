Thesis 11>67

Latin

Per dictum Cabalistarum quod Caeli sunt ex igne et aqua, simul et ueritatem theologicam de ipsis sephirot nobis manifestat, et philosophicam ueritatem quod elementa in caelo sint tantum secundum actiuam uirtutem.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 27844; 1486 print folio <34v/35r>

The sheet prints a full stop mid-clause, 'simul et ueritatem. Theologicam', which breaks the sentence; read here as continuous, 'simul et ueritatem theologicam', since the following clause has no separate subject of its own.

English

The Cabalists' saying that the heavens are made of fire and water shows us, at once, the theological truth about the sefirot themselves and the philosophical truth that the elements are present in heaven only in their active power.

Pico explains the etymology behind this thesis in the Heptaplus, deriving 'shamayim' (heaven) from 'esh' (fire) and 'mayim' (water); Farmer's note assigns the theological sense to the sixth sefirah, which unites the fifth (fire) and fourth (water) (farmer1998:27902).
