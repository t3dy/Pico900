Latin

Cum Platonem audimus Palladem et amorem philosophos deos uocantem, ita intelligamus, ut amor sit philosophus ratione uiae, Pallas ratione termini.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 22358

English

When we hear Plato calling Pallas and Love 'philosopher gods,' let us understand it thus: that Love is a philosopher by reason of the way, Pallas by reason of the end. unverified draft

'ratione uiae' / 'ratione termini' ('by reason of the way' / 'by reason of the end/terminus') is a scholastic distinction between a process and its goal; the thesis is discussed by Farmer as part of the series he begins at thesis 2.12 (farmer1998:22425).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch15, from Latin at farmer1998:22358

Attribution: Pico's own opinion, in the doctrine of Plato (section 5>, farmer1998:22132). · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

The thesis glosses Plato's naming of Pallas and Love as 'philosopher gods': Love is a philosopher by reason of the way, the process toward wisdom, Pallas by reason of the end or goal (farmer1998:22358). Farmer's note places the thesis in the same series he begins discussing at thesis 2.12 and states that Pallas recurs elsewhere in Pico as a symbol, though the note is truncated in the material consulted before it names of what (farmer1998:22425).

Historiography

Busi and Ebgi print the Latin and an Italian translation of the thesis (busi2014:17341, busi2014:24858), and separately note that Pico's linking of Pallas with philosophy recurs elsewhere in his work, in a discussion of Proverbs 27:11 and a saying they attribute to the Babylonian Talmud, tractate Ta'anit 7a (busi2014:17528).
