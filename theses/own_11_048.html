Thesis 11>48

Work in progress. This entry is a draft awaiting verification: every claim carries a locator, but no second reader has yet re-found each one.

Latin

Quicquid dicant caeteri cabalistae, ego decem spheras sic decem numerationibus correspondere dico, ut ab aedificio incipiendo, Iupiter sit quartae, Mars quintae, Sol sextae, Saturnus septimae, Venus octauae, Mercurius nonae, Luna decimae; tum supra aedificium, firmamentum tertiae, Primum mobile secundae, caelum empyreum decimae.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 27423

Farmer's own English marks the terminal clause '[sic]' ("the empyrean heaven to the tenth [sic]", farmer1998:27474), signalling an apparent inconsistency (the tenth numeration has already been assigned to the moon). Akopyan's transcription of the same clause reads 'celum Empyreum prima' (the empyrean heaven corresponds to the first) rather than 'decimae' (akopyan2018:743; akopyan2021:1455), which would resolve the inconsistency, but this variant reading is not confirmed against Farmer's own edition apparatus.

English

Whatever the other Cabalists say, I hold that the ten spheres correspond to the ten numerations in this way: starting from the edifice, Jupiter to the fourth, Mars to the fifth, the Sun to the sixth, Saturn to the seventh, Venus to the eighth, Mercury to the ninth, the Moon to the tenth; then, above the edifice, the firmament to the third, the Primum Mobile to the second, and the empyrean heaven to the tenth. unverified draft

'numerationibus' = the sefirot. 'aedificio'/'aedificium' ('the edifice') is a Kabbalistic term Farmer glosses as 'the fourth through tenth sefirot' (paraphrase; farmer1998:27453, cross-referring to 28.9). The terminal 'caelum empyreum decimae' is translated as printed in the packet's Farmer-sourced Latin (farmer1998:27423), though see latin_note on Akopyan's variant reading (akopyan2018:743; akopyan2021:1455).

Doctrine

Thesis 11>48 correlates the ten celestial spheres of Ptolemaic cosmology with the ten sefirot, against 'whatever the other Cabalists say' (farmer1998:27423). Farmer's note glosses 'numerations' as the sefirot and 'edifice' as the fourth through tenth sefirot (paraphrase; farmer1998:27453), and reports that Wirszubski (1989) read Pico's 'inferior chariot' of the companion thesis 11>50 as 'a symbol of a lower triad of the ten sefirot' and 'an example of magical use of the sefirot system', while Farmer counters that Pico's own thesis 11>22 (printed in the source OCR as '1122', per this packet's stated digit convention for '>') defines the 'inferior chariot' as the realm of 'sensible natures' (paraphrase; farmer1998:27453).

Context

Farmer's cross-reference apparatus ties the thesis to 11>2, Pico's fourfold division of speculative Kabbala (farmer1998:26383); to 11>49, on the correspondence of the ten commandments to the numerations (farmer1998:27430); to 11>50, on the 'inferior chariot' (farmer1998:27439); and to 28.9 and 28.22 on the 'edifice' and the chariots (farmer1998:17649, 17959).

Reception

Dougherty groups the thesis under a specific rubric: "Among the Kabbalistic theses with magical themes are four that dealt with astrology" (dougherty2008:7890), of which 11>48 is the first cited. Akopyan quotes the thesis's correspondence scheme in both of his studies of Pico and astrology (akopyan2018:743; akopyan2021:1455), and Howlett and Busi and Ebgi likewise quote its Latin in full (howlett2021:7774; busi2014:24617). A German study of Pico's kabbalistic paradigm pairs the thesis with 11>66 as together illustrating the linkage of soul-faculties and planetary spheres to the sefirotic pattern (paraphrase; kabbalah_paradigma:1566).

Historiography

Dougherty situates 11>48 within a set of four astrological-Kabbalistic theses with 'magical themes' (dougherty2008:7890). Akopyan's two transcriptions of the thesis's final clause (akopyan2018:743; akopyan2021:1455) diverge from the Farmer-sourced text used here (farmer1998:27423), a divergence not resolved in the sources consulted.
