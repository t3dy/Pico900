Thesis 6.6

Latin

Ideo angelus est obstinatus et inpenitens, quia subtracti sunt ei diuini impetus speciales.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 12689

English

Therefore the angel is obstinate and unrepentant, because special divine impulses have been withdrawn from it. unverified draft

'impetus speciales' ('special impulses') names the particular divine motions of the will whose withdrawal, on this account, fixes the fallen angel in its obstinacy; 'obstinatus et inpenitens' is the standard scholastic pairing for the fixed, unchangeable will of a fallen angel (farmer1998:12689).

Attribution: Reports Giles of Rome (farmer1998:12689). · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

The thesis draws its conclusion ('ideo', therefore) from the immediately preceding 6.5 ('Angeli non fuerunt creati in gratia', farmer1998:12686). Farmer's note groups 6.5-6 together as 'again anti-Thomistic', citing Thomas, Summa Ia, question 62 article 3, and Ia, question 64 article 2, as the position opposed (paraphrase, farmer1998:12768). Edelheit states that 'Thesis 6 is directly connected to the previous thesis through the adverb "therefore" (ideo)' and glosses it: 'an angel is obstinate and impenitent since divine and special powers were taken from it' (edelheit2022:16567), connecting it to an earlier discussion of the possibility of removing grace from angels by God's absolute power (paraphrase, edelheit2022:16567).

Context

Edelheit divides the eleven Giles theses into a first group, theses 1-7, on theological issues including angelology, and a second, theses 8-11, on natural philosophy (edelheit2022:15967). He reports that theses 5, 6 and 7 of this set 'are included in a recent English translation of selections from Pico's 900 Theses', in Copenhaver's *Magic and the Dignity of Man* (edelheit2022:16386); that translation is in copyright and is not reproduced here.

Historiography

Edelheit calls thesis 6 'almost deduced' from thesis 5, noting that 'while thesis 5 opens the whole discussion of whether angels were created in grace, a complicated issue which cannot be reflected in a short thesis, thesis 6 brings into relief Pico's unique formulation' (edelheit2022:16988). He quotes the Latin of 6.6 verbatim (edelheit2022:16588).
