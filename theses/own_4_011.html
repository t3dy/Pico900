Latin

Si teneatur communis uia quod actu intellectus attingatur deus, dico duas sequentes conclusiones, quarum haec est prima: quod uidentes uerbum, eo actu quo essentiam diuinam attingunt, creaturas non attingunt nisi eminenter, equipollenter ad formalem cognitionem, equipollentia non actus sed obiecti.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 21670

Apparatus (Farmer): 1486 cognitionem. Equipollentia

1486 punctuates 'cognitionem. Equipollentia'.

English

Granting the common view that God is reached by an act of intellect, I put forward two conclusions, the first being this: those who see the Word attain created things, by that same act by which they attain the divine essence, only in an elevated manner equivalent to formal knowledge of them — an equivalence of object, not of act. unverified draft

Argued conditionally from within the 'common view' Pico elsewhere departs from (cf. 3>43); paired with 4>12 as its second conclusion.
