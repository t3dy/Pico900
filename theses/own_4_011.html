Thesis 4>11

Latin

Si teneatur communis uia quod actu intellectus attingatur deus, dico duas sequentes conclusiones, quarum haec est prima: quod uidentes uerbum, eo actu quo essentiam diuinam attingunt, creaturas non attingunt nisi eminenter, equipollenter ad formalem cognitionem, equipollentia non actus sed obiecti.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 21670

Apparatus (Farmer): 1486 cognitionem. Equipollentia

The 1486 print places a full stop after 'cognitionem', before 'Equipollentia' (farmer1998:21686).

English

If the common way is held, that God is attained by an act of intellect, I state the two following conclusions, of which this is the first: that those who see the Word, by the very act by which they attain the divine essence, attain creatures only eminently, equipollently as to formal cognition, an equipollence not of act but of object. unverified draft

'Si teneatur' frames what follows as conditional on granting the common view, not as Pico's own premise; Farmer's note states that Pico himself did not hold the common way of attaining God described here, which for him transcended an ordinary act of intellect (farmer1998:21754).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch14, from Latin at farmer1998:21670

Attribution: Pico's own opinion, cast hypothetically ('si teneatur communis uia') rather than as an assertion of the common view itself (farmer1998:21670; farmer1998:21754). · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

Granting for argument's sake the common view that an act of intellect attains God, Pico draws a first conclusion about the beatific vision: those who see the Word attain other creatures only 'eminently', with an equipollence of object rather than of act, through the very act by which they attain the divine essence (farmer1998:21670); Black quotes the thesis's opening clause in a discussion of Pico's theory of beatific cognition, printing it alongside a thesis from the Plotinus conclusions and alongside 4>24-25, the pair of theses immediately following this one's companion conclusion (black2006:10029).

Historiography

An unauthored German-language study of Pico's Kabbalistic epistemology groups this thesis with 3>42, on the infinity of God knowable only 'through the way of mystical theology', and notes parallel formulations Pico gives elsewhere under Averroes (7.1), Proclus (24.44), and Plotinus (7.3, 20.7) (kabbalah_paradigma:1756).
