Latin

Qui dixerit personas in diuinis absolutis proprietatibus distingui, catholicae ueritati non repugnabit.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 12149; 1486 print folio <2v/3r>

English

Whoever says that the Persons are distinguished in God by absolute properties will not be at odds with Catholic truth. unverified draft

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch5, from Latin at farmer1998:12149

Attribution: Reports Duns Scotus, third of the theses drawn from his work, part of a series beginning at 2.1 (farmer1998:12135, 12214). · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

The thesis holds that it is compatible with Catholic truth to say the divine Persons are distinguished by 'absolute properties' rather than by relations alone (edelheit2022:13850). Farmer's note observes that the phrase in divinis is best rendered simply 'in God,' and places the thesis in a series on Trinitarian distinction beginning at 2.1 (farmer1998:12214).

unverified draft

Context

Edelheit calls the thesis 'crucial for understanding one of Pico's main lines of argumentation in the Apology,' since it establishes that a view unsupported by an explicit authority is not thereby shown false: 'it is clear that this issue is not regarded negatively by authority' (edelheit2022:13910).

unverified draft

Historiography

Edelheit locates the source discussion in Scotus's Ordinatio 1, distinction 8, part 1, question 4, on the tension between divine simplicity and the distinction of Persons (edelheit2022:13850, 13874), a passage he says Farmer had already indicated (edelheit2022:13874).
