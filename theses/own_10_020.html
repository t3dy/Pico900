Latin

Per septennarium hymnorum paternae menti attributorum, Protogoni, Palladis, Saturni, Veneris, Rheae, Legis, Bacchi, potest intelligens et profundus contemplator de saeculi consumatione aliquid coniectare.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 25999

Apparatus (Farmer): 1486 Veneris | 1487 generis

The 1486 apparatus reads 'Veneris' where 1487 reads 'generis' (farmer1998:26023); the Latin above follows Farmer's printed (1486) reading.

English

By means of the sevenfold set of hymns attributed to the paternal mind — Protogonus, Pallas, Saturn, Venus, Rhea, the Law, Bacchus — an intelligent and profound contemplator can conjecture something about the consummation of the age. unverified draft

'saeculi consumatione' is rendered 'the consummation of the age', matching the theme of thesis 7a>38 on the same topic (farmer1998:24257).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch18, from Latin at farmer1998:25999

Attribution: Pico's own opinion. · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

The thesis correlates seven Orphic hymns, each attributed to a divine name, with a means of conjecturing about the end of the age (farmer1998:25999). Farmer's note states that Pico evidently intended to correlate the properties of each of the seven gods named with one of seven historical ages, ending in an age of mystic frenzy associated with Bacchus, and that there were many methods of numerological prophecy in the Kabbalah that Pico planned to demonstrate (farmer1998:26035).

unverified draft

Context

The thesis belongs to the Orphic Hymns own-opinion series (farmer1998:25560) and is directly continued by the next thesis, which states that none of the effect of these seven hymns is achieved without the work of the Cabala (farmer1998:26004). Farmer cross-references thesis 7a>38, among the numerical questions, which likewise asks 'when the consummation of the age will be' (farmer1998:24257).
