Thesis 10>8

Latin

Qui profunde et intellectualiter diuisionem unitatis uenereae in trinitatem gratiarum, et unitatis fatalis in trinitatem parcarum, et unitatis Saturniae in trinitatem Iouis, Neptunni, et Plutonis, intellexerit, uidebit modum debite procedendi in orphica theologia.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 25773

English

Whoever understands profoundly and intellectually the division of the unity of Venus into the trinity of the Graces, and of the unity of Fate into the trinity of the Fates, and of the Saturnian unity into the trinity of Jupiter, Neptune, and Pluto, will see the way of proceeding correctly in Orphic theology. unverified draft

Farmer's edition marks this thesis as the 805th in his cumulative numbering of the whole collection (farmer1998:25827).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch17, from Latin at farmer1998:25773

Attribution: Pico's own opinion, section 10> (Orphic Hymns) (farmer1998:25773). · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

Farmer's note glosses the 'method of duly proceeding in Orphic theology' as an operation of splitting monadic unities into triads, but the note excerpted breaks off before completing the explanation (farmer1998:25803).

Historiography

Busi and Ebgi quote the thesis's Latin in full from their edition, Conclusiones, page 508 (their note on thesis 10>8) (busi2014:24950), and their index records the thesis discussed at several further points in connection with the Fates and Saturn (busi2014:3941; busi2014:19045).
