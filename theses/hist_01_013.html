Latin

Licet ad receptionem speciei concurrat sensus passiue, ad iudicandum tamen de sensibili actiue concurrit.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 11149

English

Although sense concurs passively in receiving the species, it concurs actively, however, in judging the sensible object. unverified draft

'species' is the sensible form received by the sense organ; the active/passive contrast is Albert the Great's, per Farmer's note on this thesis (farmer1998:11214).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch2, from Latin at farmer1998:11149

Attribution: Reports Albert the Great (farmer1998:11149). · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

The thesis distinguishes two ways sense is said to concur in perception: passively in receiving a sensible species, and actively in judging the sensible object (farmer1998:11149). Edelheit reports that in thesis 13 Pico holds that sense concurs with the retention of species in a passive manner but concurs with judgment of a sensible object in an active manner, and traces this to Albert's commentary on De anima, Book 2, Tract 4, Chapter 2 (edelheit2022:9874). Farmer's note glosses the doctrine as Albert's view that every sense combines an active and a passive element, a duality Farmer says Pico found appealing for its symmetry with the structure of higher cognitive powers (farmer1998:11214).

Context

Farmer's note groups thesis 1.13 with related historical theses, some in conflict with it, at 8.10, 10.3, 17.1 and 17.7, and links it to Pico's own-opinion thesis 2>1 on the direct abstraction of inferior universals by the senses (farmer1998:11214).
