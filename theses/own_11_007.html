Thesis 11>7

Latin

Nullus hebraeus cabalista potest negare quod nomen Iesu, si eum secundum modum et principia cabalae interpretemur, hoc totum precise_et nihil aliud significat, id est, deum dei filium patrisque sapientiam per tertiam diuinitatis personam, quae est ardentissimus amoris ignis, naturae humanae in unitate suppositi unitum.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 26493

English

No Hebrew Cabalist can deny that the name of Jesus, if we interpret it according to the method and principles of the Cabala, signifies precisely this and nothing else: namely, God, the Son of God, and the Father's Wisdom, united to human nature in the unity of a supposit through the third Person of the divinity, who is the most burning fire of love. unverified draft

'suppositi' (a supposit): the scholastic term for the concrete individual subject in which a nature subsists, here applied to the hypostatic union of the divine and human natures in Christ; Copenhaver notes that the thesis's closing phrase, naturae humanae in unitate suppositi unitum, ‘expressed standard Christological metaphysics and trinitarian theology’ (copenhaver2022:4950).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-swarm-batch2, from Latin at farmer1998:26493

Attribution: Pico's own opinion (farmer1998:26493). · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

Farmer's note observes that Pico probably meant to generate part of the thesis's wording through a Hebrew letter-based technique, and cross-references 11>14 on the letters of the name Jesus (farmer1998:26574). Copenhaver identifies three Hebrew letters in the sacred name Yeshu as part of what, in Pico's argument, ‘convinced Pico that Jews had to accept the Trinity’ (copenhaver2022:4950). The thesis's closing formula applies to Christ the standard scholastic vocabulary of the hypostatic union, in which a suppositum is the concrete subject in which a nature subsists (copenhaver2022:4950).

Context

The following thesis, 11>8, draws an explicit exegetical consequence from this one, reading Paul's language of a name given to Jesus ‘above every name’ as itself Cabalistic (farmer1998:26500). Thesis 11>15 goes on to argue that the ineffable name said by the Cabalists to be the future name of the Messiah shows he will be made man through the Holy Spirit (farmer1998:26703).

Historiography

Wirszubski counts this thesis, together with the sixty-first, among ‘several theses which clearly presuppose the identification of the Son with Wisdom,’ quoting its phrase ‘deum dei filium patrisque sapientiam’ alongside the parallel formula of the sixty-first thesis (wirszubski1989:8544); he treats it, with the Cabalistic theses generally, in his chapter on the Trinity (wirszubski1989:8206). The study cited here as kabbalah_paradigma and the collection cited as knots_spirals both cite or translate the thesis without argument beyond identifying it and giving its Latin or an English rendering (kabbalah_paradigma:1938; knots_spirals:267).
