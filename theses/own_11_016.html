Latin

Ex mysterio trium litterarum quae sunt in dictione sciabat, id est, <NAW>, possumus interpretari cabalistice tunc sabbatiza/re mundum cum dei filius fit homo, et ultimo futurum sabbatum cum homines in dei filium regenerabuntur.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 26709; 1486 print folio <31v/32r>

'sabbatiza/re': OCR line break. Farmer's bracketed transliteration <NAW> (farmer1998:26709) differs from the form <I''OW> given in Wirszubski's transcription of the same clause (wirszubski1989:8407); which Hebrew letters either represents is not established by the sources consulted.

English

From the mystery of the three letters that are in the word 'shabbat' — that is, [bracketed letters, reading uncertain] — we can interpret, in Kabbalistic fashion, that the world then keeps sabbath when the Son of God becomes man, and that the last sabbath will come when men are regenerated into the Son of God. unverified draft

'sabbatizare' is glossed by Farmer as 'to rest' (farmer1998:26777). The bracketed transliteration in the Latin is left unrendered because the two witnesses consulted (Farmer's <NAW>, farmer1998:26709; Wirszubski's <I''OW>, wirszubski1989:8407) disagree, and neither has been checked here against a manuscript or the 1486 print.

Doctrine

Farmer glosses 'shabbat' as 'Sabbath' and 'sabbatizare' as 'to rest', and states that 'Pico's precise methods here are again unknown, although there is presumably a connection with 11>14 via the mystical symbolism of the letter shin' (farmer1998:26777). He cross-references the thesis to 11>14, on the letter shin mediating in the name Jesus, and to 11>17, on 'the purest wine' among the Cabalists, as well as to 5>7 (Plato's Timaeus) and 8>6 (the Chaldean interpreters on 'double drunkenness') (farmer1998:26697, 26715, 22237, 24842).

Historiography

Wirszubski designates the thesis 'Conclusio xvi' and reads it, with the surrounding theses, as carrying 'eminently Christian connotations,' remarking that 'Ab' and 'ben' were too obvious for words but that sabbat needed explaining, before he quotes the Latin (wirszubski1989:8407). A study of Pico's epistemology and the Kabbalah cross-lists 11>16 with 28.13, 11>45, and 23.8 (from Iamblichus) as related instances, citing Greive's Die christliche Kabbalah (kabbalah_paradigma:2167-2168).
