Thesis 6.8

Latin

Sensus gustus, ut gustus est, non solum saporabile, sed humidum percipit.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 12781

English

The sense of taste, as taste, perceives not only what is flavourable, but also what is moist. unverified draft

'saporabile' is the proper Aristotelian sensible quality of taste (what is capable of being tasted, i.e. flavour); the thesis claims taste also perceives an additional, non-proper quality, moisture (farmer1998:12781).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-swarm-batch6, from Latin at farmer1998:12781

Attribution: Reports Giles of Rome (farmer1998:12781). · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

Edelheit places thesis 8 among the last four Giles theses, 'focused on natural philosophy (theses 8, 9, and 11), and on the subordination of sciences (thesis 10)' (edelheit2022:16745), stating: 'According to thesis 8 the sense of taste, since it is a taste, perceives not only that which has flavour, but also that which has moisture' (edelheit2022:16745). He quotes Giles's own discussion, from *Opera omnia* III.2, page 377, of how someone whose taste is impaired ('infectum') judges bitter and sweet differently, and sometimes the reverse (paraphrase of the Latin quoted, edelheit2022:16763).

Historiography

Edelheit calls the attribution to Giles of 'a very standard Aristotelian view' regarding taste 'difficult to explain' as distinctively Giles's own, drawing a parallel with thesis 11, on movement in a vacuum, which he says is 'obviously taken from Averroes commenting on Aristotle's Physics' (paraphrase, edelheit2022:16993).
