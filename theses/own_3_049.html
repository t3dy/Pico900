Latin

Magis improprie dicitur de deo quod sit intellectus uel intelligens, quam de anima rationali quod sit angelus.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 20990

Edelheit transcribes the same conclusion's title, from the source he consulted, with subject and predicate reversed: 'Magis improprie dicitur de Deo quod sit intelligens vel intellectus quam de angelo quod sit anima rationalis' -- 'than of the angel that it is a rational soul,' rather than Farmer's 'than of the rational soul that it is an angel' (edelheit2022:20783; farmer1998:20990).

English

It is said more improperly of God that he is intellect or understanding, than of the rational soul that it is an angel. unverified draft

The thesis is comparative.

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-swarm-batch1, from Latin at farmer1998:20990

Attribution: Pico's own opinion (farmer1998:20990). · Pico's stance: endorses

Condemnation

Q12 in the order of the Apology: Whether God understands. Printed 7 December 1486; censured by the papal commission in February-March 1487; the work as a whole condemned by the bull of 4 August 1487.

"false and can be taken to a heretical sense" (farmer1998:21054); Dougherty's paraphrase, "Wrong and can be twisted to a heretical meaning" (dougherty2008:2245).

unverified draft

Doctrine

Farmer records that Pico planned to demonstrate the thesis through the 'way of numbers,' cross-referencing two of the numerical theses, 7a>5 ('Whether he is understanding') and 7a>6 ('How God understands') (farmer1998:21054; farmer1998:23828; farmer1998:23831). In his formal reply to the commission, Pico defended the thesis as conforming to the 'mode of speaking of Dionysius' (Dionysius's negative way), who claimed that it should not be said of God that he is either 'intellect' or the 'intelligible' or similar things (farmer1998:21054).

unverified draft

Context

Farmer cross-references the thesis to two theses among the 'questions to be answered by numbers': 7a>5, asking whether God is understanding (Vtrum sit intelligens), and 7a>6, asking how God understands (Quomodo deus intelligat) (farmer1998:23828; farmer1998:23831).

unverified draft

Reception

The papal commission found the thesis "false and can be taken to a heretical sense" (farmer1998:21054); Dougherty's summary of the commission's censures records it more tersely as "Wrong and can be twisted to a heretical meaning" (dougherty2008:2245). In the printed Conclusions, before the trial, the thesis stood early among the group later censured: Copenhaver notes that the Q1 thesis on Hell followed, in order, Q12 (this thesis), Q13, Q9, and Q6 (copenhaver2022:1253).

unverified draft

Historiography

Beyond quoting the Latin opening of the conclusion, Howlett lists the thesis among the thirteen censured propositions without further discussion (howlett2021:2029). An unidentified German-language study on Pico's Kabbalah cites the thesis alongside 22.12 ('Conclusio secundum Porphyrium') and a thesis on the doctrine of Hermes Trismegistus, without stating an argument connecting them beyond their proximity in Pico's own text (kabbalah_paradigma:1395).
