Latin

Fatum non est necessitas primae potentiae seminalis, sed est intellectualiter participata habitudo animalium rationum, indeclinans a superioribus, ineuitabilis ab inferioribus.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 17129

English

Fate is not the necessity of the primary seminal potency, but is a condition of rational living things, shared in intellectually, that does not swerve away from what is above and cannot be evaded by what is below. unverified draft

Rendered directly from the Latin at farmer1998:17129. Busi and Ebgi's Italian gloss agrees in sense with this reading (busi2014:19145).

Attribution: the Chaldean theologians · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

The thesis distinguishes fate as the necessity of a first seminal potency from fate as a condition of rational living things, received intellectually and binding without exception on what stands below it (farmer1998:17129). Farmer's note directs comparison to thesis 24.2, which sets out different legal orders governing different classes of soul, as the parallel for this thesis's hierarchy of necessity (farmer1998:17214).

Historiography

Busi and Ebgi print the Latin and note its resemblance to a fragment of the Chaldean Oracles in George Gemistus Pletho's recension, reading the thesis against Pico's own discussion of fate in the Commento (busi2014:19145).
