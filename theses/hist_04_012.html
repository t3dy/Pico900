The body of Christ was, of itself, incapable of suffering. unverified draft

'Impassibile': incapable of suffering or undergoing change, a technical term in the theology of Christ's body and of the resurrection (farmer1998:12255).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch6, from Latin at farmer1998:12255

Attribution: Reports Scotus (farmer1998:12255). · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

The thesis concerns the impassibility of Christ's body considered in itself, apart from the Passion (farmer1998:12255). Farmer directs the reader to thesis 2.15, on the impassibility of glorified bodies after the resurrection ('Impassibilitas corporum post resurrectionem erit ex pleno dominio animae super corpus', farmer1998:11433), and to its accompanying note (farmer1998:12341). Edelheit states that thesis 12 holds the body of Christ to have been in itself incapable of suffering, and identifies the discussion in Scotus's Ordinatio, book 4, distinction 49, part 2, question unica, titled 'whether the body of the blessed person after resurrection will be incapable of suffering' (edelheit2022:14473).

Historiography

Edelheit's identification of the Ordinatio question, and his naming of its formal title, are the only treatment of thesis 12 among the sources consulted (edelheit2022:14473).
