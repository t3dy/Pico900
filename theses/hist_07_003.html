Apparatus (Farmer): 1486 Johanes de Gandago | 1487 Johannes de gandago

English

The ultimate happiness of man is when the agent intellect is conjoined to the possible [intellect] as its form; and this conjunction the other Latins whom I have read, and especially John of Jandun, understood perversely and erroneously -- he who corrupted and utterly depraved the doctrine of Averroes, not only on this point but in almost all the questions of philosophy. unverified draft

'Iohanes de Gandauo' is John of Jandun (d. 1328), the Latin Averroist commentator; the 1487 printing varies the spelling to 'Iohannes de gandago' (farmer1998:12961), and 'continuatur...ut forma' reflects the Averroist doctrine of the conjunction (continuatio) of the separate agent intellect with the individual's possible intellect as its form (farmer1998:12940).

Doctrine

The thesis states that man's ultimate happiness occurs when the agent intellect is conjoined to the possible intellect as its form, and criticizes the other Latins who have read Averroes on this point, naming John of Jandun as chief among them for corrupting and depraving Averroes' doctrine not only on this point but in almost all the questions of philosophy (farmer1998:12940). Farmer's note groups this thesis with 7.2 and 7.4 under the same problem of the unity of the intellect, cross-referencing Pico's own theses 3>67-69 (farmer1998:12967).
