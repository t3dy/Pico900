Latin

Idem est Typhon apud Orpheum, et Zamael in Cabala.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 25884

English

The Typhon of Orpheus is the same as Zamael in the Cabala. unverified draft

'Zamael' follows Farmer's OCR spelling; Wirszubski's quotation of the same conclusion likewise prints 'Zamael' (wirszubski1989:9977).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch18, from Latin at farmer1998:25884

Attribution: Pico's own opinion. · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

The thesis identifies a figure of Orphic myth, Typhon, with a figure of Kabbalistic angelology, Zamael (farmer1998:25884). Farmer's note glosses Typhon as the hundred-headed monster who rebelled against Zeus, glosses Samael as Satan, and refers the reader to Scholem's discussion of the figure (farmer1998:25919). The thesis belongs to the thirty-one own-opinion conclusions on the Orphic Hymns (farmer1998:25560).

unverified draft

Context

Pico makes the same kind of move in the immediately preceding thesis, equating Night in Orpheus with Ein-Sof in the Cabala (farmer1998:25892), and the pairing of an Orphic term with a Kabbalistic one recurs in the following thesis as well (farmer1998:25895).

Reception

Wirszubski quotes the conclusion verbatim, in Latin, in his study of Pico's encounter with Jewish mysticism (wirszubski1989:9977).
