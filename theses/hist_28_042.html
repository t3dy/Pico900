Latin

Ideo Ioseph ossibus tantum sepultus est et non corpore, quia eius ossa erant uirtutes et militie arboris superioris uocati Sadich influentis ad terram superiorem.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 18305

English

Therefore Joseph was buried in his bones only and not in his body, because his bones were the powers and host of the higher tree called Tzaddik, flowing into the higher earth. unverified draft

'Sadich' transliterates the Hebrew tzaddik ('the just'); Farmer glosses it as an alternate name for the ninth sefirah, correlated with Joseph, and 'superior earth' as a symbol of the tenth sefirah (paraphrase, farmer1998:18377). 'militie' is rendered 'host' (a military body), alongside 'uirtutes', 'powers' (farmer1998:18305).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch12, from Latin at farmer1998:18305

Attribution: Reports Hebrew Cabalists (farmer1998:18305). · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

Farmer's note glosses 'Tzaddik' as 'the just', an alternate name for the ninth sefirah normally correlated with Joseph, and notes that in thesis 11>21 Pico represents 'the just' as a typological symbol for Christ; 'superior earth' names the tenth sefirah (paraphrase, farmer1998:18377).

unverified draft

Historiography

Wirszubski quotes the thesis together with 28.43 as his 'Conclusiones XLII and XLIII' (wirszubski1989:2833, 2836). He places the thesis within Recanati's sequence, stating that 'the source of Conclusio xlii, the first of the second block, is Recanati, fol. 75rb', and that partway between the two blocks Recanati 'discusses transmigration of souls, old souls, and new souls, and quotes part of the passage of the Bahir which is the ultimate source of Pico's thesis' at 28.41 (wirszubski1989:3189).
