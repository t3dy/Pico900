Doctrine

The thesis states, without qualification, that the mathematical sciences are not true sciences (farmer1998:23613). Farmer groups it with 7>2-5 as a set, and identifies the 'moderns' targeted across the set as the fourteenth-century calculatores, Bradwardine, Swineshead, Buridan, and Oresme, who applied mathematics to problems that Aristotelian physics had treated qualitatively (farmer1998:23698).

unverified draft

Context

Farmer states that the traditional claim that Renaissance Platonism helped promote the mathematical physics of the later Scientific Revolution finds little support in these theses, especially in 7>5 (farmer1998:23698).

unverified draft

Historiography

Cassirer cites this thesis together with the following two mathematical conclusions as denying that mathematics is a true science, on the ground that 'a supersensible knowledge, which is alone able to disclose the supersensible nature' of things is distinct from mathematical knowledge (cassirer1942:1133).
