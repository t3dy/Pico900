Thesis 5.1

Latin

Datur lumen superius lumine fidei, in quo Theologi uident ueritates theologicae scientiae.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 12482

English

A light is granted that is higher than the light of faith, in which theologians behold the truths of theological science. unverified draft

'Lumen fidei': the light of faith; the thesis posits a further, higher light by which theologians see theological truths, distinct from both natural reason and faith (farmer1998:12482).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch6, from Latin at farmer1998:12482

Attribution: Reports Henry of Ghent (farmer1998:12482). · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

The thesis belongs to a series on illuminationism, the doctrine that some truths are known by a special divine light rather than by natural reason or the light of faith alone; Farmer cross-references theses 6.7 and 7.1 and refers as well to thesis 10.1, before the note is cut off in the material consulted (farmer1998:12524). Edelheit states that the thesis is 'basically present' in Henry of Ghent's Summa, article 6, question 1, where Henry distinguishes matters held by belief through faith from other matters known differently by theologians (edelheit2022:15243).

Historiography

Edelheit's identification of Henry's Summa article is the only scholarly treatment of this thesis among the sources consulted; he refers the reader to his own earlier study of Henry's reception by Pico for further discussion, without reproducing its findings here (edelheit2022:15260).
