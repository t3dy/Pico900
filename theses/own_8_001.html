Latin

Quod dicunt interpretes chaldei super primum dictum Zoroastris, de scala a Tartaro ad primum ignem, nihil aliud significat quam seriem naturarum uniuersi a non gradu materiae ad eum qui est super omnem gradum graduate protensum.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 24722

English

What the Chaldean interpreters say about Zoroaster's first saying, concerning the ladder from Tartarus to the first fire, signifies nothing other than the graded series of the natures of the universe, extended from the zero degree of matter up to that which is above every degree. unverified draft

Attribution: Pico's own opinion · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

The conclusion reads a cosmic ladder that the Chaldean interpreters attribute to Zoroaster's first oracle as a figure for the graded series of natures that Pico's own opinion sets out elsewhere among the Platonic theses, extending from the lowest degree of matter to what stands above all degree (farmer1998:24722). Farmer describes the conclusion as Pico reading his own metaphysical system into the symbolic language of the Chaldean Oracles, and compares it especially to 5>15 among the Platonic conclusions (farmer1998:24810).
