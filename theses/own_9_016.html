Latin

Illa natura quae est orizon temporis aeternalis_est proxima mago, sed infra eum.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 25354

English

The nature forming the horizon of eternal time lies near the magus, though below him. unverified draft

'orizon' (horizon) is kept as a technical term marking a threshold nature between eternity and time; the same term recurs in the paired theses 9>17-18, which assign the nature at the corresponding horizon to magic and to Cabala respectively (farmer1998:25358; farmer1998:25362).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch17, from Latin at farmer1998:25354

Attribution: Pico's own opinion, section 9> (Magic) (farmer1998:25354). · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

This is the first of a linked triad of 'horizon' theses: this thesis places a nature at the 'horizon of eternal time' just below the magus, 9>17 assigns to the neighboring 'horizon of time and eternity' nature the discipline of magic, sought 'by the due methods known to the wise' (farmer1998:25358), and 9>18 places a third, superior 'horizon of temporal eternity' nature above the magus, proper to it being Cabala (farmer1998:25362).

Historiography

Mandosio prints all three horizon theses together from Pico's 1998 edition, page 500, and states that Wirszubski 'wrongly interpreted these conclusions,' citing Wirszubski 1989 at page 194, without giving the nature of the error in the material consulted (mandosio2012:559).
