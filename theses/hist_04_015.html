Latin

Per haec uerba precise (Hoc est corpus meum), non expressis uerbis precedentibus, scilicet pridie quam pateretur, non potest consecrari.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 12357

Apparatus (Farmer): 1486 predentibus.scilicet | 1487 precedentibus scilicet

1486 print reads 'predentibus.scilicet'; 1487 print 'precedentibus scilicet' (farmer1998:12387).

English

By these words alone ('This is my body'), the preceding words not being expressed—namely, 'on the day before he suffered'—consecration cannot occur. unverified draft

'Precise': strictly, that is, these words alone and no others; the crux is whether the institution narrative's preceding words are required for a valid consecration (farmer1998:12357).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch6, from Latin at farmer1998:12357

Attribution: Reports Scotus (farmer1998:12357). · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

The thesis addresses the minimal linguistic conditions for a valid Eucharistic consecration: whether pronouncing the words of institution alone, 'Hoc est corpus meum', without the narrative words that precede them in the canon of the Mass ('pridie quam pateretur', on the day before he suffered), is sufficient (farmer1998:12357). Farmer cross-references his note on theses 2.13–14 (on the sacraments) and states that the thesis illustrates 'the word magic in the Eucharist' from a modern perspective (farmer1998:12393). Edelheit locates the underlying discussion in Scotus's Ordinatio, book 4, distinction 8, question 2, and confirms that on Pico's thesis, absent the preceding words, there can be no valid consecration (edelheit2022:14622).

Historiography

Edelheit's identification of the Ordinatio question is the only scholarly treatment of thesis 15 among the sources consulted; he adds that Pico's argumentative strategy for the (planned but unheld) Roman disputation can be reconstructed from the Apology, without citing the Apology's text at this point (edelheit2022:14622).
