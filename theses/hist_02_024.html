Latin

Subiectum et propria passio realiter distinguuntur.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 11650

English

A subject and its proper attribute are really distinct. unverified draft

'propria passio' is a scholastic technical term for a property that, while not part of a thing's essential definition, belongs to it inseparably and exists only in that subject (the standard example being 'snubness' in a nose), per Farmer's note (farmer1998:11682).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-swarm-batch4, from Latin at farmer1998:11650

Attribution: Reports Thomas Aquinas (farmer1998:11650). · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

The thesis states that a subject and its proper attribute are distinguished in reality, not merely in thought (farmer1998:11650). Edelheit finds it in Thomas's commentary on Aristotle's Physics, Book 7, Lecture 5, in a discussion of secondary change (edelheit2022:11635), and links it to Thomas's Summa theologiae I, q. 11, a. 1, where 'unum' is said to add to being only the negation of division (edelheit2022:11662). Farmer's note states that the thesis begins a complex series on the substance/accident distinction, and that on metaphysical grounds Pico elsewhere rejected all distinctions between 'separable' and 'inseparable' accidents (farmer1998:11682).

Context

Edelheit finds an analogy in William of Ockham linking theses 23 and 24: Ockham holds that 'being' and 'one' relate to each other just as, generally, 'quality' (passio) relates to 'subject' (subiectum) (edelheit2022:11733). Edelheit suggests Pico may have had this analogy in mind while composing theses 23-24, but leaves the question open: 'One can only wonder whether Pico had this analogy of William at the back of his mind while working of [sic] theses 23 and 24' (edelheit2022:11733; edelheit2022:11739).

Historiography

Edelheit is the only scholar in the sources consulted to discuss this thesis; he treats it as part of a network of theses (23-25, 27) on the being/one and substance/accident problems drawn from Thomas and, possibly, Ockham (edelheit2022:11733; edelheit2022:11870).

Pico's sources

  • Thomas Aquinas, Commentaria in octo libros Physicorum Aristotelis, Book 7, Lect. 5: Edelheit locates the thesis's doctrine here, in Thomas's discussion of a secondary kind of change (alteration) (edelheit2022:11635)
  • Thomas Aquinas, Summa theologiae I, q. 11, a. 1: 'unum enim nihil aliud significat quam ens indivisum': further Thomistic parallel Edelheit quotes alongside the thesis, on 'one' adding only the negation of division to being (edelheit2022:11662)
