Contingentia ad utrumlibet futurorum cognitorum a deo simul stat cum infallibilitate diuinae scientiae.

English

The contingency, open to either outcome, of future things known by God stands together with the infallibility of divine knowledge. unverified draft

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch2, from Latin at farmer1998:11318

Attribution: Reports Thomas Aquinas (farmer1998:11318). · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

The thesis holds that the contingency of future events known by God coexists with the infallibility of divine knowledge (farmer1998:11318). Edelheit states that thesis 4 emphasizes that the contingent nature, for us, of each future contingency is, from God's perspective, necessary, as a concomitant of the infallibility of divine knowledge, and notes that theses 3-5 originate in the same place in Thomas's Summa (edelheit2022:10328). Farmer groups this thesis with 2.2-9 under the divine omnipotence/free will problem (farmer1998:11348).

unverified draft

Historiography

Edelheit (2022) reads theses 4 and 5 together as restating, from Thomas's discussion in the Summa, 1, question 14, article 13, the compatibility of contingency for the created intellect with necessity for the divine (edelheit2022:10328).
