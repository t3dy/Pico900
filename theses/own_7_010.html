Latin

Ioachin in prophetiis suis alia uia non processit quam per numeros formales.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 23728

English

Joachim, in his prophecies, proceeded by no way other than through formal numbers. unverified draft

'Ioachin' is Joachim of Fiore (farmer1998:23792).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch16, from Latin at farmer1998:23728

Attribution: Pico's own opinion · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

The conclusion assigns Joachim of Fiore's prophetic method entirely to formal number, the same category of number that 7>9 credits with a route to natural prophecy (farmer1998:23724, farmer1998:23728). Farmer identifies 'Ioachin' as Joachim of Fiore and cross-refers to his own earlier discussion of the abbot (farmer1998:23792).

unverified draft

Historiography

John Dee cites this conclusion in his Mathematicall Praeface as authority for his own numerology, in a passage naming 'the noble Earle of Mirandula' as witness that Joachim proceeded by formal numbers alone (quoted in mandosio2012:229). Mandosio treats the passage as a direct early modern use of the conclusion (mandosio2012:229).

Quotations cited

  • “Part of this profound and divine Science, had Joachim the Prophesier atteyned unto: by Numbers Formall, Naturall, and Rationall, forseyng, concludyng, and forshewyng great particular events, long before their comming ... And the noble Earle of Mirandula, (besides that,) a sufficient witnesse: that Joachim, in his prophesies, proceded by no other way, then by Numbers Formall.” — Jean-Marc Mandosio, Beyond Pico della Mirandola: John Dee's formal numbers and real cabala (mandosio2012:229)
