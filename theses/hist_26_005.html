Latin

Coordinatio intelligibilis non est in intellectuali coordinatione ut dixit Amosis aegyptius, sed est super omnem intellectualem hierarchiam in abysso primae unitatis, et sub caligine primarum tenebrarum, inparticipaliter abscondita.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 17143

English

The intelligible ordering is not within the intellectual ordering, as Amosis the Egyptian said, but is hidden, without any participation, above every intellectual hierarchy, in the abyss of the first unity and beneath the darkness of the first shadows. unverified draft

Doctrine

The thesis denies that the 'intelligible ordering' invoked under the name of Amosis the Egyptian belongs to the order of intellectual things, placing it instead above every intellectual hierarchy, hidden in the abyss of the first unity and beneath the darkness of the first shadows (farmer1998:17143). Farmer identifies 'Amosis' as probably Ahmose II, called Amasis II, the ideal priest-king named in the Timaeus, and directs comparison to thesis 11>35 (farmer1998:17222).

Historiography

Busi and Ebgi print the Latin under the heading 'Nox, Tenebrae, Caliga, Ensoph', reading the thesis's imagery of darkness together with the kabbalistic Ein Sof (busi2014:24756).
