Latin

Anima seipsam semper intelligit, et se intelligendo quodammodo omnia entia intelligit.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 21260

English

The soul always understands itself, and in understanding itself understands, in a certain way, all beings. unverified draft

A source cited below (selfknowledge_notes:1302) preserves Pico's own restatement of this thesis in the Apologia with the word order "semper seipsam" rather than the Conclusiones' "seipsam semper"; the sense is unaffected.

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch13, from Latin at farmer1998:21260

Attribution: Pico's own opinion · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

The thesis holds that the soul always understands itself, and in understanding itself understands, in a certain way, all beings (farmer1998:21260).

unverified draft

Context

Dougherty's edited volume reports that Petrus Garsias argued against Pico that if such self-knowledge is called "hidden", it is not actual but latent (dougherty2008:9626).

unverified draft

Reception

A set of research notes titled Self-Knowledge, Illumination and Natural quotes Pico's own restatement of the thesis in the Apologia, introducing it as the conclusion immediately following his discussion there of a hidden, direct, and permanent understanding free of any phantasm or aid of sense or imagination, and states that Edelheit (2011) discusses Henry of Ghent's influence on Pico without addressing this particular passage of the Apologia (selfknowledge_notes:1302).

unverified draft

Historiography

Dougherty's volume and the research notes are the two sources located; between them they place the thesis in a debate over self-knowledge involving Petrus Garsias's fifteenth-century objection and, on the notes' further citations, later scholarship on Henry of Ghent's Quodlibeta IX.5 as a possible source (dougherty2008:9626; selfknowledge_notes:1302).
