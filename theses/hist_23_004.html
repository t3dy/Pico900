Latin

Elementa in octo coeli corporibus coelesti modo bis inueniuntur, quae quis inueniet si retrogrado ordine in illa bina numeratione processerit.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 15651

Farmer's edition additionally reproduces a facsimile transcription of the 1486 print's text of this thesis at farmer1998:10494, in the print's own orthography ('octo cceli corporibus coelefti modo bis inueniuntur...').

English

The elements are found twice, in a celestial manner, among the eight bodies of the heavens — a thing one will discover if one proceeds in reverse order through that twofold numbering. unverified draft

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch9, from Latin at farmer1998:15651

Attribution: Reports Iamblichus (farmer1998:15651). · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

Farmer places the thesis in a series on the caelum (heaven) beginning at thesis 7.9, suggesting it was apparently inspired by Iamblichus's De mysteriis 1.17 (farmer1998:15729).

unverified draft

Context

Farmer's cross-reference network links the thesis to 11>67 on the elements' presence in the heavens according to the Kabbalists (farmer1998:15651, cross-referencing 11>67).

unverified draft

Historiography

Black (2006) proposes the thesis as a possible unmentioned source for a numerological computation elsewhere in the Heptaplus, while explicitly disputing Farmer's suggestion that 'bina' in the phrase 'bina numeratione' alludes to the Kabbalistic sefirah Binah: "I do not think that Farmer's understanding of 'bina' as referring to the sefirah Binah is relevant here" (black2006:1838, referring to Farmer's discussion in Syncretism in the West).
