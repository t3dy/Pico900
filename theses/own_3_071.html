Latin

Empedocles per litem et amicitiam in anima nihil aliud intelligit quam potentiam sursum ductiuam et deorsum ductiuam in ea, quas ego credo proportionari in scientia sephirot aeternitati et decori.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 21360

English

By strife and friendship in the soul, Empedocles understands nothing other than an upward-leading and a downward-leading power within it, which I believe correspond, in the science of the sefirot, to Eternity and Beauty. unverified draft

Farmer's note identifies 'eternity' and 'adornment/beauty' as Pico's ciphers for the seventh and eighth sefirot and directs the reader to his discussion of the corresponding numbering at 11>66 (farmer1998:21419); Farmer's note also refers to Empedocles fragments in Diels's numbering, apparently frr. 16ff., though the reference is not given complete in the material consulted (farmer1998:21419).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch14, from Latin at farmer1998:21360

Attribution: Among the conclusions Pico titles 'paradoxical, according to his own opinion, introducing new teachings in philosophy' (farmer1998:21360). · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

Pico reads Empedocles's cosmic principles of strife and friendship allegorically, as an upward- and a downward-leading power within the soul, and then maps that pair onto two sefirot of the Kabbalistic decad, which he names as Eternity and Beauty/Adornment (farmer1998:21360); Farmer's apparatus cross-references this identification to 11>66, where Pico sets out a fuller, systematic adaptation of the soul's faculties to all ten sefirot: 'Ego animam nostram sic decem sephirot adapto, ut per unitatem suam sit cum prima, per intellectum cum secunda...' [I so adapt our soul to the ten sefirot, that it is with the first through its unity, with the second through intellect...] (farmer1998:27834).

Historiography

Wirszubski's edition of the passage reproduces Pico's Latin with his own interpretive glosses inserted in brackets, identifying 'aeternitati' as the seventh sefirah and 'decori' as the eighth: "quas ego credo proportionari in scientia Sephirot aeternitati [= septimae] et decori [= octavae]" (wirszubski1989:9642); this numbering matches Farmer's independent identification of the same pair of sefirot at the same point (farmer1998:21419).
