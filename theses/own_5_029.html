Thesis 5>29

Latin

Non est credendum in doctrina Platonis animam quicquam intelligere per inspectionem ad ideas, nisi cum peruenit ad illum statum qui est supremus gradus contemplatiuae perfectionis.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 22660

English

It is not to be believed, on Plato's doctrine, that the soul understands anything by looking to the Ideas, except when it has arrived at that state which is the supreme grade of contemplative perfection. unverified draft

An unidentified set of notes on self-knowledge and illumination in Pico's sources quotes the Latin together with an appended corollary denying that everyday cognition uses the light of the Ideas; that corollary's Latin is not present in the inventory record used for this entry's latin field (selfknowledge_notes:1173).

Doctrine

The thesis denies that, on Plato's doctrine, the soul understands anything by looking to the Ideas except when it has reached the supreme grade of contemplative perfection (farmer1998:22660); Farmer records no separate explanatory note on this thesis in the material consulted.
