Thesis 2.34

Latin

Nulla uirtus moralis praeter iustitiam est subiectiue in uoluntate.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 11770

English

No moral virtue other than justice resides in the will as its subject. unverified draft

'subiectiue' means 'as in a subject' in the Aristotelian sense of inherence, not 'subjectively' in a modern psychological sense (farmer1998:11770).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch4, from Latin at farmer1998:11770

Attribution: Reports Thomas Aquinas (farmer1998:11770); opposed, per Farmer, to thesis 4.10 from Scotus (farmer1998:11851). · Pico's stance: reports

Doctrine

The thesis restricts subjective location in the will, among the moral virtues, to justice alone; Edelheit reports that Thomas's Summa theologiae takes up 'whether the will can be the subject of virtue' at 1-2, q.56, a.6, and notes that Pico's term actus entitativus positivus (from the neighboring thesis 33) reflects his own particular emphasis in reading Thomas (edelheit2022:12319).

unverified draft

Context

Farmer records that the thesis stands opposed to 4.10, where Scotus places the virtues generally in the will or 'superior appetite', and links it to thesis 2.11 on the moral and cardinal virtues remaining after the resurrection (farmer1998:11851).

unverified draft

Historiography

Edelheit (2022) reads thesis 34 through Thomas's Summa treatment of justice as the will's proper virtue (edelheit2022:12319); Farmer (1998) frames it as one term of a Thomist/Scotist disagreement over where the moral virtues subjectively reside (farmer1998:11851).
