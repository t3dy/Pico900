Latin

Mathematicae scientiae non sunt propter se, sed ut uia ad alias scientias quaerendae.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 23620

English

The mathematical sciences are not to be sought for their own sake, but as a way toward other sciences. unverified draft

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch16, from Latin at farmer1998:23620

Attribution: Pico's own opinion · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

This is the third of the five mathematical conclusions Pico states as his own opinion (7>1-7>5), a series Farmer connects to the fourteenth-century calculatores, who treated physical questions mathematically where earlier Aristotelian physics had reasoned qualitatively (farmer1998:23698). It states the instrumental, propaedeutic role of mathematics that conclusion 7>4 goes on to specify in terms of mathematical objects functioning as images of higher things (farmer1998:23623).

Historiography

Cassirer cites this conclusion together with 7>1 and 7>2 in his account of Pico's early conviction that sense knowledge cannot disclose the supersensible nature of things (cassirer1942:1133).
