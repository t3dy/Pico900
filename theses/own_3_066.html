Latin

Potest anima per extrinsecam informationem ad hoc deuenire, ut omnia per substantialem suam formam indiuisibiliter intelligat.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 21278

English

The soul can come, by an extrinsic informing, to the point where it understands all things indivisibly through its own substantial form. unverified draft

'Indiuisibiliter': the mode of understanding proper to a substantial form, not the discursive, divisible mode of ordinary cognition; Farmer's note groups this thesis with the surrounding cluster as central to Pico's theory of knowledge (farmer1998:21288).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch14, from Latin at farmer1998:21278

Attribution: Among the conclusions Pico titles 'paradoxical, according to his own opinion, introducing new teachings in philosophy' (kabbalah_paradigma:1324). · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

The thesis holds that the soul, informed from outside itself, can come to understand all things at once and indivisibly through its own substantial form, rather than piecemeal (farmer1998:21278); Farmer's apparatus cross-references it to 3>60, 'Nihil intelligit actu et distincte anima, nisi se ipsam' [the soul understands nothing actually and distinctly, except itself] (farmer1998:21254).

unverified draft

Historiography

Farmer's own note describes this thesis, with its neighbors, as among the key propositions of Pico's theory of knowledge, and points to a fuller discussion elsewhere in his book without giving its content here (farmer1998:21288); an unauthored German-language study cites it together with 3>60, 3>61, and 3>52 as a further group bearing on self-knowledge and the unity of the sciences (kabbalah_paradigma:1695).
