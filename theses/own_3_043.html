Latin

Actus quo foelicitatur natura angelica et rationalis ultima foelicitate, nec est actus intellectus, nec uoluntatis, sed est unio unitatis quae est in alteritate animae cum unitate quae est sine alteritate.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 20885

English

The act by which the angelic and rational nature is made happy with ultimate happiness is neither an act of the intellect nor of the will, but is the union of the unity that is in the otherness of the soul with the unity that is without otherness. unverified draft

"foelicitate"/"foelicitatur" (happiness, made happy) are the classical terms for beatitude; kept as "happiness" rather than the more scholastic "beatitude" to match Pico's own vocabulary (farmer1998:20885).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch13, from Latin at farmer1998:20885

Attribution: Pico's own opinion · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

The thesis holds that the act by which the angelic and rational nature attains ultimate happiness is neither an act of the intellect nor of the will, but a union of the unity that is in the soul's otherness with the unity that is without otherness (farmer1998:20885).

unverified draft

Context

Giulio Busi and Raphael Ebgi discuss the thesis as Pico's resolution of the scholastic debate over whether final beatitude consists in an act of the intellect or of the will, reporting that Pico ("il Conte") took the side of neither, holding instead, in the thesis's own formulation, that ultimate happiness resides in the union of the unity that is in the otherness of the soul with the unity that is without otherness (busi2014:18739).

unverified draft

Reception

Busi and Ebgi cite Pico's own discussion of the thesis in the Apologia at pages 78-79, without giving its content in the excerpt consulted here (busi2014:18739).
