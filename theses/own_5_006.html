Thesis 5>6

Latin

Ideo amor ab Orpheo sine oculis dicitur, quia est supra intellectum.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 22234

English

That is why Orpheus calls love blind: because it stands above the intellect. unverified draft

'sine oculis', literally 'without eyes', is rendered idiomatically as 'blind' (farmer1998:22234). Howlett's own translation, 'Love is said by Orpheus to be without eyes, because it is above the intellect' (howlett2021:7664), is a related but distinct phrasing and is not reproduced here.

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-swarm-batch4, from Latin at farmer1998:22234

Attribution: Pico's own opinion (farmer1998:22234). · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

The thesis holds that Orpheus's description of love as without eyes signifies that love stands above the intellect (farmer1998:22234; busi2014:24375). Busi notes that Pico numbers the Orphic fragment behind the thesis as his ninth source, and observes that the idea of a love-power exceeding the intellect recurs in Pico's later De Ente et Uno (busi2014:6435).

Context

Farmer's cross-reference network links the thesis to eleven others across three sections: on beatitude residing essentially in the act of intellect (2.12), on the world-soul (23.9), on the six transcendentals the 'common doctrine' wrongly attributes to Aristotle (2>13), to a run of other Plato theses on love (5>7, 5>8, 5>14, 5>21, 5>24, 5>25, 5>48, 5>49), and to the Kabbalistic division of speculative and practical Kabbalah at 11>1 (farmer1998:11419; farmer1998:15761; farmer1998:19083; farmer1998:22237; farmer1998:22241; farmer1998:22358; farmer1998:22547; farmer1998:22561; farmer1998:22565; farmer1998:22987; farmer1998:22994; farmer1998:26318).

Historiography

Howlett quotes the thesis, alongside Pico's Commentary on Benivieni's canzone, without extended discussion in the excerpt available (howlett2021:7664). Busi situates the thesis within Pico's own numbered source-apparatus for the Plato conclusions and connects its claim about a supra-intellectual love to Pico's later De Ente et Uno (busi2014:6435).
