Latin

Dictum est ex Heden exire fluuium qui diuiditur in quatuor capita, ad significandum quod ex secunda numeratione procedit tertia, quae in quartam, quintam, sextam, et decimam diuiditur.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 17732

English

It is said that a river goes out from Eden and divides into four heads, to signify that from the second numeration the third proceeds, which is divided into the fourth, the fifth, the sixth, and the tenth. unverified draft

Doctrine

The thesis allegorizes the four rivers of Eden as a figure for the kabbalistic sefirot, using 'numeration' as a literal rendering of the Hebrew sefirah for the procession of the third numeration from the second, and its further division into the fourth, fifth, sixth and tenth (farmer1998:17811). Farmer directs the reader to Pico's further treatment of the symbolism of the four rivers of paradise in the Oration (farmer1998:17811).

Historiography

Busi and Ebgi print this conclusion together with its immediate neighbors in their edition, among the theses on the sefirotic 'edifice' (busi2014:24877). Wirszubski quotes the same Latin as the eleventh conclusion of the Cabalistic set (wirszubski1989:1734, 1737).
