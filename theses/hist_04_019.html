Latin

Actus intelligendi ab obiecto et intellectu tanquam duobus agentibus partialibus, ut quod causatur.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 12371

English

The act of understanding is caused by the object and the intellect as by two partial agents, taken together as the complete cause. unverified draft

'Ut quod causatur': the scholastic distinction between a cause taken 'as that which' (ut quod, the complete, per se cause) and 'as that by which' (ut quo, a formal principle), where the thesis asserts that object and intellect jointly form the former (farmer1998:12371). The rendering here is provisional, since the phrase is technical and its precise sense is not further glossed in the material consulted (farmer1998:12371).

Doctrine

The thesis is grouped by Farmer with thesis 20 under a single cross-reference to thesis 2.40, on the source of difficulty in understanding (farmer1998:12459; thesis 2.40, farmer1998:11882). The thesis addresses the causation of intellection, holding that the act of understanding is caused by the object and the intellect as two partial agents constituting a single complete cause (farmer1998:12371). Edelheit confirms that, in thesis 19, the act of understanding results from the object and the intellect together, and cites Scotus's Lectura, prologue, part 4, questions 1-2, on the practical or speculative character of a habit taken from its object (edelheit2022:14878, 14931).
