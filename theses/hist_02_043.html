Implicat contradictionem, materiam esse sine forma.

English

It implies a contradiction for matter to exist without form. unverified draft

Doctrine

The thesis denies the possibility, even to divine power, of matter existing without form; Edelheit reports that Francis of Marchia offers 'a similar formulation' concluding that metaphysics does not consider quiddity in the particular but only separated quiddities, and that Thomas, in the Quaestiones de quolibet (3, q.1, a.1), argues that God cannot make contradictories true together, and that matter existing without form would be such a contradiction (edelheit2022:12897, 12922).

unverified draft

Reception

Edelheit reports that thesis 43, together with theses 20 and 40, is 'included among the theses which were criticized by Bernardo Torni', to be discussed further in a later chapter of his book not excerpted here (edelheit2022:12897). A passage Edelheit quotes from Torni's Opuscoli filosofici e medici (p.68) argues against 'the 43rd conclusion' by presupposing a definition of matter as an ungenerable, incorruptible entity from which, together with form, the composite is precisely constituted (edelheit2022:17815).

unverified draft

Historiography

Edelheit (2022) connects thesis 43 to Francis of Marchia's parallel formulation and to Thomas's Quaestiones de quolibet on the impossibility of formless matter, and records Bernardo Torni's contemporary criticism of the thesis (edelheit2022:12897, 12922, 17815).

Quotations cited

  • “Deus non potest facere contradictoria esse simul. Sed materiam esse sine forma implicat contradictionem, eo quod esse materiae” — Amos Edelheit, A Philosopher at the Crossroads: Giovanni Pico della Mirandola's Encounter with Scholastic Philosophy (edelheit2022:12922)
  • “Contra 43am conclusionem, que in forma sic habetur: ‘Implicat contradictionem materiam esse sine forma’, arguitur presupponendo quod per materiam nos intelligimus talem entitatem ingenerabilem et incorruptibilem ad sensum phylosophorum omnium, ex qua et forma precise constituitur compositum, ita quod materia non aliud significet vel connotet quam talem entitatem.” — Amos Edelheit, A Philosopher at the Crossroads: Giovanni Pico della Mirandola's Encounter with Scholastic Philosophy (edelheit2022:17815)
