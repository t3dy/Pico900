Latin

Qui intellexerit in dextrali coordinatione subordinationem pietatis ad sapientiam, perfecte intelliget_per uiam Cabalae quomodo Abraam in die suo per rectam lineam uidit diem Christi, et gauisus est.

Copy-text: Farmer 1998, line 27217

The underscore in 'intelliget_per' reproduces an OCR artifact for a word-space in the corpus copy (farmer1998:27217).

English

Whoever will have understood, in the right-hand ordering, the subordination of piety to wisdom will fully understand, by way of the Cabala, how Abraham in his day, by a straight line, saw the day of Christ, and rejoiced. unverified draft

The allusion is to John 8:56 ('Abraham... saw my day, and was glad'); Farmer glosses 'piety' and 'wisdom' as the fourth and second sefirot respectively, correlated here with 'the day of Abraham' and 'the day of Christ' (farmer1998:27242).

Translation: original translation, drafted 2026-09-26 by WRITER-full-batch19, from Latin at farmer1998:27217

Attribution: Pico's own opinion (farmer1998:27217). · Pico's stance: endorses

Doctrine

Farmer glosses 'piety' and 'wisdom' as the fourth and second sefirot, correlated with 'the day of Abraham' and 'the day of Christ', and identifies Christ's association with divine 'Wisdom' with 1 Corinthians 1:24; he glosses the 'right-hand order' as the sefirot proper, as against an 'emanation of the left-hand' composed of evil forces (farmer1998:27242). He cross-references the thesis to 11>29, on the Cabalists' 'green line' that girds the universe, and to 25.7, on the threefold nature signified by the direct, reflected, and refracted ray in the science of optics (farmer1998:27012, 17003).

Historiography

Wirszubski quotes the thesis's Latin in full twice: first introducing it as 'Pico's thirty-seventh thesis' that 'bears out my conclusion' (wirszubski1989:7057), and again designating it 'Conclusio xxxvii' in a passage on how Christian Kabbala is created through the superposition of the symbolic language of Jewish Kabbala upon an eminently Christian text (wirszubski1989:8518, 8520).
