## Text

**Latin (Farmer 1998, farmer1998:11665):** In generatione substantiali fit resolutio usque ad materiam primam.

**Farmer's English:** farmer1998:11722

## Farmer's notes (excerpts to paraphrase, not reproduce)

- farmer1998:11743: 2.29. Thomas, who believed that matter could not exist without form (cf. 2.25, 2.43), and who posited only one substantial form in each created nature, claimed that the generation of any new substance necessitated the destruction of any previous form found in its matter. Pico up- held the opposing view that matter had a distinct form of its own preceding the substantial form. On the forma corporeitatis or “material form,” cf. 16.1-2, 2>12, 2>70.
