## Farmer's notes (excerpts to paraphrase, not reproduce)

- farmer1998:11290: 2.1. Starts a complex series on the distinction of Persons in the Chnstian Tnnity, drawn from medieval commentaries on book 1 of Peter Lombard’s Sentences. Cf. in the historical theses 3.1—
- farmer1998:11294: 1>7. Pico’s view of distinctions in the divine nature was extremely problematic. On the one hand, in his theological theses (cf. especially 4>5, 4>26-27 and notes) he tned to distance himself from such distinctions, arguing (in line with his philosophia nova) that God’s nature was indivisible. On the other hand, he was constrained on dogmatic grounds to accept the basic notion of the Trinity, and in esoteric sections of his text he intended to link other distinctions made by Christian theologians in the divine nature to similar divisions in the henads of post- Plotinian Neo-Platonism (theses 24.1—55), the kabbalistic sefirot (theses 28.1—47, 11>1-72; cf. also the Apology [Opera, 173]), and other non-Christian traditions.
