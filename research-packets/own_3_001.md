## Farmer's notes (excerpts to paraphrase, not reproduce)

- farmer1998:20304: 3>1. The first lines of the Commento (Garin, Scritti van, 461) similarly tell us that it is the “prin- cipal dogma” of the Platonists (and, by implication, of Aristotle) that everything created has three modes of existence—“participated,” “formal,” and “causal.” The first thesis of this section can thus be considered, in both the literal and figurative senses, as the first principle of Pico’s system, which he tells us was “thought out in Anstotelian and Platonic philosophy.” It is pre- sumably no accident that in the final version of Pico’s text as a whole this is the five hundredth thesis, a number that for Pico symbolized completion and return (cf. 11>68 and note).
