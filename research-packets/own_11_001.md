## Farmer's notes (excerpts to paraphrase, not reproduce)

- farmer1998:26326: 11>1. Since in medieval traditions “divine names” and “practical Kabbalah” were associated with magic, Scholem and Wirszubski (followed now by Copenhaver [1997: 217#])}—reversing the natural order of Pico’s thesis—tried to identify Pico’s "science of sefirot" with speculative science and his “science of names” with practical science or magic. However, given Pico’s disclaimer at the start of this thesis (“Whatever other Cabalists say . . .”), as well as the content of the theses that follow, no justification exists for inverting his sense: Practical science for Pico was the “science of sefirot” and speculative science the “science of names,” and not the reverse. Indeed, a number of his theses explicitly associate practical or magical operations with the sefirot or emanated states [...]
