**Latin (Farmer 1998, farmer1998:25378):** Nulla nomina ut significatiua, et inquantum nomina sunt singula et per se sumpta, in magico opere uirtutem habere possunt, nisi sint hebraica, uel inde proxime deriuata.

**1486 apparatus (Farmer):** 1486 significativa.& inquantum (farmer1998:25387)

**Farmer's English:** farmer1998:25439

## Farmer's notes (excerpts to paraphrase, not reproduce)

- farmer1998:25450: 9>22. Again drawn from De mystenis 7.4, with Pico replacing barbarian languages, and in particular Egyptian, as Iamblichus had it, with Hebrew! On the magic in Hebrew, see the theses listed in note 28.33. By names "closely derived” from Hebrew, Pico presumably had “Chaldean” names in mind, although in his theses from the Orphic Hymns he also implies that magic lay hidden in Greek.
