## Text

**Latin (Farmer 1998, farmer1998:11047):** Lumen non habet in medio nisi esse intentionale.

**Farmer's English:** farmer1998:None

## Farmer's notes (excerpts to paraphrase, not reproduce)

- farmer1998:11115: 1.7-8. Cf. 8.9-10, 9.9-10. Arısing from commentary on Anstotle De anima 2.7-8. For Albert, the greater “intentionality” (or "spintuality") of light as compared to sound was shown by the fact that sounds are affected by winds but colors are not; all sensual images become “spiritual” once they pass through the senses, however. In medieval debates, this thesis was usually tied to broader issues involving sensual abstraction.
