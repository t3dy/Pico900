## 1. Verdict

1. The passage layer is real but mislabelled. The 15 Oration, 22 Heptaplus and 12 De ente et uno passages are genuine text, copied verbatim (minus footnote numerals) from a translation. But the labelled sources are wrong: the Heptaplus English is Carmichael 1965, not "Borghesi + Papio (pending)" (BPR contains only the Oration); "critical edition, p. 82-83, lines 2854-2856" on the De ente entries are line numbers of a Markdown file (WMC l. 2853-2854), not a critical edition; only 14 of 69 entries contain any Latin.
2. None of the 20 Commento entries is Pico. Their `english_translation` is Michael Allen's scholarly prose (verbatim, spliced, or partly reworded), labelled "Allen critical translation". Allen did not translate the Commento; the English translation is Sears Jayne (Peter Lang 1984) (ALLEN l. 6717-6722). C.3.4's "translation" appears nowhere in the corpus. C.4.1 is Ficino's Philebus commentary as reported by Allen, filed as the Commento.
3. "Pages" such as "Allen pp. 6076-6089" are Markdown line numbers. The Garin page cited for the binsica passage (484-485) is wrong: Allen's own endnote gives Commento particulare 557-8 [Jayne 150].
4. The Heptaplus "seven-layer" scheme (literal, allegorical, tropological, anagogical, Kabbalistic, astrological, numerological) that organises all 22 Heptaplus entries, `lineage_kabbalah.json`, the debate log and `ANGELICRESEARCH.md` is not in Pico or in Black. The Heptaplus has seven expositions, one per world or theme (BLACK l. 11600-11745). All 22 entries come from the proem and the Third Exposition, yet the same chapter is tagged "Allegorical" in one entry and "Anagogical" in another.
5. The "Kabbalistic layer" (8 entries) rests on text in which Pico says he will not give the Hebrew angelology (WMC l. 5085-5116; BLACK l. 2013-2026), and the Third Exposition contains zero occurrences of "cabal*" or "sefir*" (my count, WMC l. 5069-5491). The Sefirot / angelic-order table presented as "Pico's mapping" is not in any corpus source I could search.
6. 11 of 12 De ente et uno entries are tagged "angelology" (all but U.2.2); only 2 (U.1.1, U.1.2) mention angels in the quoted passage, and the one other "angel" hit in chapters 5-10 is "my Angelo" (Poliziano, WMC l. 3040). The seraph/demon readings of U.4.x, U.6.x are imported.
7. Scholarly attribution: no entry quotes any scholar. 12 entries carry 36 one-line "citations"; none has a page or quotation; at least four are unfindable ("Black on demonic privation": Black has no discussion of demons; the debate-log "Farmer: Goetia": the word occurs in one corpus file, as a Latin term-of-art at COP-MD l. 17346).
8. `sources.json` is a list of Pico's supposed sources, not a bibliography. It says 87 sources and holds 46; `conclusions_linked` values are hand-entered "estimates" (SOURCES_FEATURE.md l. 196). At least eight of the ten rows I checked contain a factual or attribution error, including Luria (b. 1534) as a source for Pico (d. 1494), "Tahafut al-Tahafut" attributed to al-Ghazali (it is Averroes'), and the Cloud of Unknowing, Eckhart and Imitation of Christ as influences on Pico on the strength of passing mentions that say something else.
9. The validators certify JSON shape, key presence, whitelist membership and the presence of the literal string "[VERIFIED]". 66/69 "passing" means 66 files contain one of three bracket strings. A pass certifies nothing about truth, quotation, page, or provenance. The approval documents' "citations backed by corpus", "cross-references verified" and "Latin incipits match critical edition" are not tested by any code: 50 of 146 ID-shaped cross-references resolve to nothing; 46 of 129 lineage passages still contain "[PLACEHOLDER ...]".
10. The prose is AI boilerplate of the "overclaiming" kind (139 self-applied [VERIFIED] tags, zero [CITED], 62 of 69 entries closing with "Being & Unity grounds this metaphysically"), and it misses the angelology that scholars in the corpus actually find in Pico: separated substances and the location of angels (COP-TR), the Third Exposition's Dionysian plan, mutual containment, Metatron as Agent Intellect. The house style guide's own "voice models" are pastiche attributed to real scholars and cannot be found in the corpus.

## 7. Exemplar: O.1.5 commentary rewritten (about 250 words)

Locators are BPR, WIRSZ, COP-MD, ALLEN as defined above; "l." is the Markdown line. Every quotation was matched in the file at the line cited (Copenhaver's quotation of Pico's thesis is his translation).

> **O.1.5. Enoch and Metatron (Oration §35)**
>
> Pico's one Kabbalistic gesture in the Oration is a single clause, and it works by contrast. In §35 "the most secret Hebrew theology at one time transforms holy Enoch into an angel of divinity", while the Pythagoreans deform the wicked into brutes: an upward metamorphosis set against downward ones, before the ladder of §74 appears [BPR §35, English l. 757-758; Latin l. 739-741].
>
> The Hebrew name is a textual event. The *princeps* prints a blank; both Basel editions print "a Hebrew retroversion of the Latin *angelum divinitatis*"; the reading מטטרון comes from the earlier draft in Florence, Palatino 885, fol. 145r [BPR n. 34, l. 783-785; WIRSZ p. 200, l. 10235-10241]. Pico repeats the identification in the Commento, where the "Cabalist wise men" say "Enoch was transformed into Metatron, angel of divinity" [BPR n. 34, quoting Pico 1984, 147, l. 791-793; Garin 554, per ALLEN n. 23, l. 6740].
>
> Metatron is not a generic angel. In Kabbalistic thesis 10 Pico equates him with Orpheus's Pallas, Zoroaster's Father's Mind and Parmenides' Intelligible Sphere [COP-MD p. 365, l. 15028-15032], and he "is sometimes identified, notably by Abulafia, with the Active Intellect" [WIRSZ p. 200, l. 10244]. Copenhaver infers that this Pallas ties the Cherub's knot at §66 [COP-MD p. 372, l. 15328-15332]; that is his inference, not Pico's statement. The Oration itself, Copenhaver observes, supplies "none of this context" and gives "hints about Kabbalah entirely beyond the grasp of Christians" [COP-MD p. 365, l. 15040-15042].
>
> Two cautions. Wirszubski: "I am not suggesting that Metatron is a specifically Kabbalistic figure" [WIRSZ p. 193, l. 9850-9851]. The editors add that Pico "more likely elaborates on a complex, and apocryphal, textual tradition" than on one Enoch text [BPR n. 33, l. 769-771]. The clause proves no doctrine of Sefirotic angel-orders; it stakes the Oration's ascent on a figure whose Kabbalistic identity Pico had already fixed in the Conclusiones.

What changed relative to the repo entry: removed "Merkabah mystics" as a gloss for "Chaldean interpreters" (no Chaldeans in §35), removed Heptaplus "Sefirotic correspondences", replaced two unlocated [VERIFIED] tags with locators, kept the cross-reference to Conclusiones 1.19.2 and 2.11.10 only where BPR n. 34 supports it (Themistius/Agent Intellect; Kabbalistic conclusions), which the repo had misapplied to §29. Page numbers for WIRSZ (200, 193) are inferred from the printed bracket page markers "[199]" (l. 10224) and "[192]" (l. 9831) in the Markdown; COP-MD pages 365 and 372 from the printed running heads (l. 15017-15059, 15313).
